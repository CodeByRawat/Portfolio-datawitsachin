The AI automation market is crowded with people who've learned three tools and call themselves agencies. Here's how to separate the useful from the noisy.

## What to look for

- **They ask about your process first.** If the first call is a tool pitch, leave.
- **Real examples** of workflows in production, not demo videos.
- **Honest about limits:** willing to say "don't automate that" or "a spreadsheet will do".
- **They handle failure:** error handling, monitoring, alerts and logs are part of the build.
- **You own the result:** code, workflows, credentials and documentation are yours.

## Red flags

- Guaranteed "10x" results with no knowledge of your operation.
- Locking you into their platform or an unlimited retainer.
- No plan for human review of AI output.
- Vague pricing, with no scope or milestones.

## Questions to ask

1. What would the pilot be, and how will we measure success?
2. What happens when it breaks, and who finds out first?
3. Where does my data go, and which AI providers are involved?
4. What do I get at the end (source, docs, handover)?
5. Who actually does the work?

## A good first project

Small, valuable and measurable: one workflow with clear volume, delivered in a few weeks, with a baseline to compare against. Use the [readiness check](/tools/automation-readiness) to pick it. If it works, expand. If it doesn't, you've lost little.
