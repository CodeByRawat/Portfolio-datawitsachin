**Business process automation (BPA)** uses software to run recurring work with less manual effort. Adding **AI** extends this to the tasks that used to need a human to read, interpret or decide. Here's the approach that works.

## Step 1: Pick the right process

Look for work that is repetitive, frequent, rule-based and digital. Ask the people doing it where they lose time. Common winners: data entry between systems, approvals, report building, document intake, customer onboarding, follow-up emails.

## Step 2: Map it as it really runs

Write down every step, input, decision and exception, not how the manual says it works. You'll usually find hidden steps and rules that live in someone's head. This map is the spec for the automation.

## Step 3: Decide what to automate

Automate the predictable core. Route the odd cases to a human with all context attached. Aim for **60-70% straight-through** at first rather than 100%.

## Step 4: Choose the approach

| Situation | Good fit |
|---|---|
| Systems have APIs, logic is simple | No-code tools (Make, n8n, Zapier) |
| Legacy screens, no APIs | RPA |
| Unstructured documents or emails | AI extraction/classification plus workflow |
| Complex, varied cases | AI agent with approvals |
| Core business logic, unique needs | Custom software |

## Step 5: Add governance

For anything AI-driven, define: who approves what, what's logged, how errors are caught, where data is processed, and who owns the automation when it breaks. Governance isn't paperwork. It's what lets you trust the system with more work over time.

## Step 6: Measure

Track hours saved, error rate, cycle time and exceptions per week. Use the [ROI calculator](/tools/roi-calculator) to set a baseline and compare after launch.

## Business automation services: what to look for

A good provider will ask about your process before talking about tools, show you a small pilot, give you the source and documentation, and be honest about what shouldn't be automated. Be wary of anyone who sells a platform before understanding the work.
