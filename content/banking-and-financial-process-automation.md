Banks, lenders, fintechs and finance teams run on repeatable, document-heavy, rule-driven work, which is exactly what automation does best. It's also regulated, so how you automate matters as much as what.

## High-value use cases

- **Customer onboarding & KYC:** collect documents, extract details, run checks, flag mismatches for review.
- **Loan processing:** intake applications, pull data from payslips and statements, calculate ratios, assemble the credit file.
- **Reconciliation:** match transactions across ledgers and bank feeds, queue only the breaks.
- **Payments exceptions:** classify failed payments and route with the right context.
- **Reporting & compliance:** assemble recurring reports from multiple systems with a trail of sources.
- **Accounts payable / receivable:** invoice capture, matching and reminders.

## The controls that make it acceptable

1. **Audit trail** for every automated action.
2. **Segregation of duties.** The bot that prepares shouldn't be the one that approves.
3. **Human approval** for credit decisions, large movements and exceptions.
4. **Access control** with least-privilege credentials for bots.
5. **Accuracy testing** on real documents before go-live, and monitoring after.
6. **Data residency and retention** rules respected end to end.

## Where to start

Pick a back-office process with clear volume and low decision risk, such as reconciliation or document intake. Prove accuracy, show the time saved with the [ROI calculator](/tools/roi-calculator), then extend into higher-stakes steps with approvals built in.
