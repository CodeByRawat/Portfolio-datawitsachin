A **chat box** on your site can answer questions at 3 a.m., qualify leads, and take repetitive load off your team, or it can frustrate everyone who touches it. The difference is in the setup.

## What modern AI chatbots can do

- **Answer from your knowledge:** policies, pricing, product details, using your own documents as the source (RAG).
- **Take actions:** check an order, book a slot, create a ticket, capture a lead into your CRM.
- **Qualify visitors:** ask a few questions and route serious enquiries to you.
- **Hand over cleanly:** pass the full conversation to a human when needed.

## Where chatbots go wrong

1. **Making things up.** Ground answers in your documents and let the bot say "I don't know".
2. **No escape hatch.** Always offer a human option.
3. **Stale content.** Keep the knowledge base updated or answers rot.
4. **No measurement.** Review real conversations weekly and fix the gaps.

## Build, buy or embed?

Hosted tools (the "YesChat"-style widgets and similar) get you live fast and suit simple FAQ use. A **custom chatbot** makes sense when it must connect to your systems: orders, bookings, internal data, or strict privacy requirements.

## Launch checklist

- Define what it should and shouldn't answer.
- Load accurate source documents.
- Connect only the actions you're comfortable automating.
- Add human handoff and a visible "talk to a person" link.
- Test with real past customer questions.
- Monitor and iterate for the first month.
