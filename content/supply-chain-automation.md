Supply chains generate a constant stream of small, time-sensitive tasks: confirm this order, chase that supplier, update the customer. Individually trivial; together they eat a team's day. That makes them ideal for automation.

## What to automate

- **Order intake:** orders arriving by email, EDI, portals or spreadsheets are parsed and entered once.
- **Purchase orders:** reorder triggers create POs for approval when stock drops below a threshold.
- **Supplier communication:** automatic confirmations, delivery-date chasers and escalation if there's no reply.
- **Shipment tracking:** carrier updates pushed to your system and customers, with exceptions flagged.
- **Three-way matching:** PO, goods receipt and invoice compared automatically; only mismatches reach a human.
- **Reporting:** daily stock, late-order and supplier-performance summaries.

## Automated business models

You'll also see "automated business" and "automated franchise" pitched online: businesses designed so operations run through systems instead of constant manual effort. The honest version: automation reduces the labour in a business, but you still need a viable product, customers and someone accountable. Automate repeatable operations; don't expect software to replace the business itself.

## A sensible order of attack

1. Automate **visibility** first (dashboards, alerts). It's low risk and shows where time goes.
2. Then automate **communication** (confirmations, chasers).
3. Then automate **transactions** (POs, matching) with approval steps.
