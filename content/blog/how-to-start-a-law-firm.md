<p class="lede">To start a law firm: choose your <strong>practice areas and target clients</strong>, pick a <strong>business entity</strong> allowed for lawyers in your jurisdiction (e.g., PLLC/PC or local equivalents), satisfy <strong>bar and licensing requirements</strong>, set up <strong>trust (client) accounting</strong>, get <strong>malpractice insurance</strong>, choose a <strong>virtual or physical office</strong>, set up <strong>practice management, intake and billing software</strong>, launch a <strong>professional website and Google Business Profile</strong>, and build referral relationships to win your first clients, all while following your jurisdiction's <strong>professional conduct rules on advertising</strong>.</p>

## Step 1: Define your practice

- **Practice areas:** focus beats generalism for marketing and expertise
- **Ideal clients:** individuals vs businesses, local vs national (where permitted)
- **Fee model:** hourly, flat fees, contingency, subscriptions/retainers

## Step 2: Legal structure and registration

Choose an entity permitted for law practices in your jurisdiction, register the business, obtain tax IDs, and check bar rules on firm names and partnerships.

## Step 3: Bar and compliance

- Active licence in good standing
- Registration of the firm where required
- **Trust/IOLTA account** setup and rules
- Conflict-checking procedures
- Data protection and confidentiality obligations

## Step 4: Startup costs

Costs can range from a few thousand dollars for a lean virtual practice to much more for a staffed office. Typical items: malpractice insurance, bar fees, software, website, office or virtual office, phone, equipment, research tools, marketing and working capital.

## Step 5: Office setup

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Virtual office** | Lowest cost, flexible | Need meeting space, mail address |
| **Shared/co-working** | Affordable, professional address | Less privacy |
| **Executive suite** | Reception and meeting rooms | Moderate cost |
| **Traditional office** | Full control and presence | Highest cost |

## Step 7: Website and online presence

A fast, professional website with practice area pages, attorney bios, FAQs, reviews where permitted, clear contact and consultation booking, plus a Google Business Profile (see [How to create a Google Business page](/blog/how-to-create-a-google-business-page/)).

## Step 8: First clients

- Referrals from former colleagues, other lawyers and your network
- Bar association referral services
- Local networking and speaking
- Content and SEO for your practice areas
- Fast, responsive intake (see [How to get clients as a lawyer](/blog/how-to-get-clients-as-a-lawyer/))

## Ethics and advertising rules

Lawyer advertising is regulated (e.g., rules on testimonials, claims, specialisation, solicitation and disclaimers). Check your jurisdiction's rules before marketing.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start my own law firm?
Choose practice areas, form an allowed business entity, meet bar requirements, set up trust accounting and insurance, choose an office, implement practice management and billing software, launch a website and build referral sources.

### How much does it cost to start a law firm?
From a few thousand dollars for a lean virtual practice to substantially more for a staffed physical office.

### Can I start a law firm right out of law school?
Many jurisdictions allow it once licensed, but mentorship and strong systems are important.

### What software does a new law firm need?
Practice management, billing with trust accounting, intake/CRM, document management, e-signatures and secure communication.

### How do new law firms get clients?
Through referrals, networking, bar referral services, a strong website and Google profile, content and fast intake.

### Do I need an office to start a law firm?
Not necessarily; many firms start virtually with access to meeting rooms when needed, subject to local rules.
