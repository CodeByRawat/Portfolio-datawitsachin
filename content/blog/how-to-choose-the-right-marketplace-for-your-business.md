<p class="lede">To choose the right marketplace, match <strong>your product and customers</strong> to the platform where they already shop, then compare <strong>fees, competition, fulfilment options, rules and how much control you keep</strong>. As a rule of thumb: <strong>Amazon</strong> for mass-market products with search demand, <strong>Etsy</strong> for handmade, vintage and personalised items, <strong>eBay</strong> for used, collectible and parts, <strong>Walmart Marketplace</strong> for established sellers of everyday goods, <strong>TikTok Shop</strong> for visual impulse buys, and <strong>your own website</strong> for brand control, margins and customer relationships.</p>

## Major marketplaces compared

| Marketplace | Best for | Audience | Typical fees (approximate, check current) | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon** | Branded or commodity products with search demand | Huge, buyers ready to purchase | Monthly plan + referral fees (~8–15% in many categories) + FBA fees | Intense competition, price pressure, strict rules |
| **eBay** | Used, refurbished, collectibles, parts | Bargain hunters, collectors | Final value fees (~13% in many categories) + optional store fees | Auction/pricing complexity |
| **Etsy** | Handmade, vintage, craft supplies, personalised | Gift and unique-item shoppers | Listing fee + transaction fee + payment processing; offsite ads fees | Copycat competition, limited branding |
| **Walmart Marketplace** | Established sellers of everyday goods | Mainstream US shoppers | Referral fees by category, no monthly fee | Approval required |
| **TikTok Shop** | Visual, trending, impulse products | Younger social shoppers | Commission per sale | Needs constant content/creators |
| **Facebook Marketplace / Shops** | Local and casual selling, social brands | Local buyers, social users | Selling fees on shipped items | Low-trust buyers, haggling |
| **Niche marketplaces** (Reverb, Poshmark, Wayfair, Faire, Zalando, Flipkart, etc.) | Specific categories or regions | Targeted buyers | Varies | Smaller reach |

Fees change frequently; always check each marketplace's current fee page.

## A decision framework

1. **Where do your customers shop?** Search for your product on each marketplace and see who sells similar items.
2. **Category fit and rules:** some categories are restricted or need approval.
3. **Demand vs competition:** many sellers and reviews = proven demand but harder entry.
4. **Unit economics:** price − product cost − fees − shipping − ads − returns = profit. Calculate per marketplace.
5. **Fulfilment:** can you ship fast enough, or use FBA/WFS-type programmes?
6. **Brand control:** how much can you customise listings and communicate with buyers?
7. **Operational load:** inventory sync, customer service, returns.
8. **Growth plan:** marketplaces for reach; own site for loyalty and margin.

## Marketplace vs your own website

| | Marketplace | Own website/store |
|---|---|---|
| **Traffic** | Built-in buyers | You must bring traffic |
| **Trust** | Borrowed from the marketplace | Must be built (reviews, design, policies) |
| **Fees** | Higher per sale | Lower per sale (platform + payment fees) |
| **Customer data** | Limited | Full ownership |
| **Branding** | Limited | Full control |
| **Rules risk** | Account suspensions, policy changes | You set the rules |
| **Repeat sales** | Hard to market to buyers | Email, WhatsApp, loyalty |

**Best of both:** start where buyers already are (marketplace) while building your own store to own customer relationships. (See [How to create an online store](/blog/how-to-create-an-online-store/).)

## Multichannel selling tips

- **Sync inventory** across channels to avoid overselling (multichannel tools or platform integrations)
- **Keep pricing consistent** with channel fees in mind
- **Repurpose content:** the same product photos and videos across channels (see [How to make a promotional video](/blog/how-to-make-a-promotional-video/))
- **Automate order and stock updates** (see [How to integrate data from multiple sources](/blog/how-to-integrate-data-from-multiple-sources/))
- **Include inserts** inviting marketplace buyers to join your list where policies allow
- **Track profit per channel,** not just revenue

## Examples

- **Handmade jewellery:** Etsy first, own Shopify store for repeat customers and gifting campaigns
- **Phone accessories:** Amazon + Walmart for volume, TikTok Shop for viral products
- **Refurbished electronics:** eBay + own site with warranties
- **Local furniture:** Facebook Marketplace + simple website with delivery booking

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I choose the right marketplace for my business?
Identify where your target customers shop, check category fit and competition, calculate profit after each marketplace's fees and fulfilment costs, and consider how much brand control you need.

### Which marketplace is best for beginners?
Etsy is beginner-friendly for handmade and creative products; eBay is easy for used items; Amazon offers the largest audience but more competition and complexity.

### Is it better to sell on Amazon or my own website?
Amazon brings ready buyers but charges higher fees and limits customer relationships. Your own website gives control and margins but needs marketing. Many businesses do both.

### What are the cheapest marketplaces to sell on?
It depends on category and price; Facebook Marketplace for local sales and some niche marketplaces have lower fees, but reach and buyer intent matter more than fees alone.

### Can I sell on multiple marketplaces at once?
Yes. Use inventory management or multichannel tools to keep stock and orders in sync.

### How much do marketplaces charge sellers?
Commonly a percentage of each sale (often around 6–15%), plus possible listing, subscription, payment and fulfilment fees. Check current fee pages.
