<p class="lede">To start a trucking company: choose your <strong>niche and equipment</strong> (dry van, reefer, flatbed, hotshot, box truck), get a <strong>CDL and experience</strong> (or hire drivers), form a <strong>business entity</strong>, obtain the required <strong>registrations and operating authority</strong> (in the US: USDOT number, MC authority for for-hire interstate, BOC-3, UCR, IFTA/IRP), buy <strong>commercial truck insurance</strong>, <strong>buy or lease</strong> your truck, set up <strong>ELD and compliance</strong>, and find <strong>loads</strong> through brokers, load boards and direct shippers, while managing <strong>cash flow</strong> carefully.</p>

## Step 1: Choose a niche and equipment

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| Dry van | Most common freight |
| Reefer | Temperature-controlled, higher rates, more maintenance |
| Flatbed | Specialised loads, securement skills |
| Hotshot | Smaller trucks with trailers, lower startup |
| Box truck | Local/regional deliveries, sometimes no CDL |

## Step 2: Licences and experience

CDL of the right class, clean driving record and experience (insurers often require it).

## Step 3: Registrations and authority (US example)

USDOT number, MC operating authority (for-hire interstate), BOC-3 process agent, UCR registration, IFTA fuel tax and IRP apportioned plates where applicable, state permits, drug and alcohol testing programme. Requirements differ by country and state.

## Step 4: Insurance

Primary liability, cargo, physical damage, bobtail/non-trucking liability, general liability, and occupational accident or workers' comp. New authorities often face high premiums.

## Step 5: Truck and trailer

Buy new/used or lease; inspect thoroughly; budget for maintenance, tyres and repairs.

## Step 6: Compliance

ELDs for hours of service, driver qualification files, maintenance records, safety programme.

## Step 7: Find loads

Freight brokers, load boards, direct shipper contracts, dispatch services, and building relationships for consistent lanes.

## Step 8: Money management

Factoring for faster payment, fuel cards, cost per mile tracking, emergency fund. (Costs: [How much does it cost to start a trucking company?](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-a-trucking-company/))

## Starting with one truck

Many owner-operators start with one truck under their own authority or leased onto a carrier (see [How to become an owner-operator truck driver](/blog/how-to-become-an-owner-operator-truck-driver/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a trucking company?
Choose a niche and equipment, get a CDL, form a business, obtain DOT/MC authority and other registrations, buy insurance, get a truck, set up compliance and find loads.

### Can I start a trucking business with one truck?
Yes; many owner-operators start with one truck under their own authority or leased to a carrier.

### How long does it take to get MC authority?
It typically takes a few weeks after filing, plus insurance and process agent filings.

### Is a trucking company profitable?
It can be with good rates, controlled costs per mile, consistent loads and healthy cash flow.

### Do I need a CDL to own a trucking company?
Not to own one if you hire drivers, but owner-operators driving their trucks need the appropriate CDL.

### How do new trucking companies find loads?
Load boards, freight brokers, dispatch services and building direct shipper relationships.
