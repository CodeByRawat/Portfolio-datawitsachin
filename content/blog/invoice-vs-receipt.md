<p class="lede"><strong>No, an invoice is not the same as a receipt.</strong> An <strong>invoice</strong> is issued <strong>before payment</strong> to request money for goods or services, showing what's owed and when it's due. A <strong>receipt</strong> is issued <strong>after payment</strong> as proof that payment was received. An invoice is <strong>essentially a bill</strong>: "bill" is the everyday word, often used from the customer's side ("we received a bill"), while "invoice" is the formal business term.</p>

## Invoice vs receipt vs bill

| | Invoice | Receipt | Bill |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Request payment | Confirm payment received | Request payment (everyday term) |
| **When issued** | Before payment (or with goods) | After payment | Before payment |
| **Shows** | Amount due, due date, payment terms | Amount paid, payment method, date paid | Amount due |
| **Common in** | B2B, services, freelancers | Retail, any completed payment | Utilities, restaurants, consumer services |
| **Accounting** | Accounts receivable (seller) / payable (buyer) | Proof of transaction | Same as invoice |

## What an invoice includes

Seller and buyer details, invoice number, dates, itemised charges, taxes, total due, payment terms and methods. (Details: [What does an invoice look like?](/blog/what-does-an-invoice-look-like/))

## What a receipt includes

Seller name, date and time of payment, items, amount paid, taxes, payment method, receipt or transaction number, and sometimes the invoice number it pays.

## Can an invoice be a receipt?

An invoice marked **"Paid"** with the payment date and method can serve as proof of payment for many purposes, but a separate receipt is clearer. Some point-of-sale documents combine both ("invoice/receipt") when payment is immediate.

## Related documents

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Quote/estimate** | Proposed price before work starts |
| **Pro forma invoice** | Preliminary invoice (e.g., for prepayment or customs), not a demand for payment in accounting terms |
| **Purchase order (PO)** | Buyer's order authorising a purchase |
| **Credit note** | Reduces or cancels an invoice |
| **Statement** | Summary of invoices and payments over a period |

## Record keeping

- Keep invoices and receipts for the period required by your tax authority (often several years)
- Store digitally with consistent naming and invoice numbers (see [What is an invoice number?](/blog/what-is-an-invoice-number/))
- Use accounting software to match invoices with payments automatically (see [What are automated financial systems?](/blog/what-are-automated-financial-systems/))

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an invoice the same as a receipt?
No. An invoice requests payment; a receipt confirms payment has been made.

### Is an invoice a bill?
Yes, essentially. "Invoice" is the formal term used by sellers; "bill" is the everyday term, often from the customer's perspective.

### Is a receipt an invoice?
No. A receipt is proof of payment issued after the transaction, while an invoice is issued to request payment.

### Which comes first, invoice or receipt?
The invoice usually comes first; the receipt is issued after payment. In retail, payment and receipt happen at the same time without a separate invoice.

### Can I use an invoice as proof of payment?
An invoice marked as paid with payment details may work for many purposes, but a receipt or bank record is stronger proof.

### Do I need to keep invoices and receipts?
Yes, for accounting and tax purposes, typically for several years depending on local law.
