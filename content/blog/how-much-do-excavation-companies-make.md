<p class="lede">Small excavation companies commonly bring in <strong>$300,000 to $1.5 million</strong> a year, with net profit often around <strong>10% to 20%</strong>. Owner-operators often earn <strong>$70,000 to $200,000+</strong>. The money is good, but so are the costs: <strong>equipment payments, fuel, maintenance and insurance</strong> take a big share.</p>

These are rough ranges; location, services and equipment use change them a lot.

## Revenue by size

| Company | Rough yearly revenue |
|---|---|
| Owner-operator, 1–2 machines | $200,000 – $600,000 |
| Small company, 3–6 machines | $600,000 – $2 million |
| Mid-size with trucking | $2 million+ |

## How much do excavation company owners make?

Owner pay depends on how busy machines are. Owner-operators often take home $70,000 to $150,000, and owners of larger companies can earn $200,000+.

## Typical hourly rates

Excavators and skid steers with an operator are often billed at **$100 to $200+ per hour**, depending on machine size and region.

## Biggest costs

1. Equipment payments and depreciation
2. Fuel
3. Maintenance and repairs
4. Operator wages
5. Insurance
6. Trucking and disposal fees

## How to be more profitable

- Keep machines working, not parked
- Bid from real costs and track job profit
- Add higher-margin services (utilities, septic, drainage)
- Own trucks when trucking spend gets high
- Build repeat builder and contractor work
- Maintain equipment to avoid downtime
