<p class="lede">To start a catering business: pick a <strong>niche</strong> (corporate lunches, weddings, private parties, cultural cuisine, drop-off catering, meal prep), get <strong>licences and a permitted commercial kitchen</strong> (many places don't allow catering from a home kitchen), buy <strong>equipment and transport</strong>, create a <strong>menu with per-person pricing</strong>, use <strong>contracts and deposits</strong>, set up a <strong>website with quote requests</strong>, and win clients through <strong>venues, event planners, offices and referrals</strong>.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your niche

| Niche | Notes |
|---|---|
| Corporate catering | Recurring weekday orders |
| Weddings | High value, seasonal |
| Private parties | Birthdays, holidays |
| Drop-off catering | Simpler staffing |
| Full-service catering | Staff, rentals, bar |
| Meal prep | Weekly subscriptions |

## Step 2: Licences and kitchen

Business licence, food service/catering permit, health inspection, food manager certification, and usually a **licensed commercial or shared commissary kitchen**. Home catering is often not allowed unless covered by specific cottage food or home kitchen rules, so check locally.

## Step 3: Equipment and transport

Chafing dishes, hot/cold holding, insulated carriers, serving ware, a suitable vehicle, and rental partners for larger events.

## Step 4: Menu and pricing

Design menus that travel well. Price per person: food cost + labour + equipment + transport + overhead + profit; add service fees, staff charges and rentals separately.

## Step 5: Staffing

Prep cooks, servers and bartenders hired per event or part-time; food safety training.

## Step 6: Contracts and payments

Menu, guest count deadlines, deposit, cancellation policy, dietary needs, setup/clean-up responsibilities. Invoice professionally (see [How to send an invoice](/blog/how-to-send-an-invoice/)).

## Step 7: Website and online orders

Menus, packages, photos, testimonials, and a quote or order form; corporate clients appreciate easy online ordering (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/)).

## Step 8: Marketing

Venue preferred-vendor lists, event planners (see [How to start an event planning business](/blog/how-to-start-an-event-planning-business/)), office managers, Google Business Profile, Instagram food content, tastings.

## Starting a catering business from home

Some areas permit limited home catering under specific rules; most require a licensed kitchen. Renting a commissary or shared kitchen is a common low-cost route.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a catering business?
Pick a niche, get licences and a permitted kitchen, buy equipment, create per-person priced menus, use contracts and deposits, build a website with quote requests and market to venues and planners.

### Can I start a catering business from home?
Often not with a regular home kitchen; many places require a licensed commercial kitchen, though rules vary.

### How much does it cost to start a catering business?
It can start modestly with rented kitchen space and basic equipment, increasing with vehicles, staff and full-service rentals.

### How do caterers price per person?
By calculating food, labour, equipment, transport and overhead per guest, then adding profit and service fees.

### How do caterers get clients?
Through venues, event planners, corporate offices, referrals, Google and social media.

### Is catering profitable?
It can be, especially recurring corporate catering and well-priced events with controlled food costs.
