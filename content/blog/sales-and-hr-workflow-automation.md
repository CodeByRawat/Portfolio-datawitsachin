<p class="lede">If you can only automate a few things, start with these: in <strong>sales</strong>, automate <strong>lead capture into the CRM, instant routing and response, follow-up reminders, meeting scheduling and call notes</strong>; in <strong>HR</strong>, automate <strong>onboarding checklists, leave and expense approvals, interview scheduling and answers to routine policy questions</strong>. Keep discovery, negotiation, performance conversations, sensitive cases and hiring decisions human.</p>

Sales and HR are two of the most admin-heavy functions in a growing business, and two of the areas where automation pays back fastest. But there are hundreds of possible automations, and trying to do everything at once leads to half-finished workflows nobody trusts. This guide is a **prioritisation guide**: what to automate first, in what order, what to leave human, and when you need a custom tool. For deep dives, see [How to automate your sales process](/blog/how-to-automate-your-sales-process/) and [What is HR automation?](/blog/what-is-hr-automation/).

## How to prioritise: the three-question filter

For each candidate automation, ask:

1. **How often does it happen?** (daily beats monthly)
2. **How much time or money does it cost when done manually, or when it's missed?** (a lost lead is expensive; a late leave approval is annoying)
3. **How simple and rule-based is it?** (simple first; judgement-heavy later, with AI and approvals)

High frequency + high cost + simple = automate this week.

## Sales process automation: the first five to build

### 1. Lead capture to CRM (week 1)
Every form, email enquiry, chat, ad lead and booking creates an enriched, deduplicated record in your CRM automatically, with its source recorded. **Why first:** you can't automate anything else if leads live in inboxes and spreadsheets. (See [What is a CRM?](/blog/what-is-a-crm/))

### 2. Instant routing and response (week 1–2)
The right rep gets the right lead within minutes (by territory, product, size or round-robin), with a Slack/Teams alert, and the prospect gets an immediate acknowledgement with a booking link. **Why:** speed to lead is one of the biggest drivers of conversion.

### 3. Follow-up reminders and sequences (week 2–3)
Tasks created automatically for every new lead and after every meeting; nurture sequences for prospects who aren't ready; alerts when deals go stale. **Why:** most lost deals are lost to silence, not competitors.

### 4. Meeting scheduling and prep (week 3)
Booking links routed to the right person; reminders to cut no-shows; an AI brief on the account before each call.

### 5. Call notes and CRM updates (week 4)
AI meeting assistants transcribe calls, summarise them, extract next steps and update CRM fields. **Why:** reps spend hours on data entry. Salesforce's 2026 State of Sales research found the average rep spends only about 40% of their time actually selling.

**Next wave:** proposal and quote generation, pipeline reporting that builds itself, win/loss analysis, renewal reminders and handoff to onboarding. (See [CRM integration and automation](/blog/crm-integration-and-automation/).)

**Keep human:** discovery calls, understanding the buyer's real problem, negotiation, relationship building and judgement on deal strategy. **Artificial intelligence in sales** works best as a prep and admin layer under the salesperson, not a replacement for them.

## HR workflow automation: the first five to build

### 1. Onboarding checklist (week 1–2)
One trigger (a signed offer) creates accounts, sends documents for signing, orders equipment, schedules orientation, assigns training and notifies IT, the manager and payroll. **Why first:** it's repeatable, multi-system and directly shapes a new hire's experience.

### 2. Leave requests and approvals (week 2)
Requests route to the right approver with reminders, balances update automatically, and the team calendar reflects absences.

### 3. Expense approvals (week 2–3)
Receipts captured on mobile, data extracted automatically, policy checks applied, approvals routed and reimbursements processed. (See [What are automated financial systems?](/blog/what-are-automated-financial-systems/))

### 4. Interview scheduling and candidate updates (week 3)
Self-scheduling for candidates, automatic reminders, status emails at each stage, and feedback reminders for interviewers.

### 5. Employee FAQ assistant (week 4)
An AI assistant answers routine policy questions (leave, benefits, expenses, holidays, IT how-tos) from your handbook, citing sources, and routes anything sensitive to HR. (See [How to create an AI chatbot](/blog/how-to-create-an-ai-chatbot/).)

**Next wave:** offboarding with access removal, document expiry alerts (visas, certifications), performance review reminders, training compliance tracking and HR reporting.

**Keep human:** performance conversations, sensitive cases (grievances, health, harassment, terminations), hiring decisions and culture. And be careful with AI in hiring: in the EU and some US jurisdictions, AI used in recruitment and employment decisions is specifically regulated.

## Quick wins vs bigger projects

| Quick wins (days) | Bigger projects (weeks) |
|---|---|
| Form → CRM + instant reply | Full lead scoring and enrichment pipeline |
| Leave approval workflow | End-to-end onboarding across 6+ systems |
| Interview booking links | AI candidate communications and pipeline automation |
| Meeting notes to CRM | Sales forecasting and pipeline analytics |
| Expense receipt capture | Policy-aware expense audit with AI |

Start with quick wins to build momentum, then tackle bigger projects once the team trusts the automations.

## Tools

- **CRMs with built-in automation:** HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM
- **HR systems with workflows:** BambooHR, HiBob, Personio, Rippling, Workday, Zoho People
- **Scheduling:** Calendly, Chili Piper, ATS-native scheduling
- **Meeting assistants:** Fathom, Otter, Fireflies, Gong, built-in Zoom/Teams/Meet AI
- **Connecting it all:** Zapier, Make, n8n, Power Automate (see [No-code vs low-code automation](/blog/no-code-vs-low-code-automation/))
- **AI assistants:** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot for drafting, summarising and answering questions

## Do you need a CRM or HR tool built around you?

Off-the-shelf CRMs and HR systems are fine until your process doesn't fit them and you're bending the team around the tool. Signs it's time for something custom:

- Workarounds in spreadsheets alongside the main system
- Paying for a large platform but using a fraction of it
- Unusual pipelines (partner referrals, multi-stage approvals, field teams)
- Integrations the vendor doesn't offer

A **custom CRM or internal tool** shaped to your pipeline, or custom automations on top of your existing system, can be cheaper than the licences and workarounds. ## Frequently asked questions

### What sales tasks can be automated?
Lead capture and enrichment, routing, instant responses, follow-up reminders and sequences, meeting scheduling, call transcription and CRM updates, proposal and quote drafting, pipeline alerts and reporting.

### What HR processes are best to automate?
Onboarding and offboarding checklists, leave and expense approvals, document collection, interview scheduling, candidate status updates, routine employee questions, document expiry alerts and training assignments.

### What should I automate first in sales?
Lead capture into your CRM and instant routing and response. Without reliable lead capture nothing else works, and responding quickly has a large impact on conversion.

### What should I automate first in HR?
Employee onboarding, because it is repeatable, involves several systems and people, and directly affects the new hire's experience. Leave approvals are another fast, visible win.

### What should stay human in sales and HR?
In sales: discovery, negotiation and relationship building. In HR: performance conversations, sensitive employee cases, hiring decisions and culture. Automation and AI should support these, not replace them.

### How long does it take to automate sales and HR workflows?
Simple automations like form-to-CRM or leave approvals can be live in days. Multi-system workflows like full onboarding or lead scoring pipelines typically take a few weeks including testing.
