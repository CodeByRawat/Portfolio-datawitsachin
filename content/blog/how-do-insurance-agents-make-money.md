<p class="lede">Insurance agents mainly make money from <strong>commissions</strong> paid by insurance companies: a percentage of the premium when they sell a policy (often higher in the <strong>first year</strong>, especially for life insurance) and smaller <strong>renewal commissions</strong> each year the policy stays active. Some agents also earn a <strong>base salary</strong>, <strong>bonuses</strong> or <strong>contingency/profit-sharing payments</strong>, and some charge <strong>fees</strong> where allowed. Income ranges widely, from modest earnings for new agents to high incomes for agency owners with large renewal books.</p>

## How insurance commissions work

| Type | Typical pattern (varies by company, product and country) |
|---|---|
| **Life insurance** | High first-year commission (often a large percentage of the first-year premium), lower renewals |
| **Health insurance** | Per-member or percentage commissions; regulated in many markets |
| **Property & casualty (auto, home, business)** | Percentage of premium on new business and renewals |
| **Annuities** | Percentage of the amount invested |

Commission rates are set by carriers and regulations; check specific contracts.

## Captive vs independent agents

| | Captive agent | Independent agent/broker |
|---|---|---|
| **Sells for** | One insurer | Multiple insurers |
| **Pay** | Often base salary + commission, support and leads | Commission-based, own business |
| **Upside** | Lower risk | Higher upside, owns book of business |

## Salary vs commission-only

New agents may start with salary or training stipends at large companies; commission-only roles offer higher upside but less stability.

## How much do life insurance salespeople make?

Earnings vary dramatically by experience, market and effort. Many new agents earn modest incomes or leave the industry in the first years, while experienced producers and agency owners can earn well into six figures, largely thanks to renewal income and team overrides.

## How agents increase income

- **Build a renewal book** (retention is everything)
- **Cross-sell** (auto + home + umbrella + life)
- **Niche focus** (business owners, seniors, specific professions)
- **Referral partnerships** (real estate agents, mortgage brokers, accountants)
- **Digital lead generation** (see [How to get insurance leads](/blog/how-to-get-insurance-leads/))
- **Open an agency** and hire producers (see [How to start an insurance agency](/blog/how-to-start-an-insurance-agency/))

## Is selling insurance hard?

It can be challenging at first: building a pipeline, handling rejection and learning products. Training, a niche, consistent prospecting and good systems make it easier (see [How to sell insurance](/blog/how-to-sell-insurance/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do insurance agents make money?
Mainly through commissions from insurers on new policies and renewals, plus salaries, bonuses or contingency payments depending on the role.

### Do insurance agents get paid on renewals?
Yes, typically smaller renewal commissions each year the policy remains in force, depending on product and contract.

### How much does a life insurance salesman make?
It varies widely; new agents often earn modest incomes, while experienced producers can earn six figures.

### Do insurance agents get a salary?
Some captive agents receive a base salary plus commission; many independent agents are commission-only.

### What is the difference between captive and independent agents?
Captive agents sell for one insurer; independent agents represent multiple insurers and usually own their book of business.

### Is selling insurance a good career?
It can be rewarding for people who enjoy sales and relationships, especially as renewal income builds over time.
