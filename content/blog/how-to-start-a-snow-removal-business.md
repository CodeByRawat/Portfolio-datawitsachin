<p class="lede">To start a snow removal business, <strong>choose residential or commercial work</strong>, register the business, get <strong>liability insurance</strong>, buy or finance a <strong>truck with a plow, a snow blower and a salt spreader</strong>, and sell <strong>seasonal contracts before the first snowfall</strong>. Many owners run lawn care or landscaping in summer and snow removal in winter.</p>

## Step 1: Pick your market

| Market | Equipment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Residential driveways | Snow blower, shovels, or a plow truck | Lots of small jobs, easy to start |
| Commercial lots | Plow trucks, skid steers, salt spreaders | Bigger contracts, higher liability |
| Sidewalks and steps | Snow blowers, shovels, ice melt | Good add-on for crews |
| HOAs and property managers | Mix | Seasonal contracts |

## Step 2: Register and stay legal

- Form an LLC and get an EIN
- Get a local business license (most towns require one; few have a snow-specific license)
- Check rules on pushing snow into streets
- Get commercial auto insurance for plow trucks

More on licenses: [How to run a successful snow removal business](/blog/how-to-run-a-successful-snow-removal-business/).

## Step 3: Get insurance

General liability is essential because slip-and-fall claims are the biggest risk. Add commercial auto, and workers' comp when you hire. Many commercial clients will ask for a certificate of insurance.

## Step 4: Buy equipment

- Pickup truck with a plow (used is fine to start)
- Walk-behind snow blowers for walkways
- Tailgate or hopper salt spreader
- Shovels, ice melt, safety lights and markers
- Backup equipment for breakdowns during storms

## Step 5: Set your pricing

| Model | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Per push / per visit | Charge each time you plow | Residential, small lots |
| Seasonal contract | Fixed price for the season | Steady cash flow |
| Per inch / trigger depth | Price changes with snowfall | Commercial |
| Hourly | Per hour per machine | Large lots, heavy storms |

Salting is usually charged separately. More help: [How to bid snow plowing jobs](/blog/how-to-bid-snow-plowing-jobs/).

## Step 6: Find customers before winter

- Offer snow contracts to your lawn care customers
- Door hangers in late summer and fall
- Google Business Profile and a website with a quote form
- Call property managers, churches, offices and small retail centres
- Neighbourhood apps and social media

## Step 7: Plan your routes

Group customers close together, set priorities (commercial lots before opening hours) and keep a log with times and photos to protect against slip-and-fall claims.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a snow removal business?
Choose your market, register the business, get insurance and equipment, and sell seasonal contracts before winter.

### How much does it cost to start a snow removal business?
From under $2,000 with a snow blower to $20,000 to $60,000+ with a used plow truck and spreader.

### Do I need a truck to start snow removal?
No. You can start with snow blowers and shovels for driveways and sidewalks.

### Is snow removal a good business?
It can be very profitable in snowy areas, but income depends on the weather, so seasonal contracts help.

### How do snow removal companies get customers?
Lawn care customer lists, door hangers, Google, and calling property managers and businesses.
