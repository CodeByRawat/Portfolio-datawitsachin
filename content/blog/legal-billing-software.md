<p class="lede">For most firms, billing is where good work turns into revenue, or doesn't. <strong>Legal billing software</strong> makes sure every billable minute is recorded, every fee arrangement is applied correctly, client money is handled according to trust accounting rules, invoices go out promptly, and payments are collected. The best systems capture time almost automatically and show you exactly where revenue leaks.</p>

Billing problems are rarely visible in a single invoice. They show up across a year as unrecorded short tasks, write-downs nobody analyses, invoices sent weeks late and clients who pay slowly. This guide explains the features to look for, where firms leak revenue, how to estimate your own leakage, how AI is changing time capture, e-billing for corporate clients, the metrics to watch, popular tools, and when to buy versus build.

## Features to look for

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Easy time capture** | Time you forget to record is revenue you never invoice: timers, mobile entry, and automatic suggestions |
| **Flexible fee types** | Hourly, fixed fee, capped, contingency, retainers, subscriptions, split billing and third-party payers |
| **Rates management** | Different rates by fee earner, client, matter and date; agreed discounts applied automatically |
| **Expenses and disbursements** | Recorded against matters and billed correctly |
| **Trust / client-account accounting** | Client money has strict, jurisdiction-specific rules: three-way reconciliation, ledgers, audit trails |
| **Invoice templates & approval** | Partners review draft bills (pre-bills) before they go out |
| **Billing narratives** | Clear descriptions that reduce disputes, with AI assistance increasingly common |
| **E-billing (LEDES/UTBMS)** | Required by many corporate and insurance clients (see below) |
| **Online payments** | Card and bank payments, payment plans; faster collection |
| **Automated reminders** | Polite, scheduled chasers for overdue invoices |
| **Reporting** | Realisation, utilisation, WIP, lockup, aged debtors, write-offs |
| **Accounting integration** | Avoid double entry with your bookkeeping (QuickBooks, Xero, Sage, NetSuite) |

## Where firms leak revenue

1. **Short tasks that never get logged:** calls, emails, quick reviews, text messages. Six minutes here and there add up.
2. **Time recorded days later from memory,** which is consistently under-recorded.
3. **Unbilled WIP:** time recorded but not invoiced for weeks or months.
4. **Invoices delayed to month end,** hurting cash flow and increasing disputes.
5. **Write-downs and write-offs nobody analyses:** are particular clients, matter types or fee earners consistently written down?
6. **Inconsistent rates** across fee earners or outdated rates never updated.
7. **Scope creep on fixed fees** without change requests.
8. **Unrecovered disbursements.**
9. **Slow collection:** debts that age past 90 days become much harder to recover.

### Estimate your own leakage
Multiply the unrecorded minutes per fee earner per day by your hourly rate and working days. For example: **12 unrecorded minutes × $250/hour × 220 days ≈ $11,000 per fee earner per year.** Across ten fee earners, that's $110,000. Even small daily gaps add up across a year.

## How AI and automation help

- **Automatic time capture:** suggesting time entries from emails sent, calendar events, calls, documents edited and portal activity (now offered by several platforms)
- **AI billing narratives:** turning rough notes into clear, client-friendly descriptions, checked against client billing guidelines
- **Draft invoices automatically** at set intervals for partner review
- **Billing guideline checks:** flagging entries likely to be rejected by corporate clients (block billing, vague descriptions, non-billable tasks)
- **Automated payment reminders** and payment links
- **Weekly digests** of unbilled time and WIP per fee earner and matter
- **Anomaly alerts:** unusual write-downs, matters over budget, slow-paying clients

Note that under ABA Formal Opinion 512 (US), lawyers billing hourly may charge only for time actually spent, not for time AI saved, which is pushing many firms toward fixed and value-based fees for AI-accelerated work. ## E-billing for corporate and insurance clients

Many corporate legal departments and insurers require **e-billing**:

- **LEDES** (Legal Electronic Data Exchange Standard): standard invoice file formats
- **UTBMS codes:** standard task and activity codes (e.g., litigation phase codes)
- **E-billing portals** (e.g., Legal Tracker, Brightflag, Onit, Tymetrix) where invoices are submitted and reviewed against **billing guidelines**

Your billing software should export LEDES, support UTBMS codes and help you comply with each client's guidelines to avoid rejected invoices and payment delays.

## Trust accounting: get this right

Client money rules (IOLTA and state trust rules in the US, the SRA Accounts Rules in England and Wales, and equivalents elsewhere) require:

- Separate client and office accounts
- Individual client ledgers
- Regular reconciliations (often **three-way reconciliation**: bank statement, client ledgers and trust account journal)
- Prompt handling of client funds and clear audit trails

Use software with built-in trust accounting for your jurisdiction, or a proven integration, and confirm compliance with your regulator or accountant.

## Key billing metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Utilisation** | Billable hours ÷ available hours | Are fee earners busy on billable work? |
| **Realisation (billing)** | Billed value ÷ recorded value | How much recorded time is actually billed? |
| **Realisation (collection)** | Collected ÷ billed | How much billed work is actually paid? |
| **Lockup** | WIP days + debtor days | How long cash is tied up |
| **Aged debt** | Unpaid invoices by age | Collection risk |
| **Write-offs by client/matter type/fee earner** | Value written down | Pricing and scoping problems |
| **Average days to bill** | Time from work done to invoice | Billing discipline |

Clio's Legal Trends research has long highlighted that lawyers bill only a fraction of their working day, and that billing and collection gaps reduce revenue further. Tracking these metrics turns that into something you can fix.

## Buying vs building

If a standard tool covers your fee structures, **buy it**. If you have unusual billing (per-claim fees, panel arrangements, referral fee splits, insurer-specific rates, bulk matter billing), consider:

- **Automation layered on top of your system:** unbilled-time alerts, invoice drafts, guideline checks, reporting dashboards
- **A custom billing module** integrated with your practice management and accounting systems

## Frequently asked questions

### What should legal billing software include?
Time and expense capture, flexible rate and fee arrangements, pre-bill review, invoice generation, LEDES e-billing where needed, payment collection, trust or client-account handling that meets your jurisdiction's rules, accounting integration and reporting on realisation, WIP and aged debt.

### Can general accounting software handle law firm accounts?
Often not on its own. Client money (trust) accounting has specific rules. Many firms pair general accounting software with legal billing that handles trust accounting, or use a legal-specific system with both.

### How do I reduce unbilled time?
Make time capture frictionless (timers, mobile entry, automatic suggestions from email, calendar and documents), review unbilled time weekly, set billing cycles, and automate invoice drafts so billing isn't a month-end scramble.

### What is LEDES billing?
LEDES is a standard electronic invoice format required by many corporate and insurance clients, typically combined with UTBMS task and activity codes and submitted through e-billing portals that check invoices against billing guidelines.

### What is realisation rate in a law firm?
Realisation measures how much of the value of recorded time is actually billed (billing realisation) and collected (collection realisation). Low realisation signals pricing, scoping, billing or collection problems.

### Can AI help with legal billing?
Yes. AI can suggest time entries from activity, write clear billing narratives, check entries against client billing guidelines, draft invoices, flag anomalies and automate payment reminders, with lawyers reviewing before invoices are sent.
