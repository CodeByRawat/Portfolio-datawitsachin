For most firms, billing is where good work turns into revenue, or doesn't. **Legal billing software** exists to make sure every billable minute is recorded, invoiced and collected.

## Features to look for

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Easy time capture** | Time you forget to record is revenue you never invoice |
| **Flexible fee types** | Hourly, fixed fee, contingency, retainers and splits |
| **Trust / client-account accounting** | Client money has strict, jurisdiction-specific rules; get this right |
| **Invoice templates & approval** | Partners review drafts before they go out |
| **Online payments** | Faster collection, fewer chasers |
| **Reporting** | Realisation, utilisation, WIP, aged debtors |
| **Accounting integration** | Avoid double entry with your bookkeeping |

## Where firms leak revenue

- Short tasks (calls, emails) that never get logged.
- Time recorded days later from memory, so it's under-recorded.
- Invoices delayed to month end, so cash flow suffers.
- Write-downs nobody analyses.
- Inconsistent rates across fee earners.

Estimate your own leakage: multiply the unrecorded minutes per fee earner per day by your hourly rate and working days. Even small daily gaps add up across a year.

## Automation that helps

- Auto-suggest time entries from emails, calendar events and documents opened.
- Draft invoices automatically at set intervals for review.
- Automated payment reminders.
- Weekly digests of unbilled time per fee earner.

## Buying vs building

If a standard tool covers your fee structures, buy it. If you have unusual billing (per-claim fees, panel arrangements, referral fee splits), automation layered on top of your system, or a custom billing module, may be the fix. [Contact me](/#contact) with how you bill today.

*Trust accounting and tax rules vary by jurisdiction; confirm compliance with your regulator or accountant.*

## Frequently asked questions

### What should legal billing software include?
Time and expense capture, flexible rate and fee arrangements, invoice generation, payment collection, trust or client-account handling that meets your jurisdiction's rules, and reporting on realisation and aged debt.

### Can general accounting software handle law firm accounts?
Often not on its own. Client money (trust) accounting has specific rules. Many firms pair general accounting software with legal billing, or use a legal-specific system.

### How do I reduce unbilled time?
Make time capture frictionless (from email, calendar and documents), review unbilled time weekly, and automate invoice drafts so billing isn't a month-end scramble.
