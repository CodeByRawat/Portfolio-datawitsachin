<p class="lede">Opening a gym can cost from <strong>roughly $10,000–$50,000 for a small personal training studio</strong>, to <strong>$50,000–$250,000+ for a boutique or functional gym</strong>, to <strong>$500,000 or more for a large full-service health club</strong>. The biggest costs are <strong>lease deposits and build-out</strong>, <strong>equipment</strong>, and <strong>working capital</strong> to cover expenses until membership revenue covers costs. Figures vary widely by city, size and whether you buy new, used or leased equipment.</p>

## Estimated startup costs by gym type (rough ranges)

| Gym type | Typical size | Rough startup cost |
|---|---|---|
| Personal training studio | 500–1,500 sq ft | ~$10,000–$50,000 |
| Boutique studio (yoga, Pilates, HIIT, cycling) | 1,500–3,000 sq ft | ~$50,000–$250,000+ (specialised equipment like reformers or bikes adds cost) |
| Functional/strength gym | 3,000–6,000 sq ft | ~$75,000–$250,000 |
| 24/7 access gym | 4,000–10,000 sq ft | ~$150,000–$500,000+ |
| Full-service health club | 15,000+ sq ft | $500,000–several million |
| Franchise | Varies | Franchise fee plus build-out; see franchise disclosure documents |

These are illustrative ranges, not quotes; get local estimates.

## Cost breakdown

| Category | What's included |
|---|---|
| **Lease deposit and rent before opening** | Security deposit, first months' rent |
| **Build-out/renovation** | Flooring, mirrors, lighting, HVAC, changing rooms, showers, soundproofing |
| **Equipment** | Racks, weights, cardio machines, specialty gear |
| **Technology** | Gym software, access control, sound system, website (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/)) |
| **Licences, legal and insurance** | Business registration, permits, contracts, liability insurance |
| **Branding and pre-launch marketing** | Signage, website, ads, opening event |
| **Staff hiring and training** | Pre-opening payroll |
| **Working capital** | 3–6+ months of operating costs |

## Monthly running costs

Rent, payroll, utilities, equipment maintenance/financing, software subscriptions, insurance, cleaning, marketing and payment processing fees.

## How to reduce startup costs

- Start as a **personal training or small studio**
- Buy **quality used equipment** or **lease**
- Choose a space needing **minimal build-out**
- **Pre-sell memberships** before opening
- Use affordable **software and DIY marketing** at first
- Share space or sublet off-peak hours

## Break-even thinking

**Members needed to break even ≈ monthly costs ÷ average monthly revenue per member.** For example, $20,000 monthly costs ÷ $100 average membership = 200 members. (Profitability: [Are gyms profitable?](/blog/are-gyms-profitable/))

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to open a gym?
From about $10,000–$50,000 for a small personal training studio to $50,000–$500,000+ for boutique, functional or 24/7 gyms, and much more for large health clubs.

### How much does it cost to start a small gym?
A small studio can often start in the tens of thousands of dollars, depending on rent, build-out and equipment.

### What is the biggest expense when opening a gym?
Usually the lease and build-out, followed by equipment.

### Can I open a gym with no money?
It's difficult, but you can start very small, use financing or leasing, partner with investors and pre-sell memberships.

### How much should I budget for gym equipment?
Anywhere from a few thousand dollars for a basic studio to hundreds of thousands for a large gym; used and leased equipment reduces upfront costs.

### How many members does a gym need to be profitable?
Divide total monthly costs by average monthly revenue per member to find break-even, then aim well above it.
