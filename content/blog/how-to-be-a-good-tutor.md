<p class="lede">A <strong>tutor</strong> gives individual or small-group academic support to help students understand material, build skills and reach goals. To be a good tutor: <strong>build rapport</strong>, <strong>diagnose gaps</strong>, set <strong>clear goals</strong>, plan <strong>structured sessions</strong>, explain simply with <strong>examples</strong>, make the student <strong>do the thinking</strong> (questions and practice), give <strong>specific feedback</strong>, teach <strong>study skills</strong>, <strong>track progress</strong> and keep <strong>parents informed</strong>.</p>

## What does a tutor do?

Assesses understanding, explains concepts, guides practice, reviews homework and tests, builds confidence and study habits, and reports progress. Tutoring complements classroom teaching with personalised attention.

## 15 tips for effective tutoring

1. **Build rapport** and a safe space for mistakes
2. **Diagnose** with a short assessment
3. **Set goals** with the student (and parents)
4. **Plan sessions:** warm-up, teach, guided practice, independent practice, recap
5. **Explain simply** with examples and visuals
6. **Ask, don't tell:** guiding questions build understanding
7. **Active practice:** the student should work most of the time
8. **Check understanding** often ("explain it back to me")
9. **Adapt** to pace and preferences
10. **Teach study skills:** note-taking, spaced repetition, test strategies
11. **Specific feedback** and praise for effort and strategy
12. **Homework and review** between sessions
13. **Track progress** with records and mini-tests
14. **Communicate with parents** regularly
15. **Keep learning** your subject and teaching methods

## Tutoring online effectively

Stable connection, camera on, digital whiteboard, shared documents, shorter segments, interactive tools and clear session notes.

## How to become a tutor

Choose subjects you know well, practise explaining them, get references, consider certifications, and start with your network or tutoring platforms, or start your own business (see [How to start a tutoring business](/blog/how-to-start-a-tutoring-business/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I be a good tutor?
Build rapport, assess needs, set goals, plan structured sessions, explain clearly, use questioning and practice, give feedback and track progress.

### What does a tutor do?
Provides personalised academic support: explaining concepts, guiding practice, reviewing work and building confidence and study skills.

### What is the difference between a tutor and a teacher?
Teachers instruct whole classes on a curriculum; tutors give individual or small-group support tailored to a student's needs.

### How do I become a math tutor?
Master the levels you'll teach, practise explaining, gather references and start with your network, platforms or your own business.

### How long should a tutoring session be?
Commonly 45–60 minutes; shorter for young children, longer for exam prep.

### How do I tutor elementary students?
Use short activities, games, visuals and frequent praise, and keep parents closely involved.
