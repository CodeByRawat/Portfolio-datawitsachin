<p class="lede">To find commercial cleaning contracts: choose <strong>target industries</strong> (offices, medical clinics, schools, gyms, retail, property management), get <strong>commercial insurance and bonding</strong>, build a <strong>professional website and capability statement</strong>, then find opportunities through <strong>direct outreach to facility and property managers</strong>, <strong>bid platforms and government procurement portals</strong>, <strong>subcontracting</strong> for larger cleaning companies, and <strong>networking</strong>. Win with thorough walkthroughs, accurate pricing and clear proposals.</p>

## Target industries

| Industry | Notes |
|---|---|
| Offices | Steady after-hours contracts |
| Medical and dental clinics | Higher standards (infection control), higher pricing |
| Schools and childcare | Seasonal deep cleans, background checks |
| Gyms | High-frequency cleaning |
| Retail and restaurants | Early morning/late night |
| Property management/HOAs | Common areas, multiple sites |
| Construction | Post-construction cleans |

## Requirements to compete

- General liability, workers' comp, commercial auto
- Janitorial bond
- References and case studies
- Safety training and documentation
- Background-checked staff
(Licensing basics: [What licenses are needed to start a cleaning business?](/blog/what-licenses-are-needed-to-start-a-cleaning-business/))

## Where to find contracts

1. **Direct outreach:** visit or email office managers, clinic managers, gym owners
2. **Property and facility managers** who oversee multiple buildings
3. **Bid platforms and RFP sites**
4. **Government procurement portals** (local, state, national)
5. **Subcontracting** for national janitorial companies or general contractors
6. **Networking:** chambers, BNI, building owner associations
7. **Google search visibility** for "commercial cleaning [city]" (see [What is SEO?](/blog/what-is-seo/))
8. **Referrals** from existing commercial clients

## Walkthrough, pricing and proposals

- Walk the site; measure square footage, floor types, restrooms, frequency, special needs
- Estimate labour hours × rate + supplies + overhead + margin (or per square foot)
- Proposal: scope by area and frequency, checklist, staffing, quality control, insurance, price, terms
- Follow up promptly; offer a trial period

## Starting a contract cleaning business

Start with small offices to build references, then pursue larger contracts; invest in scheduling, quality inspections and staff retention (see [How to start a cleaning business](/blog/how-to-start-a-cleaning-business/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find commercial cleaning contracts?
Target industries, get insured and bonded, then reach out to facility and property managers, use bid and government procurement sites, subcontract and network.

### How do I bid on a commercial cleaning job?
Do a walkthrough, estimate labour and supplies, then submit a clear proposal with scope, frequency, quality control, insurance details and price.

### Do I need a bond for commercial cleaning?
Many commercial clients expect a janitorial bond along with liability insurance.

### What is the most profitable commercial cleaning niche?
Medical and specialised facilities often pay more due to higher standards, though requirements are stricter.

### How do I get government cleaning contracts?
Register on procurement portals, meet eligibility requirements and respond to requests for proposals.

### Can I subcontract commercial cleaning?
Yes; subcontracting for larger firms is a common way to gain experience and references.
