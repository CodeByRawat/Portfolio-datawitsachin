<p class="lede">To sell a vending machine business, prepare <strong>clean sales records per machine</strong>, confirm <strong>location agreements can be transferred</strong>, value the business on <strong>cash flow plus equipment</strong>, list it with <strong>brokers, online marketplaces or local operators</strong>, and plan a smooth <strong>route handover</strong> to the buyer.</p>

## How vending businesses are valued

Buyers look at **net cash flow** (often monthly or yearly), **equipment age and condition**, **location quality and contract terms**, and how much work the route requires.

## Prepare to sell

| Prepare | Why |
|---|---|
| Sales by machine and location | Proves revenue |
| Expense records | Shows real profit |
| Location agreements | Confirms buyers keep locations |
| Equipment list | Age, model, card readers |
| Route schedule | Shows time required |

## Finding buyers

- Local vending operators wanting to expand
- Online business-for-sale marketplaces
- Business brokers
- Vending owner communities

## Steps to sell

1. Value the business
2. Gather records
3. Get location permission for transfer
4. List and screen buyers
5. Negotiate price and terms
6. Sign a purchase agreement
7. Introduce the buyer to locations and train them on the route

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I sell my vending machine business?
Value it, prepare records, confirm location transfers, list it and hand over the route.

### How much is a vending route worth?
Mainly a multiple of its cash flow, adjusted for equipment and locations.

### Can I sell vending machines individually?
Yes, with or without their locations, but machines in proven locations sell for more.

### Where do I list a vending business for sale?
Business marketplaces, brokers and local operators.

### Do locations transfer to the buyer?
Only if agreements allow it or locations agree.

### How do I increase the sale value?
Clean records, strong locations, modern card-enabled machines and documented routes.
