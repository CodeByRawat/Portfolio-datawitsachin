<p class="lede">According to Google Ads guidance, a good landing page experience means the page is <strong>relevant to the ad and the search</strong>, offers <strong>useful, original content</strong>, is <strong>transparent and trustworthy</strong> (clear about the business, its offer and how data is used), is <strong>easy to navigate, especially on mobile</strong>, <strong>loads quickly</strong>, and makes it <strong>easy for people to do what they came for</strong>. Landing page experience is one of the three components of <strong>Quality Score</strong>, alongside expected click-through rate and ad relevance.</p>

If you've seen the question "Which attributes describe a good landing page experience?" in a Google Ads certification or quiz, the answer centres on **relevance, usefulness, transparency, easy navigation (mobile-friendly) and speed**. This guide explains each attribute and how to improve it.

## The attributes of a good landing page experience

| Attribute | What it means | How to achieve it |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevant, useful and original content** | The page directly addresses what the searcher wanted and what the ad promised | Match headline to keyword and ad; specific information, not generic filler |
| **Transparency and trustworthiness** | Clear who you are, what you offer, prices/terms, and how personal data is used | Business details, contact info, privacy policy, honest claims, reviews |
| **Easy navigation (mobile-friendly)** | Visitors find what they need without effort on any device | Responsive design, clear layout, large buttons, no intrusive pop-ups |
| **Fast loading** | Page appears quickly, even on mobile networks | Optimised images, minimal scripts, good hosting, Core Web Vitals |
| **Encourages engagement/conversion** | It's easy to take the next step | Clear CTA, short forms, click-to-call, booking, chat |

## Why it matters: Quality Score and costs

Google Ads rates **landing page experience** as "Above average", "Average" or "Below average" for each keyword. Together with **expected CTR** and **ad relevance**, it forms **Quality Score (1–10)**. Higher quality generally means:
- **Better ad positions**
- **Lower cost per click**
- **More conversions** from the same traffic

A poor landing page can make you pay more for every click and still convert poorly.

## How to check your landing page experience

1. In Google Ads, go to **Keywords** and add the columns **Quality Score**, **Landing page exp.**, **Exp. CTR** and **Ad relevance**.
2. Use **Landing pages** reports to see mobile-friendliness and speed metrics.
3. Test speed with **PageSpeed Insights**.
4. Check mobile usability on real phones.
5. Look at analytics: bounce/engagement rate, conversion rate by landing page.

## Checklist to improve landing page experience

**Relevance**
- [ ] Headline reflects the keyword and ad promise
- [ ] Separate pages for distinct services or audiences
- [ ] Above-the-fold content answers "Am I in the right place?"

**Useful content**
- [ ] Clear explanation of the offer, pricing or next steps
- [ ] Original photos, details and FAQs
- [ ] Benefits and proof (reviews, results)

**Trust**
- [ ] Business name, address/phone or contact details visible
- [ ] Privacy policy linked near forms
- [ ] No misleading claims; terms clearly stated
- [ ] HTTPS and no broken elements

**Navigation and mobile**
- [ ] Responsive layout, readable text, big tap targets
- [ ] No intrusive interstitials blocking content
- [ ] Logical structure with clear CTA

**Speed**
- [ ] Images compressed (WebP/AVIF) and sized correctly
- [ ] Minimal third-party scripts
- [ ] Fast hosting/CDN
- [ ] Good Largest Contentful Paint and Interaction to Next Paint

**Conversion**
- [ ] One clear call to action, repeated
- [ ] Short form, click-to-call, WhatsApp or booking
- [ ] Instant follow-up after submission (see [How to automate your sales process](/blog/how-to-automate-your-sales-process/))

## Common causes of "Below average" landing page experience

- Sending all ads to the homepage
- Slow, heavy pages with sliders and large videos
- Content not matching the keyword (e.g., ad for "emergency plumber" landing on a general services page)
- Thin or duplicated content
- Pop-ups covering content on mobile
- Missing contact information or privacy policy

## Landing page experience beyond Google Ads

The same principles apply to Meta, LinkedIn and TikTok ads, and to SEO: relevant, fast, trustworthy pages convert better everywhere. (Building one: [What is a landing page?](/blog/what-is-a-landing-page/). Ad planning: [How to make an advertisement](/blog/how-to-make-an-advertisement/).)

## Frequently asked questions

### Which attributes describe a good landing page experience?
Relevant, useful and original content; transparency and trustworthiness; easy navigation on mobile and desktop; fast loading; and making it easy for visitors to take the desired action.

### What is landing page experience in Google Ads?
Google's estimate of how relevant and useful your landing page is to people who click your ad. It's one of three components of Quality Score.

### How does landing page experience affect Quality Score?
A "Below average" rating lowers Quality Score, which can raise costs per click and reduce ad positions; "Above average" helps both.

### How can I improve my landing page experience quickly?
Match the headline to the keyword and ad, speed up the page, make it mobile-friendly, add clear contact details and privacy information, and simplify the call to action.

### Does page speed affect landing page experience?
Yes. Slow pages frustrate users and are a factor Google considers, especially on mobile.

### Should each ad group have its own landing page?
Ideally, yes for distinct services or intents, because closer relevance usually improves both landing page experience and conversion rate.
