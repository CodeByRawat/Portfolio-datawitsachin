<p class="lede"><strong>CPM</strong> means <strong>cost per mille</strong>, or <strong>cost per thousand impressions</strong>: what an advertiser pays for 1,000 views of an ad. To calculate it: <strong>CPM = (total ad cost ÷ total impressions) × 1,000</strong>. If you spend $50 and your ad is shown 10,000 times, your CPM is <strong>$5</strong>. CPM is used to measure and buy reach and awareness, and to compare the cost of attention across platforms.</p>

## How to calculate CPM

**CPM = (Cost ÷ Impressions) × 1,000**

| Spend | Impressions | CPM |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | 20,000 | $5.00 |
| $250 | 40,000 | $6.25 |
| $1,000 | 80,000 | $12.50 |

**Reverse formulas:**
- **Cost = CPM × Impressions ÷ 1,000**
- **Impressions = Cost ÷ CPM × 1,000**

## Typical CPM ranges

CPMs vary widely by country, audience, season, placement and competition. Very rough illustrations for the US:
- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** often single digits to the teens or more, spiking during Q4 holidays
- **TikTok:** frequently similar to or lower than Meta, varies by objective
- **LinkedIn:** typically much higher (professional audiences)
- **YouTube:** varies by format and targeting
- **Display networks:** often low
- **Connected TV:** typically higher (premium inventory)

Always benchmark against your own account data.

## What affects CPM?

- **Audience size and competition** (narrow, high-value audiences cost more)
- **Season** (Black Friday, holidays)
- **Placement and format** (Stories, Reels, feed, in-stream)
- **Objective** (conversion objectives often have higher CPMs than reach)
- **Ad quality and engagement:** platforms reward relevant, engaging ads
- **Country and language**
- **Frequency and creative fatigue**

## CPM vs CPC vs CPA vs vCPM

| Metric | Meaning | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **CPM** | Cost per 1,000 impressions | Awareness, comparing reach cost |
| **vCPM** | Cost per 1,000 viewable impressions | Display/video where viewability matters |
| **CPC** | Cost per click | Traffic |
| **CPA / CPL** | Cost per acquisition / lead | Performance goals |
| **ROAS** | Revenue ÷ ad spend | E-commerce profitability |

**Relationship:** CPC = CPM ÷ (CTR × 1,000). A low CPM is useless if nobody clicks or converts; a higher CPM can be fine if CTR and conversion rate are strong.

## When to use CPM bidding

- Brand awareness and reach campaigns
- Product launches and events
- Retargeting small audiences where reach matters
- Video views and top-of-funnel content

For leads and sales, optimise for conversions and judge success by CPA and ROAS.

## How to lower CPM

1. **Improve creative quality and engagement:** native, scroll-stopping videos and motion (see [How to make an advertisement](/blog/how-to-make-an-advertisement/))
2. **Refresh creatives** to avoid fatigue (see [What is dynamic creative optimization?](/blog/what-is-dynamic-creative-optimization/))
3. **Broaden audiences** or use automatic placements
4. **Test placements** (Reels/Stories often differ from feed)
5. **Avoid peak seasons** for awareness-only campaigns when possible
6. **Exclude low-value placements** in display networks
7. **Improve relevance** with matching messages per audience
8. **Use lookalike or broad targeting with strong creative**

## Frequently asked questions

### What is CPM in advertising?
CPM is cost per mille, the price paid for 1,000 ad impressions.

### How do you calculate CPM?
Divide total ad cost by total impressions and multiply by 1,000.

### What is a good CPM?
It depends on platform, audience and season; compare against your own historical data and judge alongside CTR, CPA and ROAS.

### What is the difference between CPM and CPC?
CPM is the cost per 1,000 impressions; CPC is the cost per click. CPC depends on CPM and click-through rate.

### Why is my CPM so high?
Common reasons include narrow or competitive audiences, peak seasons, low-engagement creative, ad fatigue and conversion-focused objectives.

### Is lower CPM always better?
No. A cheap impression that doesn't lead to clicks or sales is poor value; focus on cost per result and return on ad spend.
