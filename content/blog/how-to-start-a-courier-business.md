<p class="lede">To start a courier business, <strong>pick a niche</strong> (medical, legal documents, same-day B2B, auto parts or last-mile), register the business, get <strong>commercial auto and cargo insurance</strong>, use a reliable vehicle, set <strong>per-mile and per-delivery pricing</strong>, and win <strong>recurring contracts with local businesses</strong>. You can start with your own car for a few thousand dollars.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your niche

| Niche | Clients | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Medical courier | Labs, clinics, pharmacies, hospitals | Steady routes, training needed (HIPAA, specimens) |
| Legal courier | Law firms, courts | Court filings, process serving add-on |
| Same-day B2B | Offices, print shops, manufacturers | Rush deliveries |
| Auto parts | Parts stores, repair shops | Daily routes |
| Last-mile / e-commerce | Retailers, delivery networks | High volume, lower rates |
| Cannabis, floral, catering | Specialty clients | Check local rules |

Specialised niches pay more and are less crowded than general delivery.

## Step 2: Register the business

LLC, EIN, business bank account and local business license. If you use cargo vans or trucks over 10,001 lbs across state lines, you'll need a USDOT number.

## Step 3: Get insured

- Commercial auto insurance (personal auto policies usually don't cover business deliveries)
- Cargo insurance for goods in transit
- General liability
- Workers' comp when you hire drivers

## Step 4: Vehicles and startup costs

| Setup | Rough cost |
|---|---|
| Own car, part-time | $2,000 – $5,000 (insurance, phone, software, website) |
| Used cargo van | $15,000 – $35,000 |
| Small fleet | $50,000+ |

## Step 5: Set your pricing

Common models: base fee plus per-mile, flat zone pricing, rush and after-hours surcharges, wait time charges, and monthly route contracts for regular clients.

## Step 6: Use dispatch and tracking software

Clients expect online ordering, live tracking, proof of delivery (photo or signature) and invoices. Courier software handles all of these.

## Step 7: Find clients

1. Call and visit labs, law firms, pharmacies, print shops and parts stores
2. Offer a reliable daily route at a fixed monthly price
3. A website where businesses can request a quote or book a delivery
4. Google Business Profile and reviews
5. Subcontract for larger courier companies and delivery networks while you build your own clients

## Step 8: Grow

Add drivers (employees or independent contractors), add vans, and expand into nearby niches.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a courier business?
Choose a niche, register and insure the business, set pricing, use courier software and sign local business clients.

### How much does it cost to start a courier business?
From a few thousand dollars with your own car to $50,000+ for a van fleet.

### Do I need a license to start a courier service?
Usually a business license; a USDOT number only for larger commercial vehicles crossing state lines.

### Is a courier business profitable?
Yes, especially with recurring routes and specialised niches like medical or legal delivery.

### How do courier companies get clients?
By calling local businesses directly, offering reliable routes, and subcontracting for larger delivery companies.
