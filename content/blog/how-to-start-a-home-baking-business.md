<p class="lede">To start a baking business from home: check your area's <strong>cottage food laws</strong> (what foods you can sell from a home kitchen, sales limits and where you can sell), get any required <strong>permits and food handler training</strong>, choose a <strong>niche</strong> (custom cakes, cookies, bread, gluten-free), follow <strong>food safety and labelling</strong> rules, <strong>price for profit</strong>, take <strong>orders online</strong> with deposits, and sell through <strong>Instagram, local markets and partnerships</strong>.</p>

## Step 1: Cottage food laws

Many places allow selling certain "low-risk" foods (breads, cookies, cakes without perishable fillings) made at home, often with limits on sales channels or annual revenue, and labelling requirements. Some require registration or permits; others require a licensed kitchen. Check your local regulations.

## Step 2: Choose your niche

Custom celebration cakes, decorated cookies, sourdough and bread, cupcakes, gluten-free or vegan bakes, cultural specialities.

## Step 3: Kitchen and food safety

Clean, organised space; separate storage for business ingredients where required; pets kept out; temperature control; food handler certification.

## Step 4: Labelling

Product name, ingredients, allergens, net weight, your business name/address and any required "made in a home kitchen" statement.

## Step 5: Pricing

**Price = ingredients + packaging + labour (your hourly rate) + overhead + profit.** Don't underprice custom work: time spent decorating is valuable.

## Step 6: Branding and photos

Consistent packaging, logo and natural-light photos for Instagram and your order page.

## Step 7: Take orders online

Order form with product options, pickup/delivery dates and deposits; automatic confirmations and reminders (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/) and [How to schedule a WhatsApp message](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-whatsapp-message/)).

## Step 8: Where to sell

Instagram and Facebook, local farmers' markets, pop-ups, cafés and coffee shops (if allowed), corporate gifting, event planners.

## Step 9: Grow

Raise prices as demand grows, offer seasonal boxes, consider renting a commercial kitchen, and eventually open a shop (see [How to start a bakery](/blog/how-to-start-a-bakery/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a baking business from home?
Check cottage food laws, get required permits and training, pick a niche, follow food safety and labelling rules, price for profit, take orders online and sell locally.

### Can I legally sell baked goods from home?
Often yes under cottage food laws, with restrictions on foods, sales channels and labels; check local rules.

### How do I price homemade baked goods?
Add ingredient, packaging, labour and overhead costs, then a profit margin.

### Do I need a licence to sell cakes from home?
It depends on location; some require registration or permits, others restrict home sales.

### How do home bakers get customers?
Instagram, word of mouth, local markets, community groups and partnerships with event planners.

### When should I move to a commercial kitchen?
When demand exceeds cottage food limits or you want to sell wholesale or perishable products.
