<p class="lede">To start a food truck business: <strong>research local demand and rules</strong>, choose a <strong>focused concept and short menu</strong>, write a <strong>business plan</strong> with startup costs and break-even, <strong>buy or lease a truck</strong> (new, used or custom-built), secure <strong>permits, licences and a commissary kitchen</strong>, set up <strong>POS and online ordering</strong>, line up <strong>locations, events and catering</strong>, and market with <strong>social media, Google and a simple website</strong> showing where you'll be.</p>

## Step 1: Research the market

- Local food truck rules (some cities restrict locations or require permits per zone)
- Competition and gaps (cuisine, price point, dietary needs)
- High-traffic spots: office parks, breweries, campuses, events, festivals

## Step 2: Concept and menu

A clear concept (e.g., Korean tacos, gourmet grilled cheese, coffee truck) with a **short menu** of 5–8 items that can be prepped quickly in a small space. Price for **food cost around 25–35%** depending on concept.

## Step 3: Business plan

Startup costs, financing, monthly expenses, average ticket, daily customers needed, events/catering revenue, and break-even (see [How much does a food truck cost?](/blog/how-much-does-a-food-truck-cost/)).

## Step 4: Get the truck

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| New custom truck | Built to your needs | Highest cost, longer build |
| Used truck | Lower cost | Inspection, repairs, may need upgrades |
| Lease/rent | Low upfront | Ongoing cost, less control |
| Trailer or food van | Cheaper alternative | Towing, size limits |

## Step 5: Permits and licences

Business licence, food service/vendor permits, health department inspection, fire safety, food handler certificates, parking/zone permits and commissary agreement (see [Food truck permits and licences](/blog/food-truck-permits-and-licenses/)).

## Step 6: Equipment and systems

Cooking equipment, refrigeration, generator, POS with card and mobile payments, online ordering/pre-ordering, inventory and scheduling.

## Step 7: Locations, events and catering

Rotating weekly schedule, partnerships with breweries and offices, festivals, private catering and corporate lunches (often the most profitable).

## Step 8: Marketing

- **Instagram/TikTok:** daily location posts, food videos (see [How to promote a restaurant on social media](/blog/how-to-promote-a-restaurant-on-social-media/))
- **Google Business Profile** with service areas and hours
- **Simple website** with menu, weekly schedule, catering request form and online ordering
- **Truck wrap** branding
- **Loyalty programme** and WhatsApp/SMS updates

## Step 9: Operate profitably

Track daily sales by location, food cost, labour, waste and event ROI (see [How to operate a food truck business](/blog/how-to-operate-a-food-truck-business/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a food truck business?
Research local rules and demand, choose a concept and short menu, write a business plan, get a truck, secure permits and a commissary, set up POS and online ordering, and market on social media.

### How much does it cost to start a food truck?
Often from tens of thousands of dollars for a used truck to well over $100,000 for a new custom build, plus permits and working capital.

### Do food trucks make money?
Many do, especially with strong locations, catering and events, controlled food costs and good marketing.

### Do I need a commissary for a food truck?
Many cities require food trucks to operate from a licensed commissary kitchen for prep, storage and cleaning.

### What is the difference between a food truck and a food van?
Food vans are usually smaller vehicles or trailers with simpler setups; regulations can be similar.

### How do food trucks find locations?
Through city-approved spots, partnerships with breweries and offices, events and festivals, and private catering.
