<p class="lede">To track tasks at work: keep <strong>one trusted place</strong> for all tasks (a task app or project tool, not your inbox and memory), <strong>capture</strong> every commitment the moment it appears, give each task an <strong>owner, a next action and a due date</strong>, <strong>prioritise</strong> daily and review weekly, and <strong>automate the capture</strong> so tasks from emails, meetings, forms and chats land in your system without manual copying. For teams, add shared boards with clear statuses and a regular review rhythm.</p>

Most people don't fail at task tracking because they chose the wrong app. They fail because tasks live in five places: email, chat, meeting notes, sticky notes and their head. Things slip, and every day starts with "what was I supposed to do?" This guide gives you a simple, practical system for individuals and teams, shows how to use AI and automation to remove most of the manual work, and recommends tools for different situations.

## The principles of good task tracking

1. **One system.** All tasks in one trusted place. If it's not in the system, it doesn't exist.
2. **Capture everything immediately.** Commitments made in meetings, requests in email or Slack, ideas: write them down at once (or let automation do it).
3. **Make tasks actionable.** "Q3 report" is a project; "Draft the Q3 revenue section and send to Priya" is a task.
4. **Owner + due date + status.** Every task needs someone responsible, a deadline (real or target), and a clear state.
5. **Prioritise ruthlessly.** You won't do everything; decide what matters most.
6. **Review regularly.** A quick daily plan and a weekly review keep the system trustworthy.
7. **Keep it simple.** The best system is the one you'll actually maintain.

## Step 1: Choose your system

| Situation | Good options |
|---|---|
| **Individual, simple** | Todoist, Microsoft To Do, TickTick, Apple Reminders, Google Tasks |
| **Individual, notes + tasks** | Notion, Obsidian with task plugins, OneNote + To Do |
| **Team task and project tracking** | Asana, Trello, Monday.com, ClickUp, Notion, Microsoft Planner, Linear (software teams), Jira (engineering) |
| **Microsoft 365 organisations** | Planner + To Do + Teams (tasks sync across them) |
| **Google Workspace organisations** | Google Tasks + Calendar, or a team tool integrated with Workspace |
| **Client and service work** | PSA tools that combine tasks, time and billing (see [What is PSA software?](/blog/what-is-psa-software/)) |

Choose one personal task list and, if you work in a team, the team's shared tool. Avoid running parallel systems.

## Step 2: Capture tasks (and automate capture)

### Manual capture habits
- **Quick-add everywhere:** use keyboard shortcuts, mobile widgets and voice capture.
- **End every meeting** by reading out action items with owners and dates.
- **Turn emails into tasks** instead of leaving them unread as reminders.
- **Do a daily "inbox sweep"** across email, chat and notes.

### Automate capture (the biggest time saver)
This is where AI and automation transform task tracking:

- **Email → task:** forward or flag an email to create a task automatically, with AI writing a clear title, summary and suggested due date. (Most task apps support email forwarding; automation tools can add AI.)
- **Meeting → tasks:** AI meeting assistants transcribe calls, extract action items with owners and deadlines, and push them into your task tool.
- **Chat → task:** create tasks from Slack or Teams messages with a shortcut or emoji reaction.
- **Forms and requests → tasks:** internal request forms create assigned tasks automatically.
- **CRM and helpdesk → tasks:** follow-ups created automatically when deals move or tickets need action. (See [What is CRM integration?](/blog/crm-integration-and-automation/))

**Example automation (n8n, Make or Zapier):**
1. Trigger: email labelled "To do" in Gmail/Outlook.
2. AI step: extract task title, description, priority and due date as JSON.
3. Create the task in Asana/Todoist/Planner with a link back to the email.
4. If priority is high, notify you in Slack.

## Step 3: Write tasks that get done

A good task has:
- **A verb and outcome:** "Send revised proposal to Acme"
- **An owner:** one person, even if many contribute
- **A due date:** real deadlines vs target dates clearly distinguished
- **Context:** links to the email, doc or ticket
- **Size:** if it takes more than a day, break it into smaller tasks or make it a project

## Step 4: Prioritise

Use a simple method consistently:

- **Eisenhower matrix:** urgent/important. Do the important-and-urgent now, schedule important-not-urgent, delegate urgent-not-important, drop the rest.
- **Top 3:** each morning, choose the three tasks that would make the day a success.
- **Time blocking:** put your most important tasks on your calendar as appointments.
- **Eat the frog:** do the hardest important task first.

AI assistants can help: ask them to review your task list against your goals and suggest priorities, but you decide.

## Step 5: Track status and progress

For teams, use a simple, shared workflow:

**To do → In progress → Waiting/Blocked → In review → Done**

- **Limit work in progress:** too many "in progress" tasks means nothing is finishing.
- **Make blockers visible:** "waiting on" tasks should name who or what you're waiting for.
- **Update status as you work,** not at the end of the week.

## Step 6: Review daily and weekly

### Daily (5–10 minutes)
- Check today's tasks and calendar
- Pick your top 3
- Process new captured tasks

### Weekly (30 minutes)
- Clear all inboxes into the system
- Review every open task: still relevant? Right date? Right owner?
- Review projects and upcoming deadlines
- Plan next week's priorities

**Automate the review prep:** a scheduled workflow can send you (or your team) a Monday summary of overdue tasks, tasks due this week and blocked items, with an AI-written summary.

## Tracking tasks as a team

### How to manage a project
1. **Define the goal, scope and success criteria.**
2. **Break the work down** into deliverables and tasks.
3. **Assign owners and dates;** identify dependencies.
4. **Choose a view:** board (Kanban) for flow, list for detail, timeline (Gantt) for schedules.
5. **Hold a short regular check-in** (weekly or more frequent for fast projects).
6. **Track risks, issues and decisions:** a simple **RAID log** (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies/Decisions) keeps them visible.
7. **Communicate status** with a brief, regular update (AI can draft it from the task tool).
8. **Close properly:** confirm deliverables, capture lessons learned.

### Team rules that make tracking work
- **If it's not in the tool, it's not a commitment.**
- **One owner per task.**
- **Status updated before meetings,** so meetings discuss decisions, not status.
- **Use comments in the task,** not side conversations, for context.
- **Keep the board clean:** archive done items, close stale tasks.

## How AI is changing task tracking in 2026

- **Automatic action-item extraction** from meetings, emails and chats
- **AI task summaries** and status reports drafted from activity
- **Smart prioritisation** suggestions based on deadlines, dependencies and goals
- **Natural-language queries:** "What's overdue for the marketing launch?"
- **AI agents** that complete simple tasks themselves (scheduling, drafting, researching, updating records) and mark them done, with your approval where needed (see [What is an AI agent?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent/))

Use AI to reduce admin and improve visibility, but keep humans responsible for commitments and priorities.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using the inbox as a to-do list.**
2. **Too many tools.** Tasks scattered across apps.
3. **Vague tasks** that are actually projects.
4. **No dates or owners.**
5. **Never reviewing,** so the list becomes untrustworthy and ignored.
6. **Over-engineering** with too many tags, fields and statuses.
7. **Tracking but not prioritising:** a long list without focus.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I track tasks at work?
Use one trusted task tool, capture every task as soon as it appears (ideally automatically from email, meetings and chat), give each task an owner, next action and due date, prioritise daily, update statuses as you work, and do a weekly review.

### What is the best way to keep track of tasks?
The best way is a single system you trust and maintain: a task app or project tool, consistent capture habits, clear actionable task descriptions, daily prioritisation and a weekly review. Automation and AI can capture tasks from email and meetings so nothing slips.

### What is the best app to track tasks at work?
For individuals, Todoist, Microsoft To Do or TickTick work well. For teams, Asana, Trello, Monday.com, ClickUp, Notion, Microsoft Planner, Linear or Jira, depending on your work type and existing tools.

### How can AI help with task management?
AI can extract action items from meetings and emails, create tasks automatically, write summaries and status reports, suggest priorities, answer questions about project status and, with AI agents, complete simple tasks.

### How do I manage a project at work?
Define the goal and scope, break work into tasks with owners and dates, choose a board or timeline view, hold regular check-ins, track risks and issues in a RAID log, communicate status regularly and close the project with lessons learned.

### What is a RAID log in project management?
A RAID log tracks Risks, Assumptions, Issues and Dependencies (sometimes Decisions) for a project, keeping important uncertainties and problems visible so they are managed proactively.
