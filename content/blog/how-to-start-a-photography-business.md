<p class="lede">To start a photography business: <strong>choose a niche</strong> (weddings, portraits, families, newborns, real estate, products, events, headshots), <strong>build skills and a strong portfolio</strong> in that niche, get <strong>reliable gear</strong> (a camera body, 1–2 versatile lenses, lighting, backups), <strong>register your business</strong> and get <strong>insurance and contracts</strong>, set <strong>packages and prices</strong> that cover your time and costs, launch a <strong>portfolio website with online booking</strong> and Google Business Profile, then win first clients through <strong>your network, referrals, social media and partnerships</strong>.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your photography niche

| Niche | Clients | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Weddings** | Couples | High ticket, seasonal, demanding |
| **Portraits and families** | Individuals, families | Repeat clients, mini sessions |
| **Newborn and maternity** | Parents | Specialised safety skills |
| **Headshots and branding** | Professionals, businesses | Fast sessions, corporate deals |
| **Real estate and architecture** | Agents, hosts, builders | Volume work, quick turnaround |
| **Product and e-commerce** | Online brands | Studio setup, repeat orders |
| **Events** | Companies, organisers | Weekday/evening work |
| **Food** | Restaurants, brands | Styling skills |
| **Stock photography/video** | Licensing platforms | Passive income (see [How to sell stock video footage](/blog/how-to-sell-stock-video-footage/)) |

A clear niche makes marketing, pricing and gear choices easier.

## Step 2: Build skills and a portfolio

- Master exposure, composition, lighting and editing
- Do styled shoots and practice sessions with friends (with model releases)
- Show 20–40 of your best images in your niche, not everything you've shot
- Get testimonials early

## Step 3: Gear and startup costs

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Camera body (+ backup eventually) | Mirrorless is common |
| Lenses | e.g., a standard zoom and a fast prime |
| Lighting | Speedlight/strobes, modifiers |
| Memory cards and storage | Dual-slot camera, backup drives, cloud |
| Editing software | Lightroom/Photoshop or alternatives (Capture One, etc.) |
| Computer | Capable of editing RAW files |

Startup costs range from a few thousand dollars (using existing gear) to more for studio space and premium lenses. Buy used or rent specialty gear to start.

## Step 4: Business setup

- Business registration and local licence
- Business bank account and bookkeeping
- **Contracts** (scope, deliverables, usage rights, cancellation, deposits)
- **Model releases** for portfolio use
- **Insurance:** liability and equipment
- Sales tax where photography/prints are taxable

## Step 5: Pricing and packages

Calculate your **cost of doing business** (gear, software, insurance, marketing, taxes, your salary), estimate time per session (shooting + editing + admin), then build packages: e.g., mini, standard, premium, plus add-ons (prints, albums, extra hours). Don't price by competitors alone.

## Step 6: Portfolio website and booking

Your website is your storefront: niche-focused galleries, pricing ("starting from"), about page, testimonials, FAQs, contact/booking with deposits, and fast-loading images (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/) and [What is a photography portfolio?](/blog/what-is-a-photography-portfolio/)).

## Step 7: Get your first clients

1. Friends, family and your network (ask for referrals)
2. **Google Business Profile** with reviews (see [How to create a Google Business page](/blog/how-to-create-a-google-business-page/))
3. **Instagram and TikTok:** behind-the-scenes Reels and client galleries
4. **Partnerships:** wedding planners, venues, real estate agents, salons, boutiques
5. **Mini-session events** (seasonal, holidays)
6. **Local SEO:** "[niche] photographer in [city]"

## Step 8: Workflow

Inquiry → consult → contract + deposit → shoot → cull/edit → gallery delivery → review request → follow-up for repeat bookings. Automate inquiries, contracts, invoices and reminders (see [How to automate your sales process](/blog/how-to-automate-your-sales-process/)).

## Step 9: Grow

Raise prices as demand grows, add products (albums, prints), build referral partners, and consider associate shooters (see [How to grow a photography business](/blog/how-to-grow-a-photography-business/)).

## Starting a photography business from home

Very common: shoot on location or outdoors, edit at home, and rent studio space when needed. Check home business rules locally.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a photography business?
Choose a niche, build skills and a portfolio, get reliable gear, register the business with contracts and insurance, set packages and prices, launch a portfolio website and Google profile, and find clients through referrals, social media and partnerships.

### How much does it cost to start a photography business?
Often a few thousand dollars for gear, software, insurance and a website; more for studio space and premium equipment.

### Do I need a licence to start a photography business?
Usually a business registration and local licence; check local requirements and sales tax rules.

### Can I start a photography business from home?
Yes. Many photographers shoot on location and edit from home, renting studios as needed.

### How do photographers get clients?
Referrals, Google Business Profile and reviews, social media, partnerships with planners and venues, and local SEO.

### What is the most profitable photography niche?
Weddings, commercial/product and branding photography often earn more per job, but profitability depends on pricing and efficiency.
