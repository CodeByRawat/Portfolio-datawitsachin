<p class="lede">To start a contracting business: choose your <strong>trade and target customers</strong> (residential remodelling, roofing, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, general contracting), obtain required <strong>licences, bonding and insurance</strong>, form a <strong>business entity</strong>, get the <strong>tools, vehicle and software</strong> you need, set up <strong>estimating, contracts and invoicing</strong>, build a <strong>network of reliable subcontractors and suppliers</strong>, and market with a <strong>website, Google Business Profile, reviews and referrals</strong>.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your trade and niche

General contractor vs specialty trade; residential vs commercial; service/repair vs projects. Niches (kitchen remodels, decks, solar, restoration) help marketing.

## Step 2: Licences, bonding and insurance

Requirements vary by country/state/city: contractor licence (often with exams and experience), business licence, **general liability insurance**, **workers' compensation** if you have employees, **surety bonds** where required, and permits for projects.

## Step 3: Business setup

Entity (LLC or local equivalent), tax registration, business bank account, accounting system, contracts and templates.

## Step 4: Tools, vehicle and costs

Startup costs depend on trade: tools, vehicle, safety equipment, software, insurance, licences, marketing and working capital.

## Step 5: Estimating and pricing

- Materials + labour + subcontractors + overhead + profit margin
- Use estimating software or templates
- Written scope, change order process and payment schedule

## Step 6: Contracts and payments

Clear contracts with scope, timeline, payment milestones, warranties and change orders; professional invoices (see [How to send an invoice](/blog/how-to-send-an-invoice/)).

## Finding subcontractors

- Referrals from suppliers and other contractors
- Trade associations and local builder groups
- Online directories and platforms
- Check licences, insurance, references and past work
- Clear subcontract agreements, scopes and payment terms

## How to become a subcontractor

1. Get trade skills and required licences
2. Register a business, get insurance
3. Build a portfolio and references
4. Contact general contractors, builders and property managers
5. Register with procurement portals for commercial/government work
6. Deliver reliably to get repeat work

## Software

Field service/contractor software for scheduling, quotes, invoicing and job tracking (see [What is field service management software?](/blog/what-is-field-service-management-software/)), plus accounting and a CRM.

## Marketing

Website with services and project galleries, Google Business Profile and reviews, yard signs, referrals and local partnerships (see [How to market your construction business](/blog/how-to-market-your-construction-business/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a contracting business?
Choose your trade, get licences, insurance and bonding, set up the business entity, acquire tools and software, create estimating and contract processes, build subcontractor relationships and market locally.

### Do I need a licence to be a contractor?
In many places yes, depending on trade and project size; check your state, provincial or local requirements.

### How do I find subcontractors?
Through referrals, suppliers, trade associations and directories, verifying licences, insurance and references.

### How do I become a subcontractor?
Get trade skills and licences, register a business with insurance, build a portfolio and reach out to general contractors and builders.

### How much does it cost to start a contracting business?
From a few thousand dollars for a small trade with existing tools to much more for vehicles, equipment and bonding.

### How do contractors get clients?
Through referrals, Google Business Profile and reviews, a professional website, yard signs and partnerships.
