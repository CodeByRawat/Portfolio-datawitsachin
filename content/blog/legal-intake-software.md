<p class="lede">Prospective clients often contact several firms and instruct the first one that responds well. <strong>Legal intake software</strong> captures every enquiry from every channel, responds instantly, qualifies the matter, runs conflict checks, books consultations, sends engagement letters for e-signature, and hands the new client into your matter system, so fewer good enquiries slip away and lawyers spend less time on admin.</p>

Your intake process decides whether an enquiry becomes a client or a missed opportunity. It's also where many firms quietly lose the most revenue: enquiries that wait a day for a call back, out-of-hours web forms nobody sees until Monday, prospects who go quiet and are never followed up. This guide explains what good intake looks like, what to automate, how AI fits, compliance points, how to choose between software, outsourced intake and custom builds, and the metrics that matter.

## Why intake matters so much

- **Speed wins.** People with a legal problem are often stressed and want reassurance quickly. The firm that responds first, clearly and professionally has a strong advantage.
- **Every lost enquiry has a real cost.** If your average matter is worth $3,000 and you lose five qualified enquiries a month through slow or missed follow-up, that's $180,000 a year.
- **Intake shapes the client relationship.** A smooth, empathetic intake sets expectations and builds trust before the first meeting. (See [How to build relationships with clients](/blog/how-to-build-relationships-with-clients/).)
- **Bad intake wastes lawyer time:** unqualified consultations, conflicts discovered late, and re-keying data into multiple systems.

## What good intake looks like

1. **Capture** every enquiry from every channel into one place: web form, chat, phone, email, text/WhatsApp, referrals, directories and ads.
2. **Acknowledge instantly,** with an automated response that sets expectations ("A member of our team will call you within 2 business hours").
3. **Qualify** with a few smart questions: matter type, urgency, jurisdiction, opposing party, key dates (limitation periods), budget or funding route.
4. **Check conflicts** before anyone invests significant time.
5. **Book** a consultation with the right lawyer straight from the confirmation.
6. **Engage:** send the engagement letter and terms for e-signature, collect ID verification and any payment on account.
7. **Hand off** to the matter or practice management system with all data already filled in.
8. **Follow up** with prospects who don't book or sign, and record why lost enquiries were lost.

## What to automate

| Stage | What to automate |
|---|---|
| **Capture** | Web forms, chat, call tracking and missed-call text-back, email parsing, all creating one record in your CRM |
| **Response** | Instant acknowledgements, out-of-hours replies, SMS confirmations |
| **Qualification** | Dynamic questionnaires by matter type; routing rules by practice area, location and value |
| **Conflicts** | Automatic search of client and party names against your database, with results to the responsible lawyer |
| **Scheduling** | Self-service booking with the right lawyer; reminders to reduce no-shows |
| **Engagement** | Auto-generated engagement letters from intake data; e-signature; ID/KYC checks; online payment |
| **Handoff** | Matter opened in your practice management system with contacts, parties, key dates and documents |
| **Follow-up** | Sequences for prospects who go quiet; review requests after matters close |
| **Reporting** | Enquiries by source, conversion rates, response times, lost reasons |

## How AI is changing legal intake

- **AI chat and voice assistants** answer common questions 24/7 (fees, process, what to bring), collect structured details, and book consultations, without giving legal advice. (See [How to create an AI chatbot](/blog/how-to-create-an-ai-chatbot/).)
- **AI triage** reads free-text enquiries ("My landlord is keeping my deposit and won't respond") and classifies matter type, urgency and potential value, routing to the right team.
- **Summaries** give the lawyer a one-paragraph brief before the call.
- **Document intake:** AI extracts key facts and dates from documents prospects upload.
- **Follow-up drafting:** personalised follow-ups for prospects who haven't booked.

Clio's legal trends research has highlighted intake automation as one of the areas where the most successful firms are using AI.

**Guardrails:** AI assistants must not give legal advice or create the impression of a lawyer-client relationship prematurely; they should clearly identify as AI, use approved wording, collect only necessary information, and hand over to a human for anything sensitive or urgent.

## Conflict checks: don't skip them

Conflict checks protect the firm and the client. Good intake software:

- Searches prospect names, related parties and opposing parties against existing clients, past clients and matters
- Handles name variations and company relationships
- Records who reviewed the conflict result and the decision
- Prevents detailed confidential information being collected before conflicts are cleared, where your rules require it

## Compliance points

- **Confidentiality starts before engagement.** Information from prospective clients can be protected (in the US, see ABA Model Rule 1.18 on duties to prospective clients). Secure your forms, chat and storage accordingly.
- **No legal advice from bots or non-lawyers.** Use clear disclaimers; train intake staff on what they can and can't say.
- **Advertising and solicitation rules:** follow your regulator's rules on marketing, testimonials, follow-up contact and text messaging (e.g., TCPA considerations in the US for automated texts and calls).
- **Data protection:** consent, retention periods and privacy notices (GDPR, CCPA and similar laws).
- **AML/KYC:** where required, verify identity and source of funds before acting.

## Intake software, intake companies and custom builds

You'll see three approaches:

### 1. Intake software you configure
Examples include **Clio Grow**, **Lawmatics**, **Law Ruler**, **Lead Docket** and intake modules in practice management systems such as **Smokeball**, **MyCase**, **PracticePanther** and **LEAP**. **Pros:** proven workflows, integrations, reporting. **Cons:** configuration effort; may not fit unusual workflows.

### 2. Outsourced intake companies
Call centres and virtual receptionist services (such as Smith.ai, Ruby and legal-specific intake teams) answer calls and chats 24/7 and log enquiries in your systems. **Pros:** human answering around the clock. **Cons:** ongoing cost; quality depends on scripts and training; still need good systems behind them.

### 3. Custom intake workflows
Forms, automations and AI built around your specific practice (for example, high-volume claims with referral partners, multi-step eligibility checks, or integration with an insurer or government portal). **Pros:** exact fit, can integrate anything. **Cons:** you need a partner to build and maintain it.

**Many firms combine all three:** off-the-shelf CRM and intake software, an answering service for calls, and custom automations to fill the gaps.

### Questions to ask any intake provider
- How quickly does every enquiry get a response, including out of hours?
- How do leads reach our CRM and matter system, and is anything re-keyed?
- How are conflicts checked, and when?
- What happens to prospect data, where is it stored, and for how long?
- What reporting do we get on sources, conversion and response times?
- How do we change questions and routing ourselves?

## Metrics to track

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Response time** (enquiry to first meaningful contact) | The biggest driver of conversion |
| **Enquiry-to-consultation rate** | Quality of qualification and follow-up |
| **Consultation-to-instruction rate** | Quality of consultations and pricing |
| **Conversion by source** | Where to invest marketing budget |
| **Lost reasons** | Pricing, availability, fit, no response |
| **No-show rate** | Effectiveness of reminders |
| **Time from enquiry to engagement signed** | Friction in the process |
| **Cost per acquired client** | Marketing and intake efficiency |

## A simple intake workflow you can build this month

1. Website form and chat create a contact and enquiry in your CRM (see [Legal CRM](/blog/legal-crm-for-law-firms/)).
2. Instant email and SMS acknowledgement with a booking link.
3. AI summarises the enquiry and suggests matter type and urgency.
4. Routing assigns it to the right lawyer; Slack/Teams alert if urgent.
5. Conflict check runs automatically; results go to the lawyer.
6. If no booking within 24 hours, a follow-up sequence starts.
7. After the consultation, the engagement letter is generated and sent for e-signature.
8. On signature, the matter opens automatically in your practice management system (see [Law practice management software](/blog/law-practice-management-software/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is legal intake?
The process of capturing a potential client's details, assessing whether you can and should take the matter, running conflict checks, and moving them to engagement and an open matter.

### What is legal intake software?
Tools that automate intake: web forms and chat, call or message capture, qualification questions, conflict checks, scheduling, engagement letters and e-signature, and handoff into your case or CRM system, with reporting on conversion.

### Should I outsource intake or automate it?
Outsourced intake covers calls around the clock with a human voice. Automation handles forms, chat, qualification and follow-up consistently and cheaply. Many firms combine the two.

### Can AI handle legal intake?
AI can answer common questions, collect structured information, classify and route enquiries, summarise them for lawyers and book consultations. It must not give legal advice, and sensitive or urgent matters should be handed to a person quickly.

### How fast should a law firm respond to enquiries?
As quickly as possible: ideally an instant automated acknowledgement and a meaningful human response within minutes to a few business hours. Prospects frequently contact several firms and choose the first that responds well.

### What's the difference between intake software and a legal CRM?
Intake software focuses on capturing, qualifying and converting new enquiries; a legal CRM manages the whole client relationship, including pipeline, referrals, marketing and past clients. Many products combine both.
