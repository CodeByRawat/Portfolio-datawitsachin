<p class="lede">Often <strong>not entirely from a home kitchen</strong>. Most state <strong>cottage food laws</strong> only cover low-risk foods (baked goods, jams, candy) and <strong>exclude perishable meats and cheeses</strong>, which are central to charcuterie. However, you can usually run the <strong>business</strong> from home (admin, marketing, orders) while preparing food in a <strong>licensed commercial or commissary kitchen</strong>. A few areas allow home kitchen permits; check locally.</p>

This is general information. Check your state and county health department.

## What cottage food laws usually allow

| Usually allowed | Usually not allowed |
|---|---|
| Breads, cookies, cakes | Cured meats |
| Jams and jellies | Cheese plates |
| Candy and popcorn | Foods needing refrigeration |
| Dry mixes | Cut fruits and vegetables (often) |

## Legal options for charcuterie businesses

1. **Rent a commissary or commercial kitchen** by the hour or month
2. **Home kitchen operation permit** where your county allows it
3. **Partner with a licensed caterer** or restaurant kitchen
4. **Teach workshops** where guests assemble their own boards (rules still apply)

## What you'll need

- Business licence and sales tax permit
- Food handler or food manager certification
- Health permit for your kitchen
- General and product liability insurance
- Allergen labelling

## Running the business from home

You can manage orders, marketing, bookkeeping and client calls from home while prepping food in a licensed kitchen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I start a charcuterie business from home?
You can run the business from home, but food usually must be made in a licensed kitchen.

### Are charcuterie boards allowed under cottage food laws?
Usually not, because they contain perishable meats and cheeses.

### What kitchen do I need for charcuterie?
A licensed commercial, commissary or permitted home kitchen.

### Do I need insurance for a charcuterie business?
Yes. Liability insurance is strongly recommended.

### Can I teach charcuterie workshops from home?
Possibly, but food safety rules still apply; check locally.
