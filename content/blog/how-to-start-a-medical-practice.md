<p class="lede">To start a medical practice: choose your <strong>specialty and practice model</strong> (traditional insurance-based, direct primary care, concierge, cash-pay or hybrid), write a <strong>business plan</strong> and secure <strong>financing</strong>, form the right <strong>legal entity</strong>, complete <strong>licensing and insurer credentialing</strong> (which can take months), choose a <strong>location</strong> and equipment, set up <strong>EHR and practice management software</strong>, hire staff, put <strong>privacy and compliance</strong> policies in place, and launch a <strong>website with online booking</strong> and local marketing so patients can find you.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your practice model

| Model | How it earns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Insurance-based** | Billing insurers and patients | Credentialing and billing complexity |
| **Direct primary care (DPC)** | Monthly membership | Simpler admin, smaller panels |
| **Concierge** | Annual fee + insurance | Premium service |
| **Cash-pay/specialty** | Self-pay services | Aesthetics, wellness, some specialties |
| **Telehealth or hybrid** | Mixed | Lower overhead |

## Step 2: Business plan and financing

Startup costs (lease, build-out, equipment, software, staff before revenue), revenue projections by payer mix, break-even timeline, and working capital (credentialing delays mean slow early cash flow). Financing: practice loans, SBA loans (US), equipment leasing, investors.

## Step 3: Legal and licensing

- Entity (often a professional corporation or PLLC/local equivalent)
- Medical licence, DEA/controlled substance registration where relevant
- Business licences, facility permits
- Malpractice insurance
- **Credentialing** with insurers (start early: 60–180 days is common)

## Step 4: Location and equipment

Patient accessibility, parking, demographics, competition, referral sources nearby, exam rooms, accessibility compliance, medical and office equipment.

## Step 5: Technology

- **EHR** and **practice management** (scheduling, billing)
- **Online booking** and reminders (see [How to schedule a doctor's appointment](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-doctors-appointment/))
- **Patient portal**, e-prescribing, telehealth
- **Revenue cycle management** (in-house or outsourced)
- **Automation** for intake forms, reminders and follow-ups (see [Automation in healthcare](/blog/automation-in-healthcare/))

## Step 6: Staff

Front desk, medical assistants, nurses, billing; clear workflows and training.

## Step 7: Compliance

Privacy and security (e.g., HIPAA in the US), OSHA/workplace safety, clinical protocols, record retention, and marketing rules for healthcare.

## Step 8: Marketing and launch

Website with services, doctor bios, insurance accepted and online booking; Google Business Profile; physician referral relationships (see [How to increase referrals in private practice](/blog/how-to-increase-referrals-in-private-practice/)); community events. (See [What is healthcare marketing?](/blog/what-is-healthcare-marketing/))

## Buying an existing practice

Faster patient base and cash flow. Evaluate financials, payer contracts, patient retention, staff, equipment, lease and reputation; use a healthcare attorney and valuation expert.

## Running a medical practice day to day

Track appointments, no-show rate, collections, days in A/R, patient satisfaction and provider productivity; review weekly and automate admin.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a medical practice?
Choose your model, write a business plan, secure financing, form a legal entity, get licensed and credentialed, set up location, equipment and EHR, hire staff, ensure compliance and market your services.

### How long does it take to start a medical practice?
Often six to twelve months, with insurer credentialing a common bottleneck.

### How much does it cost to start a medical practice?
It varies widely by specialty and model, from modest amounts for lean or telehealth practices to large investments for equipment-heavy specialties.

### Should I buy or start a practice?
Buying gives immediate patients and revenue but requires careful due diligence; starting gives full control but slower growth.

### What software does a new medical practice need?
EHR, practice management and billing, online scheduling, patient portal, telehealth and secure communication.

### How do new practices get patients?
Through physician referrals, insurer directories, Google Business Profile, a strong website with online booking and community outreach.
