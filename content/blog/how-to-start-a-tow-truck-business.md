<p class="lede">To start a tow truck business, <strong>pick your niche</strong> (light-duty, roadside, private property impounds or heavy-duty), <strong>form an LLC and get your state towing license, USDOT number and insurance</strong>, buy a <strong>used rollback or wrecker</strong>, then get calls from <strong>motor clubs, police rotation lists, body shops and Google</strong>. Most new owners start with one truck and drive it themselves.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your type of towing

| Type | What it covers | Startup cost |
|---|---|---|
| Light-duty | Cars, small SUVs, roadside help | Lowest |
| Roadside assistance | Jump starts, lockouts, tire changes, fuel | Very low (no tow truck needed to begin) |
| Private property / impound | Towing from lots under contract | Needs a storage yard |
| Medium-duty | Box trucks, vans, small buses | Higher |
| Heavy-duty | Semis, buses, recovery | Highest; experienced operators only |

Light-duty plus roadside assistance is where most people start.

## Step 2: Research local rules

Towing is regulated by state, county and city. Check:

- State towing license or permit (some states require one, many cities do)
- USDOT number if you tow across state lines or the combined weight is over 10,001 lbs
- A CDL if your truck's gross weight rating is over 26,000 lbs (most light-duty rollbacks are under this)
- Non-consent towing and impound rules, including maximum fees
- Storage yard zoning

## Step 3: Form the business

Register an LLC, get an EIN, open a business bank account and set up bookkeeping from day one. Fuel, maintenance and insurance add up quickly.

## Step 4: Get insurance

Towing insurance is one of your biggest costs. Typical coverage:

- Commercial auto liability
- On-hook coverage (damage to the car you're towing)
- Garagekeepers (vehicles in your storage yard)
- General liability
- Workers' comp once you hire

## Step 5: Buy your truck and equipment

- **Rollback / flatbed:** most versatile; safe for AWD and damaged cars
- **Wheel-lift / wrecker:** faster for parking and repo work
- Used trucks cost much less than new
- Add dollies, straps, chains, lights, jump packs, lockout kit and safety vests

Full numbers: [How much does it cost to start a towing company](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-a-towing-company/).

## Step 6: Set your prices

Common charges: a hook-up fee, a per-mile rate, after-hours and holiday surcharges, winching, storage per day and roadside service calls. Check your local maximums for police and non-consent tows.

## Step 7: Get calls

1. **Motor clubs** (roadside assistance networks) send steady but lower-paying jobs
2. **Police rotation lists:** apply with your city or county
3. **Body shops, dealerships and mechanics** for transport
4. **Apartment complexes and businesses** for private property towing contracts
5. **Google Business Profile and a mobile-friendly website** with a click-to-call button, because people call the first tow company they find

## Step 8: Use dispatch software

Towing software tracks calls, drivers, invoices, photos and impounds. It also helps you get paid faster by motor clubs.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you start a tow truck business?
Choose your niche, get licenses and insurance, buy a truck, and sign up with motor clubs, police rotations and local businesses.

### Do I need a CDL to start a towing business?
Usually not for light-duty rollbacks under 26,001 lbs GVWR. Heavy-duty wreckers need a CDL.

### How many trucks do I need to start?
One. Many owners drive their first truck themselves and add trucks as calls grow.

### How do tow companies get business?
Motor clubs, police rotation lists, body shops, private property contracts and Google searches.

### Can I start towing without a tow truck?
You can start with roadside assistance (jump starts, lockouts, tires) and add a truck later.
