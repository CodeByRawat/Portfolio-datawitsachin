<p class="lede"><strong>Record to report (R2R)</strong> is the end-to-end finance and accounting process of <strong>recording</strong> business transactions in the general ledger, <strong>processing and reconciling</strong> them, <strong>closing</strong> the books each period, and <strong>reporting</strong> financial results to management, investors, regulators and tax authorities. It turns thousands of individual transactions into accurate financial statements and insights.</p>

R2R sits at the heart of the finance function. It takes in data from the other major finance processes, especially **procure to pay (P2P)** and **order to cash (O2C)**, and produces the numbers leaders use to run the business. It's also one of the most deadline-driven, manual and error-prone processes in many companies, which is why it's a prime target for automation. This guide explains R2R step by step, common challenges, KPIs, and how to automate it.

## What does R2R stand for?

**R2R stands for record to report.** You'll also see it written as **RTR**. It's one of the core end-to-end finance processes, alongside:

| Process | Scope |
|---|---|
| **P2P: Procure to pay** | Purchasing goods and services, receiving them, processing supplier invoices and paying suppliers |
| **O2C: Order to cash** | Taking customer orders, fulfilling, invoicing and collecting payment |
| **R2R: Record to report** | Recording all financial transactions, closing the books and producing reports |
| **FP&A** | Planning, budgeting, forecasting and analysis |

P2P and O2C generate transactions; R2R records, validates and reports on them.

## The R2R process step by step

### 1. Data collection and transaction recording
Financial transactions flow into the general ledger (GL) from sub-ledgers and systems: accounts payable, accounts receivable, payroll, fixed assets, inventory, banking, expenses and operational systems.

### 2. Journal entries
Accountants record entries that aren't captured automatically: **accruals** (expenses incurred but not yet invoiced), **prepayments**, **depreciation and amortisation**, **reclassifications**, **corrections**, and **allocations** (e.g., shared costs across departments).

### 3. Reconciliations
Verifying that balances are correct by comparing them with independent sources:
- **Bank reconciliations** (GL cash vs bank statements)
- **Balance sheet account reconciliations** (prepaid expenses, accruals, fixed assets, etc.)
- **Sub-ledger to GL reconciliations** (AP, AR, inventory)
- **Intercompany reconciliations** (transactions between group entities)

### 4. Period-end close
The month-end (or quarter/year-end) close: completing all entries and reconciliations, reviewing account balances, investigating variances, posting adjustments, and locking the period.

### 5. Consolidation
For groups with multiple entities: combining results, eliminating intercompany transactions, translating currencies and handling ownership structures.

### 6. Financial reporting
Producing:
- **Financial statements:** income statement (P&L), balance sheet, cash flow statement
- **Management reports:** performance by business unit, product, region; budget vs actual
- **Statutory and regulatory reports** under accounting standards (GAAP, IFRS)
- **Tax reporting**

### 7. Analysis and insight
Explaining results: variance analysis, KPI commentary, and recommendations to leadership.

### 8. Audit and compliance
Maintaining documentation, controls and evidence for internal and external audits (e.g., SOX controls in US-listed companies).

## Common R2R challenges

- **Slow close:** many companies still take a week or more to close the books each month.
- **Manual journal entries and spreadsheets:** error-prone and hard to audit.
- **Reconciliation backlogs:** hundreds of accounts reconciled manually under deadline pressure.
- **Data from many systems:** inconsistent formats and timing.
- **Intercompany mismatches** across entities and currencies.
- **Late adjustments** discovered at the last minute.
- **Limited time for analysis:** finance teams spend most of the close producing numbers rather than explaining them.
- **Control and audit risk** from manual processes.

## R2R KPIs

| KPI | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Days to close** | Time from period end to closed books |
| **Days to report** | Time to issue management/financial reports |
| **Number of manual journal entries** | Automation level and error risk |
| **% of reconciliations completed on time** | Process discipline |
| **% of reconciliations auto-certified** | Automation of low-risk accounts |
| **Post-close adjustments** | Accuracy of the close |
| **Unreconciled items / ageing** | Quality of reconciliations |
| **Audit findings** | Control effectiveness |
| **Cost of finance as % of revenue** | Efficiency |

## How to automate record to report

R2R is highly automatable because it's rule-based, repetitive and data-driven. Key automation opportunities:

### Transaction recording and journal entries
- **Recurring journals** generated automatically each period (rent, depreciation, standard accruals)
- **Rule-based allocations** of shared costs
- **Automated accruals** based on purchase orders received but not yet invoiced
- **Integrations** so sub-ledgers and operational systems post to the GL automatically

### Reconciliation automation
- **Automated bank reconciliation** with matching rules and AI for complex matches
- **Transaction matching** for high-volume accounts
- **Auto-certification** of low-risk, low-variance accounts
- **Exception workflows** routing unmatched items to the right person

### Close management
- **Close checklists** with task assignment, dependencies, deadlines and real-time status
- **Automated reminders and escalations**
- **Continuous accounting:** reconciling and reviewing daily or weekly to reduce month-end crunch

### Intercompany and consolidation
- Automated intercompany matching and eliminations
- Currency translation and consolidation in dedicated tools or ERP modules

### Reporting and analysis
- Automated report generation and distribution
- **AI-generated variance commentary** for review
- Natural-language Q&A over financial data
- Dashboards updated in near real time

### Where AI and agents help
AI goes beyond rules for the messy parts of R2R:
- Matching transactions with missing or inconsistent references
- Flagging unusual journal entries or account movements (anomaly detection)
- Drafting reconciliation explanations and variance commentary
- **AI agents** that prepare reconciliations, investigate breaks across systems and propose adjusting entries, **for human review and approval**

Gartner's 2026 finance research highlights that data extraction, AP/AR automation and report creation tend to deliver returns in under a year, while also noting that relatively few finance teams have scaled AI beyond pilots, which makes R2R automation a competitive opportunity. ## Controls: automate without losing control

- **Segregation of duties** between preparing and approving entries
- **Approval workflows** with thresholds for manual journals
- **Change control** on automation rules, bots and AI prompts
- **Complete audit trails** of automated postings and reconciliations
- **Human review** of AI-suggested entries and commentary
- **Involve auditors early** in automation design

## A roadmap to a faster, automated close

1. **Baseline:** measure days to close, manual entries, reconciliation volumes and pain points.
2. **Standardise:** chart of accounts, close calendar, policies and templates.
3. **Implement a close checklist tool** for visibility and accountability.
4. **Automate high-volume reconciliations** (bank, intercompany, high-transaction accounts).
5. **Automate recurring and standard journals.**
6. **Move towards continuous accounting.**
7. **Add AI** for matching exceptions, anomaly detection and commentary.
8. **Measure and iterate,** targeting a shorter close and more time for analysis.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is record to report (R2R)?
Record to report is the finance process of recording transactions in the general ledger, reconciling and adjusting accounts, closing the books each period, consolidating entities and producing financial and management reports.

### What does R2R stand for?
R2R stands for record to report, also written RTR. It is one of the core end-to-end finance processes alongside procure to pay (P2P) and order to cash (O2C).

### What are the steps in the R2R process?
Data collection and transaction recording, journal entries, reconciliations, period-end close, consolidation, financial reporting, analysis, and audit and compliance.

### What is the difference between R2R, P2P and O2C?
P2P covers purchasing and paying suppliers, O2C covers selling to customers and collecting payment, and R2R records all resulting transactions, closes the books and produces financial reports.

### How can R2R be automated?
Through recurring and rule-based journal entries, automated transaction matching and reconciliations, close management workflows, intercompany automation, automated reporting, and AI for exception matching, anomaly detection and variance commentary with human review.

### What is a good month-end close time?
It varies by company size and complexity, but many organisations aim to close within about three to five business days, and leading finance teams move toward continuous accounting to close even faster.
