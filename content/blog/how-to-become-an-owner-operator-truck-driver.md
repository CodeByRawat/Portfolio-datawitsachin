<p class="lede">To become an owner-operator truck driver: get your <strong>CDL and experience</strong> (often 1–2 years as a company driver), choose between <strong>leasing onto a carrier</strong> (using their authority) or running under <strong>your own authority</strong>, <strong>buy or lease a truck</strong>, set up your <strong>business and insurance</strong>, handle <strong>compliance</strong>, and find steady <strong>freight</strong>. Owner-operators can earn more than company drivers, but their <strong>net income depends on rates, miles and expenses</strong>.</p>

## Leased-on vs own authority

| | Leased onto a carrier | Own authority |
|---|---|---|
| Authority/insurance | Carrier's (with some costs to you) | Your own |
| Freight | Carrier provides loads | You find loads |
| Pay | Percentage of load or per mile | Full rate minus costs |
| Risk | Lower | Higher |

## Steps

1. CDL and experience
2. Save money and build credit
3. Decide leased-on or own authority
4. Buy or lease a truck (inspect carefully)
5. Form a business, set up accounting
6. Insurance and registrations (see [How to start a trucking company](/blog/how-to-start-a-trucking-company/))
7. Compliance: ELD, maintenance, safety
8. Find freight: carrier, brokers, load boards, direct shippers

## How much do owner-operators make?

Gross revenue can be high, but after fuel, truck payments, insurance, maintenance and other costs, net income varies widely. Successful owner-operators know their cost per mile, choose good lanes and keep equipment running.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I become an owner-operator truck driver?
Get a CDL and experience, decide between leasing onto a carrier or your own authority, acquire a truck, set up the business and insurance, and find freight.

### How much does an owner-operator truck driver make?
It varies widely; net income depends on rates, miles and expenses.

### Is it better to lease onto a carrier or get my own authority?
Leasing on is lower risk and simpler; own authority offers more control and potential profit but more responsibility.

### How much experience do I need?
Many insurers and carriers prefer at least one to two years of driving experience.

### What are the biggest owner-operator expenses?
Fuel, truck payments, insurance, maintenance and tyres.

### Can owner-operators make more than company drivers?
Often yes, but with higher risk and responsibility for all business costs.
