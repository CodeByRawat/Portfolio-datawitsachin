<p class="lede">To write a newsletter people read: <strong>decide who it's for and what value they'll get every time</strong>, pick a <strong>consistent format</strong> (one deep idea, a curated roundup or updates + tips), write a <strong>specific, curiosity-driven subject line</strong>, open with the most useful point, keep sections <strong>short and skimmable</strong>, include <strong>one main call to action</strong>, and send on a <strong>predictable schedule</strong>. Then measure opens, clicks and replies and refine.</p>

## Step 1: Define purpose and audience

- **Who** reads it? (customers, prospects, industry peers)
- **What do they get?** (tips, news, deals, insights, inspiration)
- **What do you want?** (trust, bookings, sales, event registrations)

Write a one-line promise: "Every Tuesday: one practical automation idea for small businesses."

## Step 2: Choose a format

| Format | Description | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **One big idea** | A single essay or tutorial | Thought leadership, experts |
| **Curated roundup** | Links and short commentary | Busy readers, industry news |
| **Company updates + tips** | News, offers and a helpful tip | Small businesses |
| **Digest** | Your latest articles/videos | Content publishers |
| **Promotional** | Offers and product highlights | E-commerce (mix with value) |

## Step 3: Write a subject line that gets opened

- Be **specific:** "3 ways to cut no-shows by 30%"
- Spark **curiosity** without clickbait
- Keep it **short** (roughly under 50 characters is a common guideline)
- Use **preview text** to extend the subject line
- Avoid spammy words and ALL CAPS

## Step 4: Structure the newsletter

1. **Hook:** the most valuable or interesting point first
2. **Main content:** short sections with subheadings or bullets
3. **Examples or stories** that make it real
4. **One primary CTA** (read, book, buy, reply)
5. **Optional extras:** quick links, tip of the week, behind the scenes
6. **Sign-off** from a real person

## Newsletter ideas for businesses

- How-to tips related to your service
- Customer success stories
- Answers to common customer questions
- Industry news explained simply
- Behind-the-scenes and team stories
- New products, services or features
- Seasonal checklists
- Curated tools and resources
- Exclusive offers for subscribers
- Polls and reader questions

(Content strategy: [Why is content marketing important?](/blog/why-is-content-marketing-important/))

## Design and length

- **Mobile-first:** single column, large text, big buttons
- **Mostly text with a few images** often performs well and avoids spam filters
- **Length:** as short as possible while delivering the promised value (often 200–800 words)
- **Alt text** on images; brand colours and logo at the top

## How often to send

Consistency matters more than frequency: **weekly, bi-weekly or monthly** depending on how much value you can deliver. Tell subscribers what to expect at sign-up.

## Newsletter tools

| Tool | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Mailchimp** | Small businesses, templates |
| **Kit (ConvertKit)** | Creators |
| **Beehiiv** | Newsletter-first publishers, growth tools |
| **Substack** | Writers, paid subscriptions |
| **Brevo, MailerLite** | Affordable, automation |
| **HubSpot** | B2B with CRM integration |

## Metrics to track

- **Open rate** (less reliable since Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens)
- **Click-through rate**
- **Replies** (great engagement signal)
- **Unsubscribes and spam complaints**
- **Conversions** from newsletter traffic
- **List growth**

Keep your list healthy by managing inactive subscribers (see [When to suppress inactive email subscribers](/blog/when-to-suppress-inactive-email-subscribers/)).

## Simple newsletter template

```
Subject: [Specific benefit or curiosity]
Preview: [Extends the subject]

Hi [First name],

[1–2 sentence hook with the key insight]

## [Main tip / story]
[3–6 short paragraphs or bullets]

## Quick links
- [Resource 1]
- [Resource 2]

[One clear CTA button]

[Sign-off, name, role]
P.S. [Extra nudge or question to reply]
```

## Automating your newsletter

Automate welcome sequences, segment by interests, pull your latest blog posts automatically (RSS-to-email), use AI to draft summaries for editing, and schedule sends. (See [What is content automation?](/blog/what-is-content-automation/))

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I write a newsletter?
Define your audience and promise, choose a consistent format, write a specific subject line, lead with the most useful point, keep it skimmable, include one CTA and send on a regular schedule.

### What should a business newsletter include?
Helpful tips, customer stories, company updates, offers and resources, with one primary call to action.

### How long should a newsletter be?
Long enough to deliver the promised value and no longer, typically a few hundred words for most business newsletters.

### How often should I send a newsletter?
Weekly, bi-weekly or monthly, whatever you can sustain consistently with valuable content.

### What is the best newsletter platform?
Mailchimp, Kit, Beehiiv, Substack, Brevo and MailerLite are popular; choose based on audience, budget and features like automation or paid subscriptions.

### How do I get more newsletter subscribers?
Add sign-up forms across your website, offer a lead magnet, promote on social media, include a link in your email signature and encourage sharing.
