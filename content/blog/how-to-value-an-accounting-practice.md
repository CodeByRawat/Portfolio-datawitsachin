<p class="lede">Accounting practices are commonly valued using a <strong>multiple of annual recurring revenue</strong>, adjusted for <strong>client retention, profitability, service mix, client concentration, staff stability, technology and location</strong>. Deals often include <strong>retention-based payment terms</strong> (earn-outs or clawbacks) because value depends on clients staying after the sale. Practices with recurring monthly services, strong margins and modern cloud systems usually command better valuations.</p>

## Key valuation factors

| Factor | Higher value when… |
|---|---|
| **Recurring revenue** | Monthly bookkeeping, payroll, advisory retainers |
| **Retention** | Long-standing, loyal clients |
| **Profitability** | Strong margins and efficient workflows |
| **Client concentration** | No single client dominates revenue |
| **Service mix** | Advisory and niche specialisation |
| **Staff** | Experienced team that stays post-sale |
| **Technology** | Cloud systems, documented processes (see [What tools do accountants use?](/blog/what-tools-do-accountants-use/)) |
| **Fee levels** | Prices aligned with market |

## Common valuation approaches

- **Revenue multiple:** practices are often discussed as a multiple of gross recurring fees (the multiple varies with the factors above and market conditions)
- **Earnings (EBITDA/SDE) multiple:** used for larger firms
- **Discounted cash flow:** less common for small practices

## Deal structures

- Upfront payment + retention-based payments over 1–3 years
- Earn-outs tied to revenue retained
- Seller transition period (introductions, handover)
- Non-compete and non-solicit agreements

## Buying an accounting firm: due diligence

Financial statements and fee breakdowns, client lists and retention history, engagement letters, staff contracts, technology and data security, quality review history, lease and liabilities.

## How to increase your practice's value

Grow recurring and advisory revenue, raise prices, reduce client concentration, document processes, adopt cloud tools and automation, and build a strong team (see [How to grow an accounting practice](/blog/how-to-grow-an-accounting-practice/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you value a CPA firm?
Usually as a multiple of recurring revenue, adjusted for retention, profitability, service mix, client concentration, staff and technology.

### How much is an accounting practice worth?
It depends on revenue quality and the factors above; practices with recurring fees and high retention attract higher multiples.

### How do I buy an accounting firm?
Find practices through brokers and networks, perform due diligence, agree on valuation and retention-based terms, and plan a client transition.

### What is a retention clawback?
A deal term that reduces the price if clients leave within an agreed period after the sale.

### Why do recurring fees increase valuation?
They make future revenue more predictable and transferable to the buyer.

### How can I make my accounting firm more valuable before selling?
Increase recurring advisory revenue, improve margins, document processes, modernise technology and reduce reliance on the owner.
