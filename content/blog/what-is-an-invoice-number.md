<p class="lede">An <strong>invoice number</strong> (invoice no.) is a <strong>unique identifier assigned to each invoice</strong> a business issues. It lets the seller and buyer <strong>track, reference and match payments</strong> to the right invoice, keeps accounting records organised, and is often <strong>required by tax rules</strong>. Invoice numbers should be <strong>unique and sequential</strong> (e.g., INV-1001, INV-1002, INV-1003), never reused, and appear clearly on the invoice.</p>

## Why invoice numbers matter

- **Payment matching:** clients reference the number when paying
- **Record keeping and audits**
- **Tax compliance:** many jurisdictions require unique, sequential numbering on tax invoices
- **Avoiding duplicates** and confusion
- **Customer service:** quick lookups for queries

## How to number invoices

1. **Choose a format** (see below)
2. **Start at any number** (e.g., 1001 looks more established than 1)
3. **Increase sequentially** for each new invoice
4. **Never reuse or skip** without a record
5. **Use invoicing software** to automate numbering (see [How to send an invoice](/blog/how-to-send-an-invoice/))

## Common invoice number formats

| Format | Example | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sequential** | 1001, 1002, 1003 | Simple businesses |
| **Prefix + sequence** | INV-1001 | Clear document type |
| **Date-based** | 2026-11-001 | Organising by year/month |
| **Year + sequence** | 2026-0045 | Annual resets (check local rules) |
| **Client code + sequence** | ACME-007 | Tracking per client (keep a global sequence too) |
| **Project-based** | WEB24-003 | Project businesses |
| **Location/branch prefix** | NY-1001, LA-1001 | Multi-location (ensure overall uniqueness) |

## Rules to follow

- Each number must be **unique**
- Keep a **consistent format**
- **Sequential** order helps prove no invoices are missing
- **Don't delete** issued invoices; issue a **credit note** to cancel or correct
- Follow **local tax requirements** (some countries specify format rules for e-invoicing)

## What if I make a mistake?

- **Wrong details:** issue a credit note and a new invoice with the next number (or a corrected invoice, depending on local rules)
- **Skipped number:** document the reason
- **Duplicate number:** correct immediately and inform the client

## Where to find an invoice number

Usually near the top right of an invoice, labelled "Invoice No.", "Invoice #" or "Inv. No.", next to the invoice date. In online stores, it may appear in order emails or your account's order history. (Invoices vs receipts: [Is an invoice the same as a receipt?](/blog/invoice-vs-receipt/))

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an invoice number?
A unique identifier assigned to each invoice for tracking, payment matching, record keeping and tax compliance.

### What does "invoice no." mean?
It's short for invoice number, the unique reference for that invoice.

### How do I number my invoices?
Choose a consistent format (e.g., INV-1001) and increase the number sequentially for every invoice without reusing numbers.

### Can I start my invoice numbers at any number?
Generally yes; many businesses start at 1001. Then continue sequentially.

### Do invoice numbers have to be sequential?
Many tax authorities require sequential, unique numbering; even where not required, it's best practice.

### Is an order number the same as an invoice number?
No. An order number identifies a purchase order or sales order; an invoice number identifies the bill issued for payment.
