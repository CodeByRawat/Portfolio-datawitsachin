<p class="lede">To get on the first page of Google: <strong>target keywords you can realistically win</strong> (specific, lower-competition, matching your offer), <strong>create the most helpful page for that search intent</strong>, optimise <strong>titles, headings and content</strong>, make sure the site is <strong>fast, mobile-friendly and indexable</strong>, add <strong>internal links</strong>, earn <strong>quality backlinks and reviews</strong>, and for local searches build a strong <strong>Google Business Profile</strong>. Then track rankings in Search Console and improve pages that are close to page one.</p>

## What is a SERP?

**SERP** stands for **search engine results page**: the page Google shows after a search. Modern SERPs contain more than ten blue links:

| SERP feature | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Ads** | Paid results at the top and bottom |
| **AI Overviews** | AI-generated summaries with source links |
| **Local pack / map** | Three local businesses with a map |
| **Featured snippet** | A highlighted answer from one page |
| **People also ask** | Expandable related questions |
| **Videos, images, shopping** | Media and product results |
| **Organic results** | Standard ranked web pages |

"First page" today can mean organic results, the local pack, a snippet or a mention in AI Overviews.

## Step 1: Choose winnable keywords

New and small sites rarely win broad terms like "SEO" or "website design". Target:
- **Long-tail and question keywords** ("how much does a website cost for a dentist")
- **Local modifiers** ("web designer in Pune")
- **Low keyword difficulty** terms
(Guide: [How to choose keywords for SEO](/blog/how-to-choose-keywords-for-seo/).)

## Step 2: Match search intent

Google the keyword and study the top 10: are they guides, product pages, lists, tools, local businesses? Create the same type of page, but better.

## Step 3: Create the best page for the search

- Answer the main question **immediately**
- Cover related questions comprehensively
- Add **original value:** experience, examples, data, images, video
- Use clear structure: headings, lists, tables, FAQs
- Keep it **accurate and up to date**
- Show **who wrote it** and why they're credible

## Step 4: On-page SEO

- Keyword in **title tag**, **H1**, URL and early content
- Compelling **meta description** to win clicks (see [What is a meta description?](/blog/what-is-a-meta-description/))
- **Descriptive subheadings**
- **Image alt text**
- **Structured data** (FAQ, Article, LocalBusiness, Product)

## Step 5: Technical essentials

- Page is **indexed** (check URL Inspection in Search Console)
- **Fast** (Core Web Vitals) and **mobile-friendly**
- **HTTPS**, no broken links or redirect chains
- **XML sitemap** submitted
- Clean, crawlable site structure

## Step 6: Internal links

Link to the page from related pages on your site with descriptive anchor text. Internal links help Google discover pages and understand importance.

## Step 7: Earn backlinks and mentions

Links from relevant, trusted sites remain important for competitive terms (see [How to build backlinks](/blog/how-to-build-backlinks/)).

## Step 8: Local SEO for local searches

- Complete and verified **Google Business Profile** (see [How to create a Google Business page](/blog/how-to-create-a-google-business-page/))
- Reviews, photos and posts
- Consistent citations (see [What are local citations?](/blog/what-are-local-citations/))
- Location pages on your site

## Step 9: Improve click-through rate

Higher rankings matter less if nobody clicks. Test clearer titles with benefits, numbers and the year when relevant.

## Step 10: Measure and iterate

- **Search Console › Performance:** find queries where you rank **positions 8–20** ("striking distance") and improve those pages first
- Update content, add sections, improve internal links, earn a few links
- Track rankings monthly

## Where does my site rank?

- **Google Search Console** shows average position per query and page (free)
- **Rank trackers** (Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking) track specific keywords and locations
- Manual checks are unreliable because results are personalised by location and history

## How long does it take?

Low-competition questions can reach page one in weeks; competitive terms often take 6–12 months or more. Consistency matters more than tricks.

## What doesn't work

- Paying for "guaranteed #1 rankings"
- Keyword stuffing
- Buying links or reviews
- Thin, AI-spun pages without value
- Copying competitors' content

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get on the first page of Google?
Target winnable keywords, create the most helpful page for the search intent, optimise on-page elements, fix technical issues, add internal links, earn quality backlinks and, for local businesses, optimise your Google Business Profile.

### What is a SERP?
A search engine results page: the page of results shown after a search, including ads, organic results, local packs, featured snippets and AI Overviews.

### How long does it take to rank on Google's first page?
From a few weeks for low-competition terms to many months for competitive keywords, depending on your site's authority and content quality.

### Can I pay Google to rank on the first page?
You can pay for ads that appear on the first page, but you can't pay Google for organic rankings.

### How do I check where my website ranks?
Use Google Search Console's Performance report or a rank tracking tool rather than manual searches, which are personalised.

### Why is my website not on Google?
It may not be indexed, may be blocked by robots.txt or noindex tags, may be too new, or may lack content and links. Check Search Console's URL Inspection tool.
