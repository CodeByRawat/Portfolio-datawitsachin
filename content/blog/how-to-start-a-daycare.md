<p class="lede">To start a daycare: decide between a <strong>home daycare, a daycare center or a preschool</strong>, research <strong>local licensing rules and demand</strong>, write a <strong>business plan</strong> with startup costs and tuition, find a <strong>safe, compliant location</strong>, complete <strong>licensing, inspections and background checks</strong>, hire qualified <strong>staff that meet ratio requirements</strong>, set <strong>policies, curriculum and tuition</strong>, get <strong>insurance</strong>, set up <strong>software for enrolment, billing and parent communication</strong>, and market to local families.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your model

| Model | Capacity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Family/home daycare** | Small group in your home | Lower costs; home-based licensing (see [How to start a home daycare](/blog/how-to-start-a-home-daycare/)) |
| **Group home daycare** | Larger group, may need assistant | Additional requirements |
| **Daycare/childcare center** | Commercial facility | Higher costs and revenue |
| **Preschool** | Education-focused, often age 3–5 | Curriculum requirements |
| **Franchise** | Brand and systems | Fees and royalties |

## Step 2: Research demand and rules

Waitlists at local centres, family demographics, employer needs, and your state/country's childcare licensing regulations (ratios, space, training, safety).

## Step 3: Business plan and costs

Startup costs (facility, renovations, equipment, licensing, insurance), operating costs (staff are the largest), enrolment projections, tuition and subsidies (see [How much does it cost to open a daycare?](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-daycare/)).

## Step 4: Location and safety

Square footage per child, outdoor play space, fire exits and alarms, child-safe fixtures, kitchen and bathroom requirements, accessibility, zoning approval.

## Step 5: Licensing

Application, background checks, health and fire inspections, staff training (CPR/first aid), policies and records (see [How to get a daycare license](/blog/how-to-get-a-daycare-license/)).

## Step 6: Staff

Qualified caregivers and teachers meeting **adult-to-child ratios**, background checks, training, and a director if required.

## Step 7: Policies and curriculum

Parent handbook (hours, fees, illness, pickup, discipline), daily schedules, age-appropriate curriculum, meals and nap routines.

## Step 8: Insurance

General liability, property, workers' comp, and sometimes accident and abuse/molestation coverage.

## Step 9: Systems and software

Childcare management software for enrolment, attendance, billing, staff scheduling and **parent communication** (see [How to manage daycare communication](/blog/how-to-manage-daycare-communication/)).

## Step 10: Market to families

Website with programmes, photos, tuition info and tour booking (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/)); Google Business Profile and reviews; parent groups; local employers; open days.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a daycare?
Choose a model, check licensing rules and demand, write a business plan, find a compliant location, get licensed, hire staff to meet ratios, set policies and tuition, get insurance, set up software and market to families.

### How do I open a daycare center?
Secure a suitable commercial space, meet licensing and building requirements, hire a director and staff, and complete inspections before enrolling children.

### Do I need a licence to start a daycare?
Usually yes; most places require licensing for caring for unrelated children above a small number.

### Is owning a daycare profitable?
It can be, with full enrolment, efficient staffing, appropriate tuition and controlled costs.

### How long does it take to open a daycare?
Often several months, depending on licensing, renovations and inspections.

### How do I open a preschool?
Follow similar steps with an education-focused curriculum and any additional early education requirements.
