<p class="lede"><strong>DCO (dynamic creative optimization)</strong> is an advertising technology that <strong>automatically builds many versions of an ad from interchangeable parts</strong> (headlines, images, videos, colours, offers and calls to action) and uses data and machine learning to <strong>show each viewer the combination most likely to perform</strong>, based on factors such as audience, location, device, time, weather or browsing behaviour. Instead of designing one ad, you design a system of components.</p>

DCO sits behind many of the personalised ads you see: a travel ad showing your searched destination, a retail ad featuring products you viewed, or a social ad whose headline differs from what your colleague sees. This guide explains how DCO works, how it compares with dynamic ads and A/B testing, where it's available and how to prepare creative for it.

## How dynamic creative optimization works

1. **Create components:** several headlines, primary texts, images or videos, backgrounds, offers and CTAs, designed to work together.
2. **Build a template:** the layout that defines where each component goes and the brand rules.
3. **Connect data signals:** audience segments, product feed, location, weather, time, device, past behaviour.
4. **Set rules and/or let ML decide:** rules ("show winter coats when below 5°C") and algorithms that learn which combinations perform for which audiences.
5. **Serve and learn:** the platform assembles ads in real time, measures results and shifts delivery toward winners.

## DCO vs dynamic ads vs A/B testing

| | DCO | Dynamic (product) ads | A/B testing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What changes** | Any creative element (copy, visuals, CTA, layout) | Mainly products from a catalogue feed | One variable at a time between a few versions |
| **Driven by** | Data signals + machine learning + rules | Product catalogue + user behaviour (retargeting) | Manual setup and statistical comparison |
| **Number of versions** | Hundreds to thousands | As many as products | Usually 2–5 |
| **Best for** | Personalisation at scale, many audiences and markets | E-commerce retargeting and prospecting | Learning clear causal insights |
| **Example** | Headline and image change by city and weather | "Still thinking about these shoes?" with the exact shoes | Hook A vs hook B |

**DCT (dynamic creative testing)** is a related term: using the same machinery mainly to learn which elements perform, then feeding winners into manual creative.

## Examples of DCO

- **Travel:** destination, price and imagery change based on the user's searches and departure city
- **Retail:** product images and offers change by viewed category, stock levels and season
- **Automotive:** model, dealership location and finance offer personalised by region
- **Food delivery:** menu items change by time of day (breakfast vs dinner) and weather
- **SaaS:** headline emphasises different benefits by industry or job title
- **Events and sports:** live scores or countdowns in the creative

## Where you can use DCO

| Platform | DCO-style features |
|---|---|
| **Meta (Facebook, Instagram)** | Dynamic creative / multiple text and media options, Advantage+ creative enhancements, Advantage+ catalog ads |
| **Google Ads** | Responsive search ads, responsive display ads, Performance Max asset groups, Demand Gen |
| **TikTok** | Automated creative optimisation and catalog ads |
| **LinkedIn** | Dynamic ads personalised with profile data |
| **Programmatic DSPs and DCO platforms** | Specialist tools (e.g., Innovid, Flashtalking, Celtra, Jivox, Smartly, Clinch) for display, video and CTV |

Feature names and capabilities change often; check each platform's current documentation.

## Benefits

- **Relevance at scale:** personalised ads without designing every version by hand
- **Better performance:** higher CTR and conversion when messages match context
- **Faster learning:** many combinations tested simultaneously
- **Efficiency:** one template, many markets, languages and products
- **Fresher creative:** rotating components reduces ad fatigue

## Limitations

- **Garbage in, garbage out:** weak components produce weak combinations
- **Brand consistency risk** if components weren't designed to work together
- **Less clear insight:** you learn which combination won, not always why
- **Data and privacy:** personalisation must respect consent and privacy laws; signals are reduced as third-party cookies and tracking decline
- **Volume needed:** algorithms need enough impressions and conversions to learn
- **Setup effort:** feeds, templates and tagging take work up front

## How to prepare creative for DCO

1. **Design a modular system,** not a single ad: every headline should work with every image.
2. **Vary meaningfully:** different angles (price, speed, quality, social proof), not tiny wording tweaks.
3. **Create several hooks and openings** for video (the first 3 seconds matter most).
4. **Build templates** with fixed brand elements (logo, colours, type) and flexible slots.
5. **Supply every aspect ratio:** 9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9.
6. **Use motion:** animated templates and short motion graphics often outperform static on social (see [What are motion graphics?](/blog/what-are-motion-graphics/)).
7. **Name and tag assets** clearly so reports are readable.
8. **Refresh regularly:** add new components and retire losers.

## DCO and generative AI

Generative AI is making DCO more powerful: platforms can now generate background variations, resize assets, suggest headlines and create video variations automatically. Human creative direction remains essential to keep claims accurate, brand style consistent and ideas genuinely different. (More: [How to use AI in marketing](/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-marketing/).)

## Frequently asked questions

### What is DCO in advertising?
DCO stands for dynamic creative optimization: technology that automatically assembles ad variations from creative components and uses data and machine learning to show each person the version most likely to perform.

### What are dynamic ads?
Dynamic ads automatically show products from a catalogue to people based on their behaviour, such as items they viewed. They're a common form of personalised advertising, while DCO can change any creative element.

### What is the difference between DCO and A/B testing?
A/B testing compares a few versions to learn which is better. DCO creates and serves many combinations automatically, optimising toward the best per audience in real time.

### Does Facebook have dynamic creative?
Meta offers dynamic creative options and Advantage+ creative features that combine multiple images, videos and text and optimise delivery, plus catalogue-based dynamic ads.

### What do I need for DCO?
A set of modular creative components, templates, data signals or a product feed, tracking for conversions, and enough budget and volume for the system to learn.

### Is DCO only for big brands?
No. Built-in features on Meta, Google and TikTok let small businesses use DCO-style optimisation by uploading multiple assets; specialist DCO platforms are mainly used by larger advertisers.
