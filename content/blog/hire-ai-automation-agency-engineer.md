<p class="lede">To hire the right AI automation partner: look for people who <strong>ask about your process before pitching tools</strong>, can show <strong>workflows running in production</strong>, are <strong>honest about what not to automate</strong>, build in <strong>error handling, monitoring and human review</strong>, and leave you <strong>owning the code, workflows, credentials and documentation</strong>. Start with a small, fixed-scope pilot with a clear success metric before committing to anything bigger.</p>

The AI automation market is crowded with people who've learned three tools and call themselves agencies. Some are excellent. Many deliver impressive demos that break the first week in production. And the cost of getting it wrong isn't just the fee: it's months of lost time, staff who lose faith in automation, data exposed to the wrong tools, and fragile systems nobody can maintain. Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by 2027, and MIT's 2025 research found most generative AI pilots it studied delivered no measurable P&L impact, usually because of poor integration with real workflows, not the technology. Choosing the right partner is how you avoid becoming one of those statistics.

## Who you might hire

| Role | What they do | Good fit when |
|---|---|---|
| **AI automation agency** | Team that scopes, builds and often maintains automations and AI systems | Multiple projects, need breadth (design, build, support) |
| **Freelance automation specialist / small studio** | Senior individual or small team building workflows, integrations and agents | A handful of well-defined projects; direct access to the builder |
| **AI engineer / developer (contract or in-house)** | Builds AI applications, agents, RAG systems and integrations in code | Custom, high-volume or product-grade AI systems |
| **No-code automation consultant** | Builds on Zapier, Make, n8n, Power Automate, Airtable | Team-level workflows, quick wins |
| **RPA developer** | Builds bots on UiPath, Automation Anywhere, Power Automate Desktop | Legacy systems without APIs (see [What is robotic process automation?](/blog/what-is-robotic-process-automation/)) |
| **Large consultancy / systems integrator** | Strategy, change management and large-scale implementation | Enterprise-wide programmes |
| **In-house automation team** | Builds and runs automations continuously | Ongoing, large-scale needs |

### What does an AI automation engineer do?
They design and build automated workflows and AI-powered systems: mapping processes, integrating APIs, building AI agents and document pipelines, designing prompts and tools, handling errors, setting up evaluation and monitoring, and maintaining the result in production. (See [How to create an AI agent](/blog/how-to-create-an-ai-agent/) and [What are agentic workflows?](/blog/what-are-agentic-workflows/))

## What to look for

- **They ask about your process first.** If the first call is a tool pitch, leave.
- **They talk about outcomes:** hours saved, response times, error rates, revenue, not features.
- **Real examples** of workflows in production, with results, not just demo videos.
- **Honest about limits:** willing to say "don't automate that" or "a spreadsheet will do".
- **They handle failure:** error handling, retries, monitoring, alerts and logs are part of the build.
- **They test AI properly:** evaluation sets of real cases, accuracy measurement, human review for low-confidence outputs.
- **Security-minded:** least-privilege access, credential management, data processing terms, awareness of prompt injection.
- **You own the result:** code, workflows, credentials and documentation are yours.
- **They plan for maintenance:** who fixes it when an API changes?
- **Domain understanding,** or a willingness to learn your business properly.

## Red flags

- **Guaranteed "10x" results** with no knowledge of your operation
- **Tool-first thinking:** "we use X for everything"
- **Locking you into their platform,** accounts or an unlimited retainer
- **Workflows built in their accounts** with your credentials, so you can't access or move them
- **No plan for human review** of AI output
- **No testing on your real data** before launch
- **Vague pricing,** with no scope, milestones or acceptance criteria
- **Can't explain how it works** in plain language
- **Dismissive about data protection** ("it's fine, everyone uses ChatGPT")
- **"AI agents will replace your team"** promises

## 15 questions to ask before you hire

**About the approach**
1. What would the pilot be, and how will we measure success?
2. What would you *not* automate in our process, and why?
3. How will you handle exceptions and edge cases?
4. Where will humans review or approve?

**About reliability**
5. What happens when it breaks, and who finds out first?
6. How do you test AI accuracy before launch, and monitor it after?
7. How do you handle API changes and updates?

**About data and security**
8. Where does my data go, and which AI providers and tools are involved?
9. What data processing terms apply (training, retention, location)?
10. How are credentials stored and access controlled?

**About ownership and commercials**
11. What do I get at the end (source code, workflows, documentation, handover)?
12. Whose accounts will the automations run in?
13. How is pricing structured, and what's included in support?
14. Who actually does the work, and will they be available after launch?
15. Can I speak to two clients with automations running for six months or more?

## Engagement and pricing models

| Model | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed-scope pilot** | Defined workflow, deliverables and success metric for a fixed fee | First projects (recommended) |
| **Fixed-price project** | Larger defined scope with milestones | Clear requirements |
| **Time and materials** | Pay for hours/days worked | Exploratory or evolving work |
| **Retainer** | Monthly fee for maintenance, improvements and new automations | Ongoing programmes after a successful pilot |
| **Value-based** | Fee linked to outcomes or savings | Mature relationships with measurable outcomes |

**What drives cost:** number of systems integrated, data quality, complexity of decisions, AI evaluation requirements, security/compliance needs and ongoing support, not buzzwords. A single well-defined workflow is a small project; multi-system AI agents with governance are larger. Also budget for **running costs:** platform subscriptions, AI model usage and hosting.

## Contracts: protect yourself

- **Intellectual property:** you own (or have a perpetual licence to) everything built for you, including code, workflows and prompts.
- **Accounts:** automations run in your accounts and tenants, not the provider's.
- **Data protection:** a data processing agreement where personal data is involved; list of subprocessors (including AI providers).
- **Acceptance criteria:** what "done" means, measured how.
- **Support and warranty:** a defect-fix period after launch; response times for support.
- **Documentation and handover:** architecture, how-to-run, how-to-fix, credentials inventory.
- **Exit:** handover assistance if the relationship ends.

## A good first project

Small, valuable and measurable: **one workflow** with clear volume, delivered in **a few weeks**, with a **baseline** to compare against. Use the [readiness checklist](/blog/how-to-automate-business-processes/#which-business-processes-are-suitable-for-automation) to pick it. Good candidates:

- Inbound lead capture, enrichment and routing
- Email triage with drafted replies
- Invoice or document data extraction into your systems
- Meeting notes to CRM and tasks
- Weekly reporting pulled from several tools

If it works, expand. If it doesn't, you've lost little and learned a lot.

## How to run the engagement

1. **Kickoff:** agree the success metric, scope, access, and who on your side owns the process.
2. **Process mapping:** the partner documents how the work really happens today, including exceptions.
3. **Prototype:** quick working version on real (or realistic) data.
4. **Evaluation:** test against real cases; agree accuracy thresholds.
5. **Pilot in approval mode:** humans approve outputs; measure time saved and errors.
6. **Go live with monitoring and alerts.**
7. **Handover:** documentation, training, credentials, support plan.
8. **Review after 30–60 days:** results vs baseline; decide what's next.

## Agency, freelancer or in-house hire?

- **In-house** suits continuous, large-scale needs and organisations ready to invest in a team.
- **Agencies** bring breadth and capacity but add overhead and may assign juniors.
- **A specialist freelancer or small studio** is often the best value for a handful of well-defined projects, with direct access to the person building.
- **A hybrid** is common: an external partner builds the first automations and trains internal staff, who then maintain and extend them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does an AI automation engineer do?
They design and build automated workflows and AI-powered systems: integrating APIs, building agents and document pipelines, designing prompts and tools, handling errors, testing accuracy and monitoring the result in production.

### Agency, freelancer or in-house hire?
In-house suits continuous, large-scale needs. Agencies bring breadth but add overhead. A specialist freelancer or small studio is often the best value for a handful of well-defined projects. Many organisations combine an external builder with internal ownership.

### How much should I budget for a first automation project?
Scope drives cost, not buzzwords. A single well-defined workflow is a small project; multi-system AI agents with governance are larger. Ask for a fixed-scope pilot with a clear success metric, and budget for running costs such as subscriptions and AI usage.

### What are red flags when hiring an AI automation agency?
Guaranteed dramatic results without understanding your operation, tool-first pitches, automations built in the agency's own accounts, no testing on real data, no human review of AI outputs, vague pricing and no handover plan.

### Who should own the automations an agency builds?
You should. Automations, code, prompts and documentation should be owned by (or perpetually licensed to) your organisation and run in your own accounts, with credentials you control.

### How do I know if an automation project succeeded?
Compare results with the baseline you measured before: time saved, error rates, cycle times, response times, revenue or cost impact, and whether the team actually uses it, after at least 30 to 60 days in production.
