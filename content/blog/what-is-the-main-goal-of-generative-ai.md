<p class="lede">The <strong>main goal of generative AI is to create new content</strong> (text, images, code, audio, video, 3D models, designs and data) that is <strong>original yet realistic and useful</strong>, by learning the patterns and structure of existing data and generating new outputs that follow those patterns in response to a prompt. Where traditional AI mostly <strong>analyses and predicts</strong> (is this email spam? what will sales be?), generative AI <strong>produces</strong>: it writes the email, draws the image or drafts the code.</p>

## What does generative AI aim to do?

- **Generate** new content on demand from natural-language prompts
- **Augment human creativity and productivity:** drafts, ideas, variations and prototypes in seconds
- **Make knowledge accessible:** answer questions conversationally, summarise and explain
- **Automate content-heavy work:** documentation, reports, customer replies, code
- **Personalise at scale:** tailored messages, learning materials and recommendations
- **Transform content:** translate, summarise, restyle, convert formats

## How generative AI works (simply)

1. **Training on huge datasets:** text, images, code, audio or video.
2. **Learning patterns:** the model learns statistical relationships, such as which words follow others or how pixels form objects.
3. **Generation:** given a prompt, it produces new output step by step:
   - **Large language models (LLMs)** predict the next token (piece of text) repeatedly
   - **Diffusion models** start from noise and gradually refine it into an image or video
4. **Fine-tuning and alignment:** additional training (e.g., human feedback) makes outputs more helpful and safe.

(Deeper: [What is a neural network?](/blog/what-is-a-neural-network/), [What is machine learning?](/blog/what-is-machine-learning/).)

## What makes generative AI unique?

| Traditional (discriminative/predictive) AI | Generative AI |
|---|---|
| Classifies or predicts | Creates new content |
| Output: a label, score or number | Output: text, images, code, audio, video |
| Narrow task-specific models | General-purpose foundation models usable for many tasks |
| Usually needs structured data and data scientists | Used through natural-language prompts by anyone |
| Example: fraud detection | Example: drafting a customer reply |

Its uniqueness comes from **open-ended generation, versatility across tasks and natural-language interaction**. (Classification explained: [Classification in machine learning](/blog/classification-in-machine-learning/).)

## Which tasks are generative AI tasks?

Writing, summarising, translating, generating images or video, writing code, brainstorming ideas, creating synthetic data and answering open-ended questions. Pure prediction (forecasting numbers) or classification (spam vs not spam) are typically non-generative tasks, though generative models can perform them too. (More: [Which task is a generative AI task?](/blog/which-task-is-a-generative-ai-task/), [Appropriate tasks for generative AI](/blog/appropriate-tasks-for-generative-ai/).)

## Business uses of generative AI

- **Marketing:** ad copy, social posts, image and video variations (see [How to use AI in marketing](/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-marketing/))
- **Customer service:** AI chatbots and agent assist (see [How do AI chatbots work?](/blog/how-do-ai-chatbots-work/))
- **Sales:** personalised outreach, call summaries, proposals
- **Software:** coding assistants, documentation, testing
- **Operations:** document drafting, data extraction, report generation
- **HR:** job descriptions, onboarding content, policy Q&A
- **Legal and finance:** contract drafting and review support, summaries
- **Agents:** generative models that plan and take actions with tools (see [What is an AI agent?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent/))

## Limitations and risks

- **Hallucinations:** confident but false outputs
- **Bias** inherited from training data
- **Copyright and IP questions**
- **Privacy:** sensitive data entered into tools
- **Misuse:** deepfakes, misinformation, spam
- **Inconsistency:** different answers to the same prompt
- **Cost and energy** at scale

## Using generative AI responsibly

- Keep **humans in the loop** for important outputs
- **Verify facts** and sources
- Use **approved enterprise tools** for business data
- **Disclose** AI-generated content where required
- Respect **copyright and likeness** rights
- Set clear **policies** for staff

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the main goal of generative AI?
To create new, original content such as text, images, code, audio and video that resembles human-created content, based on patterns learned from training data.

### What makes generative AI unique?
Unlike traditional AI that classifies or predicts, generative AI creates new content, works across many tasks with one model and can be used through natural-language prompts.

### What is the purpose of generative AI in business?
To boost productivity and creativity by drafting content, writing code, answering questions, personalising communication and automating content-heavy tasks.

### How does generative AI create content?
It learns patterns from large datasets and generates new outputs, for example by predicting the next word (language models) or refining noise into images (diffusion models).

### Is ChatGPT generative AI?
Yes. ChatGPT is a generative AI chatbot powered by large language models that generate text (and, with other models, images and audio).

### What is the difference between AI and generative AI?
AI is the broad field of machines performing intelligent tasks. Generative AI is a subset focused on creating new content.
