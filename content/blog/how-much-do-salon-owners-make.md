<p class="lede">Salon owner income varies enormously. A <strong>solo stylist in a suite</strong> may earn similar to or more than an employed stylist, while owners of <strong>multi-chair salons</strong> can earn anywhere from a modest income to six figures or more, depending on <strong>number of chairs filled, pricing, staff pay model, retail sales and rent</strong>. Many owners still work behind the chair; profit margins for salons are often cited in the single digits to around the mid-teens percent, varying widely by management.</p>

## What determines salon owner income

| Factor | Impact |
|---|---|
| **Business model** | Booth rental gives steady rent income; commission salons earn more per service but carry payroll |
| **Chairs and utilisation** | Revenue grows with booked chairs |
| **Average ticket** | Pricing, add-ons (treatments, colour) |
| **Rebooking and retention** | Repeat clients stabilise revenue |
| **Retail sales** | Extra margin |
| **Rent and payroll** | Biggest costs |
| **Owner working in salon** | Adds service income |

## Booth rental vs commission salon

| | Booth rental | Commission |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner income** | Fixed rent from stylists | Share of service revenue |
| **Control** | Less (independent stylists) | More (brand, standards) |
| **Risk** | Lower | Higher (payroll) |
| **Upside** | Limited | Higher if busy |

## Calculate your potential income

1. **Revenue** = chairs × days open × clients per chair per day × average ticket (+ retail)
2. **Minus costs:** staff pay, products, rent, utilities, software, insurance, marketing
3. = **Profit** (plus owner's own service income if they work)

Example: 4 chairs × 25 days × 6 clients × $60 = $36,000/month revenue. Subtract commission, rent and other costs to estimate profit.

## How to make more money as a salon owner

1. **Raise prices** gradually with value
2. **Increase rebooking** at checkout
3. **Add high-margin services** (treatments, extensions, colour)
4. **Retail:** recommend take-home products
5. **Memberships and packages**
6. **Fill quiet hours** with targeted promotions
7. **Online booking and reminders** to reduce no-shows (see [Benefits of online booking](/blog/benefits-of-online-booking/))
8. **Grow clientele** (see [How to grow your salon business](/blog/how-to-grow-your-salon-business/))

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do salon owners make a year?
It varies widely, from modest incomes for small or new salons to six figures or more for successful multi-chair salons.

### How much does a hair salon owner make?
Depends on chairs, pricing, staff model, rent and whether the owner also works behind the chair.

### Is owning a salon profitable?
It can be, with good pricing, high chair utilisation, strong retention and controlled costs.

### What is a typical salon profit margin?
Often single digits to mid-teens percent, but it varies with management, rent and business model.

### Do booth rental salons make more money?
They offer steadier, lower-risk income; commission salons can earn more when busy but carry payroll risk.

### How do beauty salon owners increase income?
Higher prices, more rebookings, add-on services, retail sales, memberships and better marketing.
