<p class="lede">To get a job in data science in 2026: master <strong>SQL, Python and statistics</strong>, learn practical <strong>machine learning</strong> and how to work with <strong>AI tools and LLMs</strong>, build a <strong>portfolio of 3–5 projects that solve real business problems</strong> (not just Kaggle copies), target a <strong>specific domain and entry role</strong> (data analyst, junior data scientist, analytics engineer), and network your way to interviews. A degree in a quantitative field helps but isn't strictly required. Demonstrable skills and business impact matter most.</p>

The data science job market has changed. Entry-level competition is intense, AI tools now handle much of the routine coding, and employers increasingly want people who can turn data into decisions, not just build models. But demand remains strong: the US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects **data scientist employment to grow 34% from 2024 to 2034**, much faster than average, with a **median salary of about $112,590** (May 2024). This guide gives you a realistic, step-by-step path.

## Understand the roles

"Data science" covers several jobs. Choosing a target helps you focus.

| Role | What they do | Key skills |
|---|---|---|
| **Data analyst** | Answers business questions with data; dashboards and reports | SQL, Excel, BI tools (Power BI, Tableau), statistics, communication |
| **Data scientist** | Builds models and analyses to predict, explain and optimise | Python/R, statistics, ML, experimentation, SQL |
| **Machine learning engineer** | Deploys and maintains ML models in production | Software engineering, ML, MLOps, cloud |
| **Analytics engineer** | Builds clean, reliable data models for analysis | SQL, dbt, data warehousing, testing |
| **Data engineer** | Builds pipelines and data infrastructure | SQL, Python, Spark, cloud, orchestration |
| **AI engineer** | Builds applications with LLMs, agents and RAG | Python/TypeScript, APIs, evaluation (see [How to learn about AI](/blog/how-to-learn-ai/)) |
| **Product / decision scientist** | Experiments, metrics and product decisions | Statistics, A/B testing, SQL, product sense |

**Tip:** many people break into data science through a **data analyst** role, then move into data science after a year or two of experience.

## The skills you need

### 1. SQL (non-negotiable)
Every data role uses SQL daily. Learn joins, aggregations, window functions, CTEs, and how to write efficient, readable queries.

### 2. Python (or R)
- **pandas** for data manipulation, **NumPy**, **matplotlib/seaborn/plotly** for visualisation
- **scikit-learn** for machine learning; **XGBoost/LightGBM** for tabular modelling
- Jupyter notebooks, plus writing clean, reusable code (functions, Git)

### 3. Statistics and experimentation
Descriptive statistics, probability, distributions, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, regression, A/B testing and experimental design, and knowing when results are meaningful.

### 4. Machine learning
Supervised and unsupervised learning, feature engineering, model evaluation (precision, recall, AUC), avoiding leakage and overfitting, and explaining models to non-technical stakeholders. (See [What is machine learning?](/blog/what-is-machine-learning/) and [Classification in machine learning](/blog/classification-in-machine-learning/).)

### 5. Data visualisation and communication
Clear charts, dashboards (Power BI, Tableau, Looker) and, above all, **storytelling**: explaining what the data means and what to do about it. This is the skill that most separates hired candidates from rejected ones.

### 6. AI and LLM skills (new essential)
- Using AI coding assistants productively (and verifying their output)
- Using LLMs for text classification, extraction and analysis
- Basics of RAG, embeddings and evaluation
- Understanding where traditional ML beats LLMs and vice versa

### 7. Business and domain knowledge
Understanding how a business makes money, which metrics matter and how decisions are made. Domain expertise (finance, healthcare, marketing, supply chain, sports) makes you far more valuable.

### 8. Nice-to-haves
Cloud platforms (AWS, GCP, Azure), Spark, dbt, Docker, MLOps tools, and deep learning (PyTorch) for specialised roles.

## Do you need a degree to be a data scientist?

**Not necessarily, but it helps.** Many data scientists hold degrees in statistics, computer science, mathematics, economics, engineering, physics or other quantitative fields, and some employers (especially for research-heavy roles) prefer a master's or PhD. However:

- **Data analyst** roles are very accessible without a specialist degree.
- **Portfolios, certifications and experience** can substitute for formal credentials at many companies.
- **Career switchers** often succeed by combining domain expertise from a previous career with new data skills.

**What degree do you need to be a data scientist?** If you're choosing a degree: statistics, computer science, mathematics, data science or economics are the most direct. If you already have a degree in another field, a focused master's, bootcamp or self-study path plus a strong portfolio can work.

## Build a portfolio that gets interviews

Hiring managers see endless Titanic and Iris dataset projects. Stand out with **3–5 projects that solve realistic business problems**:

1. **Pick real questions:** "Which customers are likely to churn and why?", "What drives late deliveries?", "Which marketing channels produce profitable customers?"
2. **Use real or realistic data:** public datasets from governments, Kaggle (beyond the classics), APIs, web data (respecting terms), or your own domain.
3. **Show the full process:** question → data cleaning → analysis → model (if needed) → evaluation → **business recommendation**.
4. **Communicate:** a short write-up or blog post, clean notebooks, a dashboard or simple app (Streamlit), and a one-paragraph summary of impact.
5. **Include one AI project:** e.g., classifying customer feedback with an LLM and comparing it to a traditional model, or building a small RAG question-answering tool.
6. **Host on GitHub** with clear READMEs, and link from your LinkedIn and CV.

**Portfolio project ideas:**
- Customer churn prediction with explainable drivers and retention recommendations
- Sales forecasting for a retail dataset, with error analysis
- A/B test analysis with proper statistics and a decision memo
- Text analysis of product reviews using both classic NLP and an LLM
- A dashboard tracking public data in your target industry
- An automated data pipeline feeding a weekly report (see [What is software automation?](/blog/what-is-software-automation/))

## How to apply and get hired

1. **Target roles and companies** where your domain knowledge is an advantage.
2. **Tailor your CV** to each role: lead with projects and impact, use numbers ("reduced reporting time by 6 hours/week").
3. **Network:** connect with data professionals, attend meetups and online communities, ask for informational chats, share your project write-ups on LinkedIn.
4. **Look for internal moves:** if you're already employed, volunteer for data work in your current company. It's often the fastest route.
5. **Consider adjacent entry points:** data analyst, BI analyst, reporting analyst, operations analyst, research assistant.
6. **Freelance or volunteer:** nonprofits and small businesses often need data help, and it builds real experience.

## Interview preparation

Typical data science interviews include:
- **SQL tests:** practise on realistic business questions
- **Python/coding exercises:** data manipulation, not just algorithms
- **Statistics and probability questions**
- **Machine learning concepts:** bias-variance, evaluation metrics, feature engineering, leakage
- **Case studies / product sense:** "How would you measure the success of feature X?" or "Why did metric Y drop?"
- **Take-home assignments:** treat them like portfolio pieces, with clear communication
- **Behavioural questions:** stakeholder management, handling ambiguity, impact

## Salaries and job outlook

- **BLS (US):** data scientist median annual wage of about **$112,590** (May 2024), with employment projected to grow **34%** from 2024 to 2034.
- Salaries vary widely by location, industry, seniority and role (analyst roles typically pay less; ML engineering and AI roles typically pay more).
- **Reality check:** entry-level roles are competitive, and many postings ask for experience. Building practical experience (internal moves, freelance work, strong projects) is the way through.

## How AI is changing data science jobs

- **Routine coding is faster:** AI writes boilerplate pandas, SQL and plotting code. Expect higher productivity expectations.
- **Business impact matters more:** framing problems, choosing what to analyse and communicating decisions are harder to automate.
- **New work:** evaluating AI systems, building LLM-powered features, data for AI (quality, labelling, retrieval), and AI governance.
- **Skills to emphasise:** statistics and experimental rigour, domain knowledge, communication, critical evaluation of AI outputs, and AI engineering basics.

Data scientists who combine solid fundamentals with AI fluency and business sense are in a strong position. (Related: [Will AI replace software engineers?](/blog/will-ai-replace-software-engineers/) and [What is an AI developer?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-developer/))

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get a job in data science with no experience?
Learn SQL, Python and statistics, build three to five portfolio projects that solve realistic business problems and communicate clear recommendations, start with data analyst or adjacent roles, seek internal moves or freelance projects for experience, and network actively.

### What degree do you need to be a data scientist?
Degrees in statistics, computer science, mathematics, data science, economics, engineering or other quantitative fields are most common. Many employers also hire candidates without such degrees if they demonstrate strong skills through portfolios and experience.

### Is data science still a good career in 2026?
Yes. The US BLS projects 34% employment growth for data scientists from 2024 to 2034. Entry-level competition is high, and AI is changing the work, so combining fundamentals, AI fluency and business skills matters more than ever.

### How long does it take to become a data scientist?
With focused study, career switchers often land data analyst roles in six to twelve months and move into data scientist roles after gaining a year or two of experience. Timelines vary with background and effort.

### Will AI replace data scientists?
AI is automating routine coding and analysis tasks, but not problem framing, experimental rigour, domain judgement and communicating decisions. Data scientists who use AI effectively are likely to be more productive and in demand.

### Should I learn Python or R for data science?
Python is more widely used across industry, especially for machine learning, AI and production systems. R remains strong in statistics and academia. Most beginners should start with Python and SQL.
