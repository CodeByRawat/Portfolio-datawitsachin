<p class="lede">To market mental health services ethically: be clear about <strong>who you help and how</strong>, create a <strong>warm, reassuring website</strong> with <strong>easy online booking</strong>, maintain strong <strong>directory profiles</strong>, optimise <strong>Google Business Profile</strong>, publish <strong>helpful, stigma-reducing content</strong>, build <strong>referral relationships</strong> with doctors, schools and employers, and run <strong>workshops</strong>, always protecting <strong>client confidentiality</strong> and following professional guidelines.</p>

## 1. Clarify your specialties

Anxiety, depression, trauma, couples, children and teens, addiction, LGBTQ+ affirming care, specific modalities (CBT, EMDR), languages, insurance/self-pay, telehealth.

## 2. Website that feels safe

- Warm, plain-language copy speaking to the client's experience
- About/approach page with photo and credentials
- Specialty pages
- Fees, insurance and sliding scale info
- Online booking or a free consultation call (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/))
- Crisis resources and clear disclaimers

## 3. Directories

Psychology Today, insurer directories, local therapist networks and platform equivalents in your country; complete profiles with a personal, specific description.

## 4. Google Business Profile and local SEO

Accurate details, specialty keywords in descriptions, and pages targeting "[specialty] therapist in [city]" (see [What is SEO?](/blog/what-is-seo/)). Reviews may be restricted by ethics codes in some professions; follow your guidelines.

## 5. Helpful content

Articles, videos and newsletters on coping skills, what therapy is like, and how to choose a therapist. Avoid giving individual clinical advice publicly.

## 6. Referral relationships

GPs and psychiatrists, schools, universities, employers' EAPs, lawyers (family law), community organisations (see [How to increase referrals in private practice](/blog/how-to-increase-referrals-in-private-practice/)).

## 7. Workshops and community

Talks on stress, parenting, burnout; group programmes; partnerships with workplaces.

## 8. Social media (carefully)

Educational posts, myth-busting and practice updates; never discuss clients; keep professional boundaries in comments and DMs.

## Compliance

Confidentiality and health privacy laws, professional ethics codes on advertising and testimonials, secure booking and telehealth tools, and careful use of tracking technologies.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I market my therapy practice?
Define your specialties, build a warm website with online booking, maintain directory profiles, optimise Google, publish helpful content and build referral relationships while protecting confidentiality.

### What is the best way for therapists to get clients?
Directory profiles, referrals from doctors and community organisations, and a clear website with easy booking typically work best.

### Can therapists use testimonials?
Many professional codes restrict soliciting client testimonials; check your licensing board's rules.

### Should therapists be on social media?
It can help with education and visibility if you maintain professional boundaries and confidentiality.

### How do I market telehealth therapy?
Highlight convenience and privacy, specify where you're licensed to practise, and target searches for online therapy in those regions.

### How much do therapists spend on marketing?
Many start with low-cost directories and a website, then add paid ads based on cost per new client.
