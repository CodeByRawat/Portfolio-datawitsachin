<p class="lede"><strong>Google Docs doesn't have a built-in AI detector</strong> that labels text as AI-written (at the time of writing). But Google Docs can still reveal a lot: <strong>version history</strong> shows how a document was written over time, including large blocks of text pasted in at once, and teachers and employers often add <strong>browser extensions and AI detection tools</strong> (such as GPTZero, Turnitin, Copyleaks, Grammarly Authorship, Draftback or Brisk) that analyse either the text or the writing process. None of these methods is perfectly reliable.</p>

Students worry about being falsely accused; teachers want to know whether work is genuine; employers want to know how documents were produced. This guide explains what Google Docs can and can't show, the tools commonly used alongside it, how accurate AI detection is, and how to prove your work is your own.

## Does Google Docs have AI detection?

There's no "Check for AI" button in Google Docs. Google Docs includes **Gemini** features to *help* write (in eligible Google Workspace plans), but it doesn't score documents for AI authorship.

What Google Docs *does* provide is **version history**, which is often more revealing than any AI detector.

## How version history reveals AI use

**File › Version history › See version history** (or Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H) shows:
- **Timestamps** of edits
- **Who** made each change
- **What changed** between versions

What reviewers look for:
- **Large chunks of polished text appearing at once** (consistent with pasting from elsewhere)
- **Very short editing time** for a long document
- **Little revision:** no drafts, deletions or restructuring
- **Typical human patterns:** typing over time, rewriting sentences, moving paragraphs

**Important:** pasting isn't proof of AI. People paste from their own notes, other drafts, email or offline editors. Version history shows *how* text arrived, not *who or what* wrote it.

## Extensions and tools used with Google Docs

| Tool | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Draftback** (Chrome extension) | Replays the document's revision history like a video, showing how it was typed |
| **Revision History / Brisk and similar teacher tools** | Visualise writing time, paste events and editing sessions; some add AI detection |
| **GPTZero** (with Google Docs integration) | Analyses text for AI patterns and can review writing process |
| **Turnitin** | AI writing detection in submissions through learning platforms |
| **Copyleaks, Originality.ai, Winston AI** | Text-based AI and plagiarism detectors |
| **Grammarly Authorship** | Tracks how text was created (typed, pasted, AI-generated) as the writer works, producing a report the writer can share |

## What about SynthID?

**SynthID** is Google DeepMind's watermarking technology, embedding imperceptible signals in content generated by Google's AI models (including some Gemini text, images, audio and video). Google has offered tools to check for SynthID watermarks. Limitations:
- It only detects content from models that apply SynthID watermarks, not text from ChatGPT, Claude or others
- Heavy editing or paraphrasing can weaken text watermarks
- It isn't a general "is this AI?" detector inside Google Docs

## How accurate is AI detection?

Not accurate enough to be used as sole proof:
- **False positives:** human writing, especially formal, simple or non-native English writing, can be flagged as AI
- **False negatives:** AI text that's been edited or paraphrased often passes
- **Short texts** are particularly unreliable
- **Mixed writing** (human drafts edited with AI) is hard to classify
- Several universities have cautioned against relying on AI detector scores alone, and OpenAI withdrew its own AI text classifier in 2023 because of low accuracy

Responsible institutions use detectors as **one signal among many**, combined with conversations with the student, drafts and knowledge checks.

## Can Turnitin detect copy and paste from ChatGPT?

Turnitin's AI writing detection analyses the **submitted text**, not your clipboard, so it doesn't know if you copied and pasted. It estimates the likelihood that passages were AI-generated based on language patterns. Turnitin also states that its scores shouldn't be used as the only basis for action. Separately, Google Docs version history can show that text was pasted in large blocks.

## What teachers and employers actually check

1. **Version history** and writing timeline
2. **Consistency** with the person's previous writing style and level
3. **Knowledge:** can the writer explain or discuss their work?
4. **Factual errors or fabricated sources** (common in AI text)
5. **Generic phrasing** lacking personal insight or class-specific references
6. **Detector scores** as a supporting signal

## How to show your work is your own

- **Write in Google Docs** (or keep drafts) so version history shows your process
- **Keep notes, outlines and sources**
- **Use authorship tools** like Grammarly Authorship if your school accepts them
- **Follow your institution's AI policy;** disclose AI help where allowed (e.g., brainstorming or grammar checks)
- **Be ready to discuss** your work and explain your choices
- **If falsely accused,** calmly share version history, drafts and notes, and ask about the evidence used

## Using AI responsibly in Google Docs

Many schools and workplaces allow AI for some tasks. Typical acceptable uses: brainstorming, outlining, grammar suggestions, summarising your own notes. Typical unacceptable uses (unless allowed): submitting AI-generated text as your own original work. Check the rules first. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can Google Docs detect AI?
Google Docs has no built-in AI detector, but its version history shows how a document was written, including large pasted blocks, and teachers often use extensions or detectors like GPTZero, Turnitin or Draftback alongside it.

### Does Google Docs have AI detection built in?
No. Google Docs offers Gemini writing assistance in some plans, but not a tool that labels text as AI-generated.

### Can teachers see if you copy and paste in Google Docs?
Teachers with edit access can view version history, which shows when large amounts of text appeared at once. Extensions like Draftback can replay the writing process. They can't see your clipboard, but pasted blocks are usually visible in the history.

### Can Google detect ChatGPT text?
Google Docs can't identify ChatGPT text specifically. Google's SynthID watermarking applies to content from Google's own AI models, not ChatGPT. Third-party detectors estimate AI likelihood from writing patterns, with limited accuracy.

### Are AI detectors reliable?
Not fully. They produce false positives on human writing and miss edited AI text, so they should be one piece of evidence, not proof.

### How can I prove I didn't use AI?
Write in Google Docs or another tool with version history, keep notes and drafts, use authorship tracking tools if accepted, and be ready to explain your work and sources.
