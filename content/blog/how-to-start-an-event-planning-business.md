<p class="lede">To start an event planning business: choose a <strong>niche</strong> (weddings, corporate, social parties, conferences, festivals), build <strong>experience and a portfolio</strong>, <strong>register the business</strong> with <strong>insurance and solid contracts</strong>, define <strong>services and packages</strong> and a <strong>pricing model</strong>, build a reliable <strong>vendor network</strong>, set up <strong>tools</strong> for CRM, timelines and payments, launch a <strong>website and social presence</strong>, and win first clients through your network, venues and vendor referrals.</p>

## Step 1: Choose your niche

| Niche | Notes |
|---|---|
| Weddings | Emotional, detail-heavy, seasonal |
| Corporate events | Conferences, offsites, launches; bigger budgets |
| Social/party planning | Birthdays, showers, anniversaries (see [How to start a party planning business](/blog/how-to-start-a-party-planning-business/)) |
| Non-profit and fundraising | Galas, auctions |
| Destination events | Travel logistics |
| Virtual/hybrid events | Tech production |

## Step 2: Experience and portfolio

Assist other planners, volunteer, and plan styled shoots; collect photos and testimonials (see [How to become an event planner](/blog/how-to-become-an-event-planner/)).

## Step 3: Business setup

Registration, business bank account, **liability insurance**, contracts (scope, payment schedule, cancellation, force majeure, liability), and vendor agreements.

## Step 4: Services and packages

Full planning, partial planning, day-of coordination, design/styling, vendor sourcing, corporate event management.

## Step 5: Pricing

Flat fees, percentage of event budget, hourly for consultations, or hybrid (see [How much do event planners charge?](/blog/how-much-do-event-planners-charge/)).

## Step 6: Vendor network

Venues, caterers, photographers, florists, AV, rentals, entertainment, transport. Strong relationships bring referrals and better pricing.

## Step 7: Tools

CRM for inquiries, project management for timelines, budgeting spreadsheets, floor plan tools, e-signatures, invoicing and online booking for consultations (see [How to make a booking website](/blog/how-to-make-a-booking-website/)).

## Step 8: Website and marketing

Portfolio galleries, services and starting prices, testimonials, inquiry form; Instagram and Pinterest; Google Business Profile; venue partnerships; wedding/corporate directories.

## Step 9: Scale

Hire coordinators, standardise checklists, specialise for premium pricing.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start an event planning business?
Choose a niche, gain experience, register the business with insurance and contracts, define services and pricing, build vendor relationships, set up tools and a website, and market through your network and venues.

### How much does it cost to start an event planning business?
Often low: registration, insurance, a website, software and marketing; costs rise if you buy décor inventory or rent an office.

### Do I need a licence to be an event planner?
Usually just a business licence; certain activities (alcohol, large public events) may need permits.

### How do event planners get clients?
Referrals from venues and vendors, social media, websites, directories and past clients.

### What is the difference between event planning and event management?
They overlap; event management often refers to running larger or corporate events end to end, including operations.

### How do I start a party planning business?
Start with social events for your network, build a styled portfolio, offer packages and partner with local vendors.
