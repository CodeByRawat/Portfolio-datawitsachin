AI is already useful in legal work, and already risky if misused. Here's a practical view.

## Good uses for lawyers and law assistants

- **First drafts** of letters, emails, clauses and summaries.
- **Summarising** long documents, depositions or email threads.
- **Extracting** key dates, parties and obligations from contracts.
- **Research assistance:** exploring an issue before you go to authoritative sources.
- **Internal assistants** that answer questions from your firm's precedents and know-how.

## Legal research software and AI

Legal research platforms now include AI features for finding and summarising authorities. Treat output as a lead, not as authority: **check every citation against the source.** Courts have sanctioned lawyers who filed AI-generated cases that didn't exist.

## Confidentiality and governance

Before using any AI tool with client material, a firm should decide:

1. **Which tools are approved,** and under what terms (no training on your data, retention limits).
2. **What data may be entered,** and what must be anonymised.
3. **Who reviews AI output** and how that's recorded.
4. **How clients are informed** where required.
5. **Where data is processed,** relevant for jurisdictions with data-residency rules.

A written AI policy is now a basic part of firm risk management.

## Will lawyers be replaced by AI?

Tasks will be replaced; the profession is unlikely to be. Clients still need judgement, negotiation, advocacy and someone accountable. What changes is leverage: one lawyer with good tooling can do more, and clients will expect it to show in pricing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can lawyers use ChatGPT?
Many do, for drafting and summarising, but with care: don't paste confidential client information into tools without appropriate terms, and verify every citation and statement of law.

### Will lawyers be replaced by AI?
Routine drafting and document work will shrink, but judgement, advocacy, client trust and accountability remain human. Lawyers who use AI well are likely to outcompete those who don't.

### Can ChatGPT give legal advice?
It can explain general concepts, but it isn't a substitute for advice from a qualified lawyer. It can be wrong, out of date or unaware of your jurisdiction.
