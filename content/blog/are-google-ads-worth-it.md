<p class="lede"><strong>Google Ads are worth it when people actively search for what you sell</strong>, your <strong>profit per customer comfortably exceeds the cost to acquire one</strong>, and you send clicks to a <strong>fast, relevant landing page</strong> with tracking in place. They can bring customers within days. They're <strong>not worth it</strong> when there's little search demand, margins are thin, campaigns are poorly targeted (broad keywords, no negatives) or the website doesn't convert.</p>

## How Google Ads work (briefly)

- You choose keywords, audiences and a budget
- Google runs an **auction** each time someone searches
- **Ad Rank** depends on your bid, ad quality (Quality Score: expected CTR, ad relevance, landing page experience; see [Good landing page experience](/blog/good-landing-page-experience/)) and other factors
- You usually pay **per click (CPC)**

Campaign types include Search, Performance Max, Shopping, Display, YouTube and Demand Gen.

## How much do Google Ads cost?

You control the budget. Costs per click vary hugely: from under a dollar in low-competition niches to tens of dollars or more in competitive fields like legal, insurance and some home services. What matters is **cost per customer**, not cost per click.

## When Google Ads are worth it

- **High-intent searches exist** ("emergency plumber near me", "buy standing desk")
- **Good unit economics:** high customer value or lifetime value (see [What is LTV?](/blog/what-is-ltv/))
- **Strong landing pages** with fast loading and clear CTAs (see [What is a landing page conversion?](/blog/what-is-a-landing-page-conversion/))
- **Conversion tracking** is set up properly
- **You need customers fast** while SEO builds
- **Local service businesses** where a single job is valuable

## When they're often not worth it

- Little or no search demand for a new category (social/video ads may work better)
- Very low-margin products with high CPCs
- Sending traffic to a slow homepage
- No tracking (you can't optimise what you can't measure)
- Broad match without negatives, wasting spend on irrelevant searches
- Expecting profit in the first week without testing

## Calculate if Google Ads can be profitable

1. **Break-even CPA** = profit per customer (or LTV-based gross profit)
2. **Expected CPA** = CPC ÷ conversion rate × close rate (for leads)

Example: CPC $4, landing page conversion 8%, close rate 30% → cost per lead $50, cost per customer ~$167. If a customer is worth $600 in gross profit, Google Ads are likely worth it.

**ROAS** (e-commerce) = revenue ÷ ad spend. Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin (e.g., 40% margin → 2.5 ROAS).

## Setup checklist

- [ ] Clear goal and conversion actions (calls, forms, purchases)
- [ ] Google tag/GA4 conversions and enhanced conversions
- [ ] Tight keyword themes with phrase/exact match to start
- [ ] Negative keyword list (jobs, free, DIY, irrelevant)
- [ ] Location and schedule targeting
- [ ] Responsive search ads with strong headlines and assets (sitelinks, calls, callouts)
- [ ] Dedicated landing pages per service
- [ ] Budget enough to gather data (several clicks per day)
- [ ] Weekly search terms review

## Common mistakes

- Optimising for clicks instead of conversions
- Ignoring the search terms report
- Too many keywords per ad group
- Not using call tracking for phone-driven businesses
- Changing everything too often
- No follow-up system for leads (see [How to automate your sales process](/blog/how-to-automate-your-sales-process/))

## Alternatives and complements

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google Ads worth it?
Yes, when there's search demand, your profit per customer exceeds acquisition cost, and you use relevant landing pages with conversion tracking.

### Are Google Ads worth it for small businesses?
Often, especially local service businesses with high-value customers, provided campaigns are tightly targeted and tracked.

### How much should I spend on Google Ads?
Start with a budget that buys enough clicks to learn (often at least a few weeks of daily spend), then scale based on cost per customer.

### How long does it take for Google Ads to work?
Ads can generate clicks immediately, but expect a few weeks of testing and optimisation for stable performance.

### Why are my Google Ads not converting?
Common causes are irrelevant search terms, weak landing pages, mismatched messaging, poor offers and missing tracking.

### Is SEO better than Google Ads?
They serve different roles: Google Ads bring immediate traffic you pay for; SEO builds free, compounding traffic over time. Many businesses use both.
