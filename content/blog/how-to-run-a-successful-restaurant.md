<p class="lede">A successful restaurant combines a <strong>clear concept for a defined audience</strong>, <strong>consistent food and service</strong>, <strong>tight cost control</strong> (especially food and labour, together called <strong>prime cost</strong>), <strong>smart menu pricing</strong>, and <strong>steady marketing</strong> that keeps new and repeat guests coming. Many restaurants struggle in the first years because of thin margins, under-capitalisation and weak cost control, so owners who <strong>track their numbers weekly</strong> and build systems have a big advantage.</p>

## What percentage of restaurants fail?

Restaurant failure statistics are often exaggerated. Research suggests a significant share of new restaurants close within the first few years (estimates vary by study, location and how "failure" is defined), but the commonly quoted "90% fail in the first year" is not supported by most data. The early years are the riskiest.

## Why restaurants fail

- **Under-capitalisation:** not enough cash to survive the ramp-up
- **High prime costs** (food + labour) and poor cost tracking
- **Wrong location or rent too high**
- **Unclear concept** or weak differentiation
- **Inconsistent food and service**
- **Poor marketing** and visibility
- **Owner burnout** and lack of systems

## Are restaurants profitable?

They can be, but margins are thin. Typical net profit margins for many full-service restaurants are often cited in the low single digits, with quick-service and well-run concepts sometimes higher. Profitability depends on rent, prime cost control, pricing, volume and efficiency.

## How to figure out food cost

**Food cost % = cost of ingredients used ÷ food sales × 100**

- **Plate cost:** sum ingredient costs for one dish (including garnish and waste)
- **Menu price** often targets a food cost % (commonly around 28–35%, varies by concept)
- **Period food cost:** (beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory) ÷ food sales

**Prime cost** = food + beverage + labour cost; many operators aim to keep it around 55–65% of sales.

## Systems of successful restaurants

1. **Standardised recipes** and portioning
2. **Inventory and ordering** routines
3. **Labour scheduling** based on sales forecasts
4. **Training and checklists** for every station
5. **Guest experience standards**
6. **Weekly P&L review**
7. **Marketing calendar** (see [How to increase restaurant sales](/blog/how-to-increase-restaurant-sales/))
8. **Technology:** POS, online ordering, reservations, inventory, review management

## Key numbers to track weekly

Sales and covers, average check, food cost %, labour cost %, prime cost %, table turnover, waste, online order share, review rating.

## What type of business is a restaurant?

A restaurant is a **food service/hospitality business** in the accommodation and food services sector. Legal structures vary (sole proprietorship, LLC/private limited company, partnership), and restaurants need food safety licences and permits.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you run a successful restaurant?
Have a clear concept, consistent food and service, strict control of food and labour costs, smart pricing, strong marketing and weekly tracking of key numbers.

### What percentage of restaurants fail?
Estimates vary; many new restaurants close within a few years, but the popular claim that 90% fail in year one isn't supported by most research.

### Why do restaurants fail?
Under-capitalisation, high costs, poor location, unclear concept, inconsistency, weak marketing and lack of systems.

### Are restaurants profitable?
They can be, though margins are often thin; cost control and volume determine profitability.

### How do you calculate food cost in a restaurant?
Divide the cost of ingredients used by food sales and multiply by 100, using inventory counts or plate costing for individual dishes.

### What is prime cost?
The combined cost of food, beverages and labour, the largest controllable expenses in a restaurant.
