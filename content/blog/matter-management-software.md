<p class="lede">Every law firm and in-house legal team runs on matters. <strong>Matter management software</strong> (called <strong>legal case management software</strong> in litigation) is the central record of each matter: what it is, who's involved, what's due, which documents and communications belong to it, what it's costing, and how it ends. Done well, it means anyone can pick up a file, deadlines aren't missed, and partners can see workload and profitability at a glance.</p>

This guide explains the core features, what litigation teams need, how in-house legal departments use matter management differently, legal project management, where AI helps (and where to be careful), costs, popular tools, and when a custom matter workflow beats a generic platform.

## Core features

- **Matter intake and opening:** standard fields by matter type, conflict check, engagement terms, budget and team assignment
- **Parties and contacts** linked to each matter (clients, opposing parties, counsel, experts, courts, witnesses)
- **Tasks and deadlines** with automatic reminders, dependencies and rule-based date calculation
- **Matter templates and checklists** so every matter of a given type starts with the right steps
- **Documents and email** filed against the matter, with version control
- **Notes and activity timeline** so anyone can see what happened and what's next
- **Budgets, time and costs** with alerts before a matter overruns
- **Status and phase tracking**
- **Client portal** for updates and secure document exchange
- **Reporting:** workload, ageing, profitability, outcomes, cycle times

## Litigation-specific needs

Litigation adds requirements that general tools often handle poorly:

- **Court calendaring with rule sets:** automatically calculating deadlines from trigger events according to court rules (US firms often use rules-based calendaring services such as LawToolBox, CompuLaw or CalendarRules integrations)
- **Pleadings, bundles and court documents**
- **Disclosure / e-discovery tracking**, often integrated with review platforms (see [Document review software](/blog/legal-document-review-software/))
- **Hearing and trial preparation:** witnesses, exhibits, chronologies
- **Settlement and outcome tracking**
- **Costs budgeting** (and, in England and Wales, costs management requirements)

Check that the tool supports the courts and procedures you actually use.

## Matter management for in-house legal teams

Corporate legal departments use matter management (often called **enterprise legal management, ELM**) differently:

- **Work intake** from the business (contract requests, advice requests, disputes)
- **Outside counsel management:** engagement, budgets, e-billing and invoice review against billing guidelines
- **Spend analytics** by matter type, business unit and law firm
- **Litigation portfolio and risk reporting** for management and audit
- **Contract and entity management integrations**

Popular in-house tools include **Brightflag**, **Onit**, **Mitratech (TeamConnect)**, **SimpleLegal**, **Legal Tracker (Thomson Reuters)** and **LawVu**. (See [Legal workflow automation](/blog/legal-workflow-automation/).)

## Legal project management

Predictable, profitable delivery, especially for fixed-fee work, needs **legal project management (LPM)**:

- **Define scope** and assumptions clearly at the start
- **Break the matter into phases and tasks** with owners
- **Budget hours and costs by phase**, then track actuals against plan
- **Staff appropriately:** the right work at the right level
- **Manage changes in scope** with the client (and adjust fees where agreed)
- **Track risks and issues** (see [What is a RAID log?](/blog/what-is-a-raid-log/))
- **Review profitability** at matter close and feed lessons into future pricing

Look for **matter templates by type**, so every new conveyance, claim or immigration application starts with the right checklist and budget.

## Where AI helps (and where to be careful)

AI case management features can:

- **Summarise** documents, correspondence and the matter history
- **Draft chronologies** from documents
- **Extract key dates and deadlines** from correspondence and court orders
- **Suggest next steps** based on matter type and stage
- **Answer questions** about a matter in natural language ("What did the other side offer in March?")
- **Draft client updates** and internal reports

These save significant time but must be **reviewed by a lawyer**, especially extracted deadlines and anything sent to a client or court. Ask any vendor:
- Is client data used to train models?
- Where is it processed and stored?
- Are AI outputs logged and attributable?
- Can AI be disabled for specific matters (e.g., highly sensitive ones)?

(Ethics and confidentiality: [AI for lawyers](/blog/ai-for-lawyers/).)

## Cloud-based or self-hosted

Cloud-based matter management is the default for most firms: lower upkeep, automatic updates and access from anywhere. If your clients or regulators restrict data location, confirm hosting regions or consider a self-hosted or custom option. Either way, insist on encryption, access controls, ethical walls, audit trails and tested backups.

## Costs

- **Small and mid-sized law firms:** matter management is usually part of a practice management subscription priced per user per month (see [Law practice management software](/blog/law-practice-management-software/)).
- **Litigation-focused platforms:** per-user pricing, often with add-ons for e-discovery or advanced workflow.
- **In-house ELM:** priced per user or by legal spend under management; implementation can be significant.
- **Hidden costs:** data migration, configuration of templates and workflows, integrations and training.

## Custom matter workflows

If your work follows a **repeatable, high-volume process** (claims, immigration, debt recovery, conveyancing, employment tribunal claims, insurer panel work), a custom matter workflow can outperform a generic tool:

- **Automated stages and tasks** that move matters forward based on events
- **Document generation** at each stage (see [Legal document automation](/blog/legal-document-automation/))
- **Integration with portals** (insurers, courts, government systems)
- **Automatic client status updates** by SMS or email
- **Dashboards** showing every matter's stage, bottlenecks and SLA compliance

In regulated high-volume areas, AI-driven firms are emerging. For example, the SRA authorised the AI-driven firm Garfield.Law for small debt recovery in England and Wales in 2025, with human solicitors remaining accountable.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is matter management?
Matter management is tracking each legal matter from opening to closure (parties, documents, tasks, deadlines, communications, costs and outcomes) in one place.

### What's the difference between case management and matter management?
Case management is the term most used for litigation and individual cases. Matter management is broader, covering any piece of legal work, including transactional and advisory matters, and is common in in-house legal teams.

### What is legal project management?
Applying project management methods (scope, phases, budget, staffing, change control and risk management) to legal matters so they're delivered predictably and profitably, especially under fixed or capped fees.

### What is enterprise legal management (ELM)?
Software used by in-house legal departments to manage matters, outside counsel, legal spend and e-billing, and reporting across the legal function.

### Can AI manage legal cases?
AI can summarise documents, extract dates, draft chronologies and updates, and suggest next steps, but lawyers must review outputs and remain responsible for the matter, deadlines and advice.

### What is the best case management software for litigation?
Options such as Filevine, Litify and CASEpeer are popular with litigation and personal injury firms, while general practice management tools such as Clio and MyCase suit firms with mixed practices. Rules-based court calendaring support is a key selection criterion.
