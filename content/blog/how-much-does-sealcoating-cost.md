<p class="lede">Professional sealcoating often costs around <strong>$0.15 to $0.35 per square foot</strong> for driveways (with small-job minimums), so a typical residential driveway is often <strong>$200 to $600</strong>. Commercial parking lots usually cost <strong>$0.12 to $0.25 per square foot</strong>, plus crack filling and line striping.</p>

These are rough ranges; prices vary by region and condition.

## Average cost by job

| Job | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Small driveway (under 600 sq ft) | Often a minimum, around $200 – $300 |
| Average driveway (600–1,200 sq ft) | Around $250 – $500 |
| Large driveway | $500+ |
| Commercial lot | Per sq ft, plus striping |

## What affects the price

- Size of the area
- Number of cracks (crack filling extra, often per linear foot)
- Oil stains needing primer
- One or two coats
- Sealer type and additives
- Access and preparation
- Local labour costs

## Add-ons

| Service | Typical pricing |
|---|---|
| Crack filling | Per linear foot or per pound |
| Oil spot priming | Per spot or included |
| Line striping | Per stall or per line |
| Patching potholes | Per square foot |

## Sealcoat cost per gallon

Bulk sealer is relatively cheap per gallon; most of what customers pay covers labour, prep, equipment and profit.

## How often should you sealcoat?

Often every 2 to 3 years for driveways, depending on traffic and weather.

## DIY vs professional

DIY kits are cheaper but often thinner and less durable. Professionals clean, crack fill and apply commercial-grade sealer evenly.
