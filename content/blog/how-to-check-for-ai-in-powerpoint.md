<p class="lede">There's <strong>no reliable one-click tool that proves a PowerPoint was made with AI</strong>. Reviewers instead combine clues: <strong>file metadata and version history</strong> (in OneDrive/SharePoint or Google Slides), <strong>design patterns</strong> typical of AI slide generators, and <strong>text analysis</strong> by copying slide text into AI detectors such as GPTZero, Turnitin, Copyleaks or Originality.ai. Some AI images may carry <strong>content credentials or watermarks</strong>. All of these signals are imperfect, and none is proof on its own.</p>

## Ways people check for AI in presentations

### 1. Version history and metadata
- **PowerPoint in OneDrive/SharePoint:** File › Info › Version history shows when versions were saved
- **Google Slides:** File › Version history shows edits over time (like Google Docs; see [Can Google Docs detect AI?](/blog/can-google-docs-detect-ai/))
- **File properties:** author, created/modified times, total editing time (can be inaccurate or altered)

A full deck appearing in one save with very little editing time can be a signal, but people also build slides offline or paste from drafts.

### 2. Text detectors
Copy slide text (and speaker notes) into an AI text detector. Limitations: slide text is short and fragmented, which makes detection **even less reliable** than for essays.

### 3. Design and content patterns
Reviewers may notice:
- Generic structure ("Introduction, Key Benefits, Challenges, Conclusion")
- Uniform bullet lengths and polished but vague phrasing
- Stock-like AI images with odd details (hands, text, logos)
- Template styles typical of AI slide tools
- Facts or citations that don't check out

### 4. Image provenance
Some AI image tools add **C2PA content credentials** or invisible watermarks (e.g., Google's SynthID). These can be checked with specific tools, but metadata is often stripped when images are copied.

### 5. Asking the presenter
The most reliable check: ask the person to **explain their slides**, sources and design choices.

## AI tools that create PowerPoints

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot** in PowerPoint (create from prompt or document)
- **PowerPoint Designer** (layout suggestions, not content)
- **ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini** generating outlines, content or files (see [Can ChatGPT make PowerPoints?](/blog/can-chatgpt-make-powerpoints/))
- **Dedicated AI slide tools** such as Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Tome-style tools, Canva Magic Design

Using AI for **layout and design** is very different from generating all the ideas and content; policies often distinguish these.

## Accuracy and fairness

- Detectors produce **false positives** (especially on short, formal or non-native English text) and **false negatives** (edited AI text)
- Short slide text is particularly unreliable
- Decisions should rely on **multiple signals and conversation**, not a detector score

## How to show your presentation is your own

- Keep **drafts and version history** (save to OneDrive/Google Drive as you work)
- Keep **notes, sources and outlines**
- **Disclose AI use** where allowed (e.g., "Layout suggestions by Designer; images generated with…")
- Be ready to **present and answer questions** confidently

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a way to check for AI on PowerPoints?
Not definitively. Reviewers use version history, metadata, design and writing patterns, AI text detectors and conversations with the creator, all of which have limits.

### Can Turnitin detect AI in PowerPoint?
Turnitin analyses submitted text; slide text can be checked if submitted in a supported format, but short slide text makes detection less reliable.

### Can teachers tell if you used AI for slides?
They may notice patterns, check version history or ask you to explain your work, but there's no foolproof detection.

### Does Copilot leave a watermark on PowerPoint?
Copilot-generated slide text typically has no visible watermark; some AI-generated images may include content credentials metadata.

### Is using PowerPoint Designer considered AI cheating?
Usually not; Designer suggests layouts. Check your institution's or employer's policy, which typically focuses on AI-generated content and ideas.

### How can I prove I made my presentation?
Keep version history, drafts, notes and sources, disclose any permitted AI help, and be ready to explain your content.
