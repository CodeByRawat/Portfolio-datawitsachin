<p class="lede">Making an app can be <strong>easy or very hard depending on what it does and how you build it</strong>. A simple app (a form, checklist, directory or booking tool) can be built in days with <strong>no-code or AI app builders</strong>. Apps with <strong>user accounts, payments, real-time features, integrations, offline sync or high security</strong> are much harder and usually need developers. Most of the difficulty isn't the first version; it's <strong>backend reliability, edge cases, app store requirements, maintenance and getting users</strong>.</p>

## Difficulty by app type

| App type | Examples | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple content/utility** | Calculator, checklist, info app | Easy |
| **Forms and directories** | Event app, catalogue, internal forms | Easy–moderate |
| **Booking and scheduling** | Salon or clinic booking | Moderate |
| **E-commerce** | Store with payments | Moderate–hard |
| **Social/marketplace** | Two-sided platforms, chat, feeds | Hard |
| **Real-time/multiplayer** | Live tracking, games | Hard |
| **AI-powered agents** | Assistants taking actions | Moderate–hard |
| **Regulated** | Health, finance | Hard (compliance) |

## Difficulty by approach

| Approach | Learning curve | Flexibility |
|---|---|---|
| **No-code** (Glide, Adalo, Softr) | Low | Limited |
| **Low-code** (FlutterFlow, Bubble) | Medium | Good |
| **AI app builders + AI coding tools** | Low to start, medium to maintain | Good with review |
| **Cross-platform code** (React Native, Flutter) | Medium–high | High |
| **Native** (Swift, Kotlin) | High | Highest |

## Skills needed

- **Product thinking:** what to build and why
- **UX/UI design**
- **Frontend development** (screens, interactions)
- **Backend** (accounts, databases, APIs)
- **Testing** on devices
- **Security and privacy**
- **App store publishing** (see [How to publish an app on the App Store](/blog/how-to-publish-an-app-on-the-app-store/))
- **Marketing and analytics**

## How long does it take to learn to make apps?

- No-code basics: days to weeks
- Building real apps with code: several months of consistent practice
- Professional proficiency: one to two years

## The hardest parts of making an app

1. **Scope creep:** trying to build everything at once
2. **Backend and data:** sync, permissions, reliability
3. **Edge cases:** poor connections, different devices, errors
4. **App store review** and policies
5. **Maintenance:** OS updates, bugs, security patches
6. **Getting and keeping users**

## How to make building an app easier

- Start with a **tiny MVP** (see [How to make an app](/blog/how-to-make-an-app/))
- Prototype in **Figma** first
- Use **backend-as-a-service** (Firebase, Supabase)
- Use **cross-platform** frameworks
- Use **AI coding tools** for boilerplate, with review
- Consider a **web app** first (see [How to build a web app](/blog/how-to-build-a-web-app/))
- **Hire help** for the hard parts (backend, security, publishing)
- Design cheaply from scratch (see [How to design an app from scratch with the least cost](/blog/how-to-design-an-app-from-scratch/))

## Frequently asked questions

### How hard is it to make an app?
Simple apps are easy with no-code tools; apps with accounts, payments, real-time features or integrations are significantly harder and usually need developers.

### How difficult is it to create an app with no experience?
Feasible for simple apps with no-code or AI builders; complex apps require learning programming or hiring developers.

### Is it easy to make an app?
Making a basic prototype is easier than ever; making a reliable, successful app that users keep using is still challenging.

### How long does it take to make an app?
From days for simple no-code apps to several months for a professional MVP and longer for complex products.

### What is the hardest part of app development?
Often the backend and data handling, edge cases, maintenance and user acquisition rather than the first screens.

### Can AI make an app for me?
AI app builders and coding assistants can generate much of an app, but you still need to define requirements, review code, test and handle publishing.
