<p class="lede">To make an advertisement: <strong>define one goal</strong> (sales, leads, sign-ups or awareness), <strong>know exactly who it's for</strong> and what problem they have, <strong>write a hook and a single clear message</strong> with a call to action, <strong>pick the format</strong> (short video, motion graphics, static image or carousel) for the platform, <strong>design and produce it</strong> in the right sizes, then <strong>launch, test variations and keep what performs</strong>. Great ads are rarely made in one attempt; they come from testing several creative ideas.</p>

Whether you're a small business making your first Facebook ad or a marketer planning a campaign, the process below works across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn, Google and offline formats.

## Step 1: Set one goal

| Goal | What the ad must do | Typical call to action |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Be memorable and branded | Follow, watch, learn more |
| **Traffic / consideration** | Spark curiosity | Learn more, see how it works |
| **Leads** | Offer something valuable in exchange for contact details | Get the guide, book a call |
| **Sales** | Remove doubts and give a reason to buy now | Shop now, get 20% off |
| **App installs** | Show the app's main benefit fast | Download |

An ad that tries to do everything does nothing well.

## Step 2: Know the audience deeply

Write down:
- **Who** they are (role, life stage, location)
- **The problem** they feel, in their own words (read reviews, comments, sales calls and support tickets)
- **What they've already tried** and why it disappointed
- **Objections:** price, trust, time, complexity
- **What would make them act today**

The best ad copy often comes directly from customers' language.

## Step 3: Craft the message

### The hook (first 1–3 seconds or first line)
The hook decides whether anyone sees the rest. Proven hook types:
- **Call out the audience:** "Running a dental clinic?"
- **Name the pain:** "Still copying leads into a spreadsheet?"
- **Surprising result:** "We cut onboarding from 3 weeks to 2 days."
- **Before/after or demo:** show the transformation immediately
- **Question or bold claim** (that you can back up)

### The body
One core benefit, supported by proof: a demo, a number, a testimonial, a guarantee. Use simple frameworks:
- **Problem → Agitate → Solution (PAS)**
- **Before → After → Bridge**
- **Hook → Demo → Proof → CTA** (great for product videos)

### The call to action
Specific and low-friction: "Book a free 15-minute call", not "Contact us".

## Step 4: Choose the format

| Format | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Short video (UGC/creator style)** | Social ads, trust, demos | Feels native in feeds |
| **Motion graphics ad** | Explaining features, offers, SaaS and apps | Brand-controlled, easy to update (see [What are motion graphics?](/blog/what-are-motion-graphics/)) |
| **Promo / product video** | Launches, websites, YouTube | Higher production value |
| **Static image** | Simple offers, retargeting, fast testing | Cheap to produce many variations (see [What is a static ad?](/blog/what-is-a-static-ad/)) |
| **Carousel** | Multiple products, step-by-step stories | Encourages swiping |
| **Search text ads** | High-intent searches | Headlines and descriptions only |
| **Audio** | Podcasts, streaming | Script and voice matter most |

## Step 5: Design and produce

- **Lead with the product or result,** not your logo
- **Big, readable text** (most people watch muted; add captions)
- **One message per frame or slide**
- **Brand consistency:** colours, fonts, tone
- **Movement in the first second** for video
- **Real product footage or UI** builds more trust than generic stock
- Keep text clear of platform **safe zones** (top and bottom UI on Reels/TikTok)

For video, write a script and storyboard before producing. (Briefing a video: [How to write a video brief for a product demo](/blog/how-to-write-a-video-brief-for-a-product-demo/).)

## Step 6: Size it for each platform

| Placement | Ratio | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Reels, Stories, TikTok, Shorts | 9:16 | 1080×1920 |
| Instagram/Facebook feed | 4:5 or 1:1 | 1080×1350 / 1080×1080 |
| YouTube in-stream, website | 16:9 | 1920×1080 |
| LinkedIn feed | 1:1 or 4:5 | 1080×1080 / 1080×1350 |
| Display banners | Various | e.g., 300×250, 728×90, 160×600 |

## Step 7: Create an advertising campaign

1. **Choose the platform** where your audience spends time and buys.
2. **Set up the campaign objective** in the ad manager (awareness, traffic, leads, sales).
3. **Define targeting:** location, interests, lookalikes, retargeting lists, or broad targeting with strong creative.
4. **Set the budget and schedule;** start small enough to test.
5. **Upload several creative variations** (different hooks, formats, angles).
6. **Set up tracking:** pixel/conversion API, UTM links, a landing page that matches the ad.
7. **Launch and let it learn** before making big changes.

## Step 8: Test and improve

Creative is usually the biggest lever in modern ad platforms. Test:
- **Hooks** (keep the rest identical)
- **Angles:** price, speed, quality, social proof, fear of missing out
- **Formats:** UGC vs motion graphics vs static
- **Offers and CTAs**

Platforms can also automatically mix and match assets (see [What is dynamic creative optimization?](/blog/what-is-dynamic-creative-optimization/)).

## Step 9: Measure what matters

| Metric | Tells you |
|---|---|
| **Hook rate / thumb-stop rate** (3-second views ÷ impressions) | Is the opening grabbing attention? |
| **Hold rate / average watch time** | Is the story keeping people? |
| **CTR** | Does the ad make people want more? |
| **CPC / CPM** | Cost efficiency |
| **Conversion rate** | Does the landing page deliver on the ad's promise? |
| **CPA and ROAS** | Is it profitable? |

## Common advertising mistakes

- No clear goal or audience
- Slow intro or logo first
- Too many messages in one ad
- Tiny text, no captions
- Mismatch between ad and landing page
- Running one creative and judging the whole channel on it
- Changing everything daily before the platform learns
- Making polished ads that look like ads, when native-looking content performs better on social

## Using AI to make ads

AI can speed up research, scripts, headlines, image backgrounds, voiceovers and variations. Use it to generate more ideas to test, but review for brand accuracy, claims and platform policies. (More: [How to use AI in marketing](/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-marketing/).)

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I make an advertisement?
Set a single goal, define your audience and their problem, write a strong hook and one clear message with a call to action, choose a format, design it in the right sizes for each platform, then launch, test variations and measure results.

### How do I start an advertisement for my small business?
Start with one platform where your customers are, one offer, and three to five creative variations on a small budget. Track results with a pixel and UTM links, then put more budget behind what works.

### What makes a good advertisement?
A strong hook, one clear benefit, proof, an easy call to action, and a format that looks natural on the platform where it runs.

### How long should a video ad be?
For social feeds, 6–30 seconds usually works best, with the key message in the first three seconds. Longer ads work for explainers and retargeting warm audiences.

### How do I create an advertising campaign?
Choose your objective and platform, define targeting and budget, upload multiple creatives, set up tracking, launch, and optimise based on cost per result.

### Should I make ads myself or hire someone?
DIY tools work for simple tests. Hiring a creative producer makes sense when you need polished video or motion ads, many consistent variations, or faster results.
