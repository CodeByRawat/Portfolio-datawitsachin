<p class="lede">A <strong>local citation</strong> is any online mention of your business's <strong>name, address and phone number (NAP)</strong>, often with your website, on directories, map platforms, social networks, review sites and other websites. Consistent citations help search engines <strong>confirm your business is real and where it's located</strong>, which supports rankings in local search and Google Maps, and they help customers find accurate information.</p>

## Types of local citations

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Structured citations** | Business listings in directory format | Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, Yellow Pages |
| **Unstructured citations** | Mentions within articles or web pages | Local news articles, blog posts, event pages |
| **Industry-specific** | Directories for your profession | Healthgrades/Zocdoc (medical), Avvo (legal), Houzz (home services), TripAdvisor (hospitality) |
| **Local/regional** | City and community directories | Chamber of commerce, local business associations |
| **Data aggregators** | Companies distributing business data to many sites | Data providers like Data Axle, Foursquare, Neustar Localeze (varies by country) |

## Why local citations matter

- **Trust signals:** consistent information across the web helps verify your business
- **Local ranking support:** citations contribute to prominence, especially on authoritative and relevant sites
- **Discovery:** customers find you on platforms they already use (Apple Maps, Yelp, industry sites)
- **Referral traffic and leads**
- **Accuracy:** prevents customers calling old numbers or visiting old addresses

Citations are a foundation rather than a major ranking lever on their own; reviews, relevance, proximity and your Google Business Profile matter a lot too (see [How to create a Google Business page](/blog/how-to-create-a-google-business-page/)).

## What is NAP consistency?

NAP = **Name, Address, Phone**. Keep them identical everywhere:
- Same business name (no keyword additions)
- Same address format (e.g., "Suite 200" vs "#200": pick one)
- Same primary phone number (local number preferred)
- Same website URL

Inconsistent or duplicate listings can confuse search engines and customers.

## The most important citation sites (start here)

1. Google Business Profile
2. Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps)
3. Bing Places for Business
4. Facebook business page
5. Yelp
6. Industry-specific directories for your field
7. Chamber of commerce and local associations
8. Better Business Bureau (US) or local equivalents
9. Major country-specific directories (e.g., Yell in UK, Justdial in India)
10. LinkedIn company page and other social profiles

## How to build local citations

1. **Create a master NAP document:** exact name, address, phone, website, hours, categories, description, logo and photos
2. **Claim the core platforms** above
3. **Add industry and local directories** relevant to you
4. **Use consistent categories and descriptions**
5. **Add photos and complete every field**
6. **Track listings** in a spreadsheet with logins
7. Consider **citation management tools** for scale (BrightLocal, Yext, Moz Local, Semrush Listing Management, Whitespark)

## How to clean up citations

- Search for your business name and old addresses/phone numbers
- Use citation audit tools to find inconsistencies and duplicates
- Update or claim incorrect listings
- Request removal of duplicates
- Pay special attention after moving or rebranding

## Mistakes to avoid

- Keyword-stuffed business names
- Using different phone numbers (tracking numbers inconsistently)
- Mass-submitting to hundreds of low-quality directories
- Forgetting to update listings after a move
- Leaving listings unclaimed for others to edit

## Frequently asked questions

### What are local citations?
Online mentions of a business's name, address and phone number on directories, maps, social platforms and other websites, which help search engines and customers verify business information.

### Do local citations still matter for SEO?
Yes, as a foundation for trust and accuracy, though reviews, relevance, proximity and Google Business Profile optimisation have a bigger impact on local rankings.

### What is NAP in SEO?
NAP stands for Name, Address and Phone number, the core business information that should be consistent across all listings.

### How many citations do I need?
There's no set number. Focus on the major platforms and relevant industry and local directories rather than quantity.

### What is the difference between structured and unstructured citations?
Structured citations are formatted directory listings; unstructured citations are mentions of your business within articles, blogs or other content.

### Are paid citation services worth it?
They can save time for multi-location businesses or agencies, but small businesses can build core citations manually.
