<p class="lede">A shaved ice business often makes <strong>$300 to $1,500+ on a busy day</strong>, and a seasonal stand or truck can bring in <strong>$20,000 to $100,000+</strong> a year. Ingredient cost per cup is often <strong>under $1</strong> while cups sell for <strong>$3 to $8</strong>, so gross margins can reach <strong>70% to 85%</strong>. Startup costs range from about <strong>$2,000 to $80,000+</strong>.</p>

These are rough ranges; weather, locations and pricing matter most.

## Cost per cup (example)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Ice | $0.10 – $0.25 |
| Syrup | $0.20 – $0.50 |
| Cup, spoon, straw | $0.10 – $0.25 |
| **Total** | **about $0.40 – $1.00** |
| Price | $3 – $8 |

## Revenue examples

| Situation | Rough revenue |
|---|---|
| Slow day at a park | $100 – $300 |
| Busy summer weekend spot | $500 – $1,500 |
| Festival or big event | $1,000 – $5,000+ |
| Season total (stand) | $20,000 – $60,000 |
| Season total (truck with events) | $50,000 – $150,000 |

## Event rentals

Many shaved ice trucks charge a flat fee or per-guest rate for schools, churches and corporate events (similar to how Kona Ice-style trucks are rented), which gives reliable income regardless of walk-up traffic.

## Startup and ongoing costs

| Cost | Range |
|---|---|
| Setup (stand to truck) | $2,000 – $80,000+ |
| Machine | $300 – $3,000+ |
| Permits and insurance | $500 – $3,000 per year |
| Event and location fees | Varies |
| Staff | Hourly |

## How to make more

- Book private events and school fundraisers
- Add creamy toppings and premium flavours
- Choose busy, hot locations
- Sell merchandise and combos
- Extend the season with other treats
