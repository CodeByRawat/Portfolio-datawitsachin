<p class="lede">To become an event planner: build core <strong>organisation, communication, budgeting and vendor management</strong> skills, gain <strong>hands-on experience</strong> (volunteering, assisting planners, venue or hospitality jobs), consider <strong>education or certifications</strong> (hospitality/event management courses, and later credentials like CMP or CSEP), choose a <strong>focus</strong> (weddings, corporate, social, conferences, festivals), build a <strong>portfolio</strong> with photos and testimonials, and either <strong>join a company</strong> or <strong>start your own planning business</strong>.</p>

## What event planners do

Concept and budget, venue sourcing, vendor booking (catering, AV, décor, entertainment), timelines, contracts, guest management, logistics, on-site coordination and post-event reviews (see [What does an event planner do?](/blog/what-does-an-event-planner-do/)).

## Event planner vs coordinator vs organiser

| Role | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Event planner** | End-to-end planning and design |
| **Event coordinator** | Logistics and on-the-day execution, often supporting a planner or venue |
| **Event organiser** | General term, often used for community, festival and conference organising |
| **Party planner** | Social events: birthdays, showers, celebrations |

## Skills you need

Organisation and time management, budgeting, negotiation, communication, creativity, problem-solving under pressure, vendor relationships, tech tools (project management, registration, floor plans). (Develop them: [How to improve event planning skills](/blog/how-to-improve-event-planning-skills/).)

## Education and certifications

- Hospitality, event management, marketing or business courses (helpful, not always required)
- **CMP (Certified Meeting Professional)** for meetings and conferences (requires experience)
- **CSEP (Certified Special Events Professional)**
- Wedding planning certificates and vendor-specific training

## Get experience

- Volunteer at charity events, festivals and conferences
- Assist an established planner
- Work at venues, hotels, caterers or AV companies
- Plan events for friends, clubs or your workplace
- Join industry associations

## Build a portfolio

Photos, event outlines, budgets managed, testimonials and a simple website (see [How to start an event planning business](/blog/how-to-start-an-event-planning-business/)).

## Career paths and pay

Corporate event planner, wedding planner, conference/meeting planner, venue event manager, festival producer, freelance planner. Pay varies by sector, location and seniority; corporate and senior roles generally pay more.

## Becoming a party planner

Start with birthdays and showers for your network, build a styled portfolio, partner with décor and bakery vendors, and offer packages (see [How to start a party planning business](/blog/how-to-start-a-party-planning-business/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I become an event planner?
Develop organisation and communication skills, gain experience through volunteering or assistant roles, consider courses or certifications, build a portfolio and apply for jobs or start freelancing.

### Do I need a degree to be an event planner?
Not always; experience and a strong portfolio often matter more, though degrees and certifications help for corporate roles.

### How do I become an event coordinator?
Gain logistics experience at venues, hotels or planning firms, develop organisation skills and apply for coordinator or assistant roles.

### How do I get into the event planning industry with no experience?
Volunteer at events, assist planners, work at venues or caterers and plan events for your network.

### What certifications are good for event planners?
CMP for meetings and conferences, CSEP for special events, and specialised wedding planning courses.

### Can event planners work for themselves?
Yes; many build experience then start their own event planning business.
