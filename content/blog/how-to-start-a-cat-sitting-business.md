<p class="lede">A cat sitting business is one of the easiest pet businesses to start: cats usually <strong>stay home</strong> while owners travel, so you offer <strong>drop-in visits</strong> for feeding, litter, medication and company. Register the business, get <strong>pet sitting insurance and bonding</strong>, set <strong>per-visit prices</strong>, and find clients through <strong>vets, cat groomers, rescues and neighbourhood groups</strong>.</p>

## Why cat sitting works as a business

- Many cats get stressed in boarding, so owners prefer in-home care
- Visits are short, so you can fit many into a day
- Lower physical demands than dog walking
- Low startup costs (often under $1,000)

## Services to offer

| Service | Notes |
|---|---|
| Daily drop-in (20–45 min) | Food, water, litter, play, photo update |
| Twice-daily visits | For kittens, seniors and medical cats |
| Medication | Pills, insulin injections, fluids (charge extra) |
| Overnights | For anxious cats or long trips |
| Home care | Mail, plants, lights, bins |

## Step 1: Register and insure

Register a business, get a business license if required, and buy pet sitting insurance with bonding. Clients trust insured, bonded sitters with their keys.

## Step 2: Get cat-specific training

Pet first aid, giving medication and injections, recognising signs of illness (not eating, not using the litter box), and handling shy or stressed cats.

## Step 3: Set your prices

Charge per visit with add-ons for extra cats, medication, holidays and last-minute bookings. Check local rates and pet sitting apps to stay competitive. Rates guide: [How much can a pet sitter make?](/blog/how-much-can-a-pet-sitter-make/).

## Step 4: Meet-and-greet and paperwork

Visit before the first booking. Collect a care form (food, litter, hiding spots, vet, emergency contact, medical history), sign a service agreement, and agree how keys are handled.

## Step 5: Find clients

1. Cat-only vets and cat groomers
2. Cat rescues and shelters (adopters need sitters)
3. Neighbourhood apps and local Facebook groups
4. Google Business Profile and reviews
5. A website with online booking and photos of happy cats
6. Pet sitting apps to get started

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a cat sitting business?
Register, get insured and bonded, set per-visit prices and market to local cat owners.

### What does a cat sitter do?
Feeds cats, cleans litter, gives medication, plays with them and checks the home.

### How much do cat sitters charge?
Usually per visit, often similar to pet sitting drop-ins, with extra for medication and holidays.

### How many visits does a cat need?
At least once a day; twice for kittens, seniors and cats on medication.

### Do I need a license to cat sit?
Usually just a general business license.
