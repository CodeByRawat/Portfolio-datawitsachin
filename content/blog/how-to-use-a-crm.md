<p class="lede">To <strong>use a CRM</strong> well: set it up around your real sales and customer process (pipeline stages, fields, owners), import and clean your contacts, log every important interaction, manage each opportunity as a deal with a next step and date, automate repetitive follow-ups, and review dashboards weekly. The golden rule: <strong>if it isn't in the CRM, it didn't happen</strong>, so make updating it fast and part of the daily routine.</p>

Many businesses buy a CRM, import a spreadsheet, and then go back to email and memory within a month. The software isn't the problem; the habits and setup are. This guide shows exactly how to use a CRM day to day, how to configure it, what to automate, which reports to watch, and how to get a team to adopt it. (New to CRMs? Start with [What is a CRM?](/blog/what-is-a-crm/))

## Step 1: Map your process before touching settings

Write down:
- How leads arrive (website forms, calls, WhatsApp, referrals, events, ads)
- The stages a deal goes through (e.g., New lead → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Negotiation → Won/Lost)
- What must happen to move from one stage to the next (exit criteria)
- Who owns leads and deals
- What happens after a sale (onboarding, renewals, upsells, support)

## Step 2: Set up the CRM

### Core objects
- **Contacts:** people
- **Companies / accounts:** organisations (for B2B)
- **Deals / opportunities:** potential sales with value, stage and close date
- **Activities:** calls, emails, meetings, notes, tasks
- **Tickets** (if using support features)

### Pipelines and stages
Create a pipeline matching your process, with **clear stage definitions** and probability if you forecast.

### Fields
Add only what you'll use: lead source, industry, company size, product interest, lost reason. Too many required fields kill adoption.

### Users and permissions
Give each person the right access; set owners for records.

### Integrations
Connect **email and calendar** (so emails and meetings log automatically), website forms, phone/WhatsApp, and accounting or billing. (See [CRM integration and automation](/blog/crm-integration-and-automation/).)

## Step 3: Import and clean your data

1. Export contacts from spreadsheets, email and old tools.
2. Standardise columns (first name, last name, email, phone with country code, company).
3. Remove duplicates and outdated records.
4. Map lead source and status.
5. Import a small test batch, check, then import the rest.
6. Turn on duplicate detection.

## Step 4: Use the CRM day to day

### Capturing leads
- New enquiries should enter the CRM **automatically** from forms, chat and ads, not by retyping.
- Each new lead gets an **owner** and a **first task** ("Call within 1 hour").

### Logging interactions
- Use the email integration or BCC address so emails attach automatically.
- After calls and meetings, write a **short note**: what they need, objections, next step.
- Use mobile apps to log on the go.

### Managing deals
Every open deal should always have:
- The correct **stage**
- An **amount** and **expected close date**
- A **next step with a due date**

A deal without a next step is a deal you're losing.

### Working your task list
Start each day from the CRM's task view or dashboard: overdue tasks, today's calls and follow-ups, deals with no activity in 14 days.

### After the sale
Mark the deal won, create onboarding tasks, record products and renewal dates, and track support and upsell opportunities. (Relationship-building ideas: [How to build relationships with clients](/blog/how-to-build-relationships-with-clients/).)

## Daily, weekly and monthly CRM routines

| When | Salesperson | Manager |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily (10–15 min)** | Clear overdue tasks, log calls and meetings, update deal stages and next steps | Check new leads are assigned and contacted |
| **Weekly (30–60 min)** | Review pipeline, clean stale deals, plan priorities | Pipeline review meeting using CRM dashboards; coach on stuck deals |
| **Monthly** | Update close dates and amounts | Review conversion rates, win/loss reasons, lead sources, forecast accuracy |
| **Quarterly** | — | Clean data, review fields and stages, retire what's unused |

## Step 5: Automate the repetitive work

Common automations:
- **Lead assignment** by territory, product or round-robin
- **Instant acknowledgement** emails or WhatsApp messages to new leads
- **Follow-up sequences** for leads who don't reply
- **Task creation** when deals change stage
- **Reminders** for stale deals
- **Notifications** to Slack/Teams for big deals
- **Onboarding tasks** when a deal is won
- **Data enrichment** of company details

(More: [How to automate your sales process](/blog/how-to-automate-your-sales-process/) and [What is sales force automation?](/blog/what-is-sales-force-automation/))

## Step 6: Use reports and dashboards

Essential reports:
- **Pipeline by stage** and value
- **Forecast** for the month/quarter
- **Conversion rates** between stages
- **Win rate** and **sales cycle length**
- **Lead sources** by volume and revenue
- **Activity** by rep (calls, meetings)
- **Lost reasons**
- **Customer metrics:** retention, renewals, lifetime value

### What analytical tools do CRMs provide?
Most CRMs include dashboards, pipeline and forecast reports, funnel and conversion analysis, activity tracking, custom report builders, and increasingly AI insights (deal risk scores, forecast predictions, summaries). For deeper analysis, many businesses send CRM data to a BI tool (see [What is business intelligence?](/blog/what-is-business-intelligence/)).

## How do CRMs help with customer rewards programs?

CRMs support loyalty and rewards by:
- Storing **purchase history and points** (natively or via a loyalty app integration)
- **Segmenting** customers by spend, frequency and tier
- **Triggering** automated rewards: birthday offers, milestone rewards, win-back campaigns
- **Personalising** communications with points balances and offers
- **Measuring** the impact of rewards on retention and revenue

## AI features in modern CRMs

- Summaries of contact history before a call
- Drafted follow-up emails
- Call recording, transcription and automatic notes
- Lead and deal scoring
- Forecast predictions and deal-risk alerts
- Natural-language questions ("Which deals over $10k haven't been touched this month?")
- AI agents that research leads, update records and handle routine follow-ups

## How to get your team to use the CRM

1. **Explain the why** for them: fewer forgotten follow-ups, more closed deals, less admin.
2. **Keep it simple:** minimal required fields, clear stages.
3. **Automate data entry** with email, calendar and call integrations.
4. **Train with real scenarios,** not feature tours.
5. **Run meetings from the CRM:** if it's not in the CRM, it's not discussed.
6. **Lead by example:** managers use it daily.
7. **Ask for feedback** and remove friction.

## Common CRM mistakes

- Copying a messy spreadsheet in without cleaning
- Too many stages and fields
- No next steps on deals
- Not integrating email and calendar
- Using it only for reporting, not daily work
- Never cleaning old deals and data
- No owner for CRM administration

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you use a CRM?
Set up pipelines and fields that match your process, import and clean contacts, capture leads automatically, log interactions, manage deals with next steps and dates, automate follow-ups and review pipeline reports regularly.

### How do I use a CRM for a small business?
Start simple: one pipeline, a few key fields, email and calendar integration, automatic lead capture from your website, follow-up reminders and a weekly pipeline review.

### How long does it take to learn a CRM?
Basic daily use (adding contacts, logging activities, updating deals) takes a few hours to learn. Setting up automation, reports and integrations takes days to weeks depending on complexity.

### What should I track in a CRM?
Contacts, companies, deals with stages, values and close dates, all customer interactions, tasks and next steps, lead sources, lost reasons, purchases, renewals and support history.

### Which CRM is easiest to use?
Pipedrive, HubSpot, Zoho CRM and Freshsales are commonly considered beginner-friendly. The best choice depends on your process, budget, integrations and team size.

### How can AI help me use a CRM?
AI can log calls and meetings automatically, summarise history, draft emails, score leads, flag at-risk deals, answer questions about your pipeline and update records, saving salespeople hours of admin each week.
