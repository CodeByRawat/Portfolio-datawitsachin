<p class="lede">A <strong>static ad</strong> is an advertisement made from a <strong>single still image</strong> (with or without text, product shots, logo and a call to action) and no video, animation or sound. Static ads run in social feeds, Stories, display networks, websites, apps and print. A <strong>static post</strong> is the organic equivalent: a non-moving image post on social media. Static ads are fast and cheap to make, easy to test in large numbers, and still drive a large share of results for many advertisers, especially for simple offers and retargeting.</p>

Video gets the headlines, but statics remain a workhorse format. This guide explains what static ads are, how they compare with video and motion ads, when they win, which sizes to use and how to design ones that convert.

## Static ad examples

- A product photo on a bold background with "20% off this weekend"
- A customer review screenshot with a star rating and product image
- A "before vs after" split image
- A comparison table: "Us vs them"
- A simple headline-only ad: "Stop chasing invoices. Automate them."
- A meme-style or native-looking post
- An infographic or stat: "Teams save 6 hours a week"
- Display banners on news sites and apps

## Static vs video vs animated (motion) ads

| | Static ad | Motion / animated ad | Video ad (filmed/UGC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | One still image | Animated graphics, text and product, often short loops | Filmed footage with people, product or demos |
| **Production cost and time** | Lowest | Medium | Medium to high |
| **Attention in feeds** | Lower on average | High | High |
| **Explaining complex ideas** | Limited | Good | Good |
| **Speed to test variations** | Very fast | Fast with templates | Slower |
| **Best for** | Offers, retargeting, clear benefits, testing angles | Feature explanations, SaaS, offers with movement, brand | Trust, demos, storytelling, UGC |

Many accounts perform best with a **mix**: statics to test messages fast, motion and video to scale winners. (More on motion: [What are motion graphics?](/blog/what-are-motion-graphics/))

## When static ads perform best

- **Simple, strong offers:** discounts, free trials, limited-time deals
- **Retargeting:** people who already know you need a reminder, not an explanation
- **Testing angles cheaply:** find the winning message with statics, then make video around it
- **Placements where video isn't possible or ideal:** some display inventory, print, email
- **Visual products:** fashion, food, furniture where the product sells itself
- **Proof-heavy messages:** reviews, ratings, press logos

## Static ad sizes

| Platform / placement | Ratio | Recommended size (px) |
|---|---|---|
| Meta feed (Facebook/Instagram) | 4:5 (or 1:1) | 1080×1350 (1080×1080) |
| Stories and Reels placements | 9:16 | 1080×1920 |
| TikTok (image/carousel ads) | 9:16 | 1080×1920 |
| LinkedIn single image | 1:1 or 1.91:1 | 1200×1200 / 1200×627 |
| X (Twitter) | 1:1 or 16:9 | 1200×1200 / 1200×675 |
| Pinterest | 2:3 | 1000×1500 |
| Google Display (responsive) | 1.91:1 and 1:1 | 1200×628 and 1200×1200 (plus logos) |
| Common display banners | Various | 300×250, 728×90, 336×280, 300×600, 320×50 |

Check each platform's current specs for file size and text guidance.

## How to design a static ad that converts

1. **One message.** If you can't say it in one sentence, simplify.
2. **Visual hierarchy:** the eye should hit the hook or product first, then the benefit, then the CTA.
3. **Large, readable headline:** 3–8 words; readable on a phone at arm's length.
4. **Show the product or result:** real product, real UI, real people.
5. **High contrast** with the platform's background (both light and dark mode).
6. **Brand cues** (colour, font, logo) without letting the logo dominate.
7. **Proof:** star rating, number of customers, testimonial, guarantee.
8. **Clear CTA** in the image or rely on the platform button.
9. **Native feel:** on social, ads that look like posts often outperform glossy banners.
10. **Respect safe zones** in 9:16 placements so text isn't covered by UI.

### Copy formulas for static ads
- **"[Desired result] without [pain]"**: "Clean books without the spreadsheet chaos."
- **Number + benefit:** "3,200 clinics book appointments on autopilot."
- **Question hook:** "Still answering the same WhatsApp questions all day?"
- **Social proof:** "'Saved us 10 hours a week.' (Real customer review)"

## Testing static ads at scale

Because statics are quick to make, they're ideal for systematic testing:
- Test **angles** first (price vs speed vs quality vs proof), then **hooks**, then **visual styles**
- Change one main variable per test when you want clear learnings
- Use platform features that combine multiple images and headlines (see [What is dynamic creative optimization?](/blog/what-is-dynamic-creative-optimization/))
- Refresh creative regularly to avoid ad fatigue

## From static to motion

A winning static is a perfect brief for a motion ad: animate the same headline, product and proof with movement in the first second. Simple motion (text reveals, product spins, counters) often lifts thumb-stop rate without needing a full video shoot. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is a static ad?
A static ad is an advertisement made from a single still image, usually with a headline, product or visual, branding and a call to action, without video or animation.

### What is a static post?
A static post is an organic social media post made of a single non-moving image (or graphic), as opposed to a video, Reel or animated post.

### Are static ads better than video ads?
Neither is always better. Video and motion ads usually win attention in feeds, while static ads are cheaper and faster to test and often perform well for simple offers and retargeting. Most advertisers use both.

### What size should a static ad be?
For Meta feeds, 1080×1350 (4:5) or 1080×1080 (1:1); for Stories, Reels and TikTok, 1080×1920 (9:16); for Google Display, 1200×628 and 1200×1200 plus standard banner sizes.

### How much text can a static ad have?
Keep it short, ideally a headline of under 8 words and a short supporting line. Less text is easier to read on mobile and generally performs better.

### What is the difference between a static ad and a dynamic ad?
A static ad shows the same image to everyone. A dynamic ad changes its content, such as products or headlines, automatically based on the viewer or context.
