<p class="lede">To become a concrete contractor, <strong>start as a labourer or finisher on a concrete crew</strong>, learn every step of forming, pouring and finishing, consider an <strong>apprenticeship or concrete certifications</strong>, then get your <strong>contractor license (where required), insurance and bond</strong> and start bidding your own jobs.</p>

## Step 1: Start on a crew

Look for labourer jobs with concrete companies, foundation contractors and builders. You'll learn site prep, forms, rebar, pouring, finishing and curing.

## Step 2: Learn the skills

- Reading plans and measurements
- Grading and base compaction
- Building forms
- Rebar and mesh placement
- Finishing (bull float, edging, brooming, power trowel)
- Decorative work (stamping, colour, sealing)
- Estimating and bidding

## Step 3: Training and certifications

Union apprenticeships (cement masons) and concrete industry certifications for finishers and field testing help you learn faster and earn more.

## Step 4: Get licensed

Many states require a contractor license for work over a certain dollar amount, often with an exam on trade and business law, proof of experience and a bond. Check your state's requirements.

## Step 5: Insurance and business setup

LLC, EIN, general liability, workers' comp, commercial auto and a contractor bond.

## Step 6: Start small

Begin with patios, sidewalks and small driveways. Rent bigger equipment. Take photos of every job and collect reviews.

| Finisher | Finishing surfaces |

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I become a concrete contractor?
Work on a concrete crew, learn the trade, get licensed and insured, and start bidding jobs.

### Do you need a license to be a concrete contractor?
Many states require one above a job-size limit; check locally.

### How long does it take to become a concrete contractor?
Usually several years of crew experience.

### Is concrete contracting a good career?
Yes. Demand is steady and owners can earn well, though the work is physical.

### Can I start a concrete business without experience?
It's risky. Partner with an experienced finisher if you lack experience.
