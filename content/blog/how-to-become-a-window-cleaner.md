<p class="lede">To become a window cleaner, learn <strong>squeegee technique and water-fed pole use</strong>, get <strong>ladder and fall-safety training</strong>, then either <strong>join a window cleaning company</strong> to learn on the job or <strong>start your own business</strong>. High-rise work needs <strong>specialised training and certification</strong>.</p>

## Skills you need

- Squeegee and scrubber technique
- Water-fed pole and pure water systems
- Ladder safety
- Screen and track cleaning
- Hard-water stain removal
- Customer service and reliability

## Training and certification

| Work type | Training |
|---|---|
| Ground and ladder | On-the-job, ladder safety course |
| Water-fed pole | Equipment training |
| High-rise / rope access | Formal safety training and certification |

## Ways to start

1. **Get hired** by a window cleaning company to learn quickly
2. **Go self-employed** with basic equipment and a few customers
3. **Specialise** in storefronts, residential or high-rise

## How window cleaners earn

Employees earn hourly or per job; owners earn from their pricing and routes. See [Is a window cleaning business profitable?](/blog/is-a-window-cleaning-business-profitable/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I become a window cleaner?
Learn the techniques and safety, then get hired or start your own business.

### Do you need qualifications to be a window cleaner?
Usually not for ground work, but safety training is important; high-rise work needs certification.

### Is window cleaning a good job?
It's flexible, physical and can be lucrative as a business owner.

### How do I learn window cleaning?
Work for a company, practise, and use training videos and courses.

### What equipment does a window cleaner need?
Squeegees, scrubbers, scrapers, ladders and optionally a water-fed pole.

### Can I start window cleaning on my own?
Yes, with insurance, basic equipment and local marketing.
