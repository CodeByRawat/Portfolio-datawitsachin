An AI assistant can give a founder, manager or small team hours back every week. The trick is aiming it at the right work and connecting it to your real tools, because a chat window alone only gets you so far.

## What an AI executive assistant or AI secretary can do well

- **Email:** triage, summarise long threads, draft replies in your voice.
- **Scheduling:** propose times, handle back-and-forth, add context to invites.
- **Meetings:** transcribe, summarise, extract action items and push them to your task tool.
- **Research:** compile briefings on companies, people or topics with sources.
- **Admin:** fill forms, update the CRM, prepare reports, chase invoices.

## What it still can't do

Make judgement calls that depend on relationships, handle genuinely novel situations, or be trusted to send sensitive messages unsupervised. Keep approval steps for anything external or irreversible.

## From chatbot to "AI employee"

The phrase *AI employee* usually means an assistant with **tools and a defined job**: it has access to your inbox, CRM and calendar, a written procedure, and limits on what it may do alone. That's an agent, and the value comes from the integrations and the procedure, not from the model alone.

## How to automate tasks with AI: a simple sequence

1. Write down your ten most repetitive tasks and how long they take.
2. Choose one with clear inputs and a clear "done" state.
3. Connect the AI to the source and the destination (email to CRM, form to spreadsheet, call to notes).
4. Run it in **draft mode**, where it prepares and you approve.
5. Remove the approval step only for the categories where it's been consistently right.

## Choosing an assistant

For personal use, any major assistant is fine. For a business, prioritise **data handling** (where does your data go?), **integrations** with your stack, and **admin controls**.
