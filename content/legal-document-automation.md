If your lawyers keep opening an old document, searching and replacing names, and fixing what they forgot, that's a job for **legal document automation**.

## How it works

1. **Capture facts** through a client questionnaire, intake form or matter record.
2. **Apply logic:** if the client is a company, use these clauses; if the term exceeds a year, add that schedule.
3. **Generate** a formatted document (Word or PDF) with the right content.
4. **Review and sign:** lawyer approval, then e-signature.

## What to automate first

Start with high-volume, low-variation documents: engagement letters, NDAs, standard supplier or employment contracts, routine notices. The payoff is highest where lawyers currently spend an hour on something that should take five minutes.

## Where AI fits

Template automation is **deterministic**. The same answers give the same document, which is what you want for standard work. AI adds value around the edges: suggesting clause variations, extracting data from instructions, summarising a counterparty's draft. Keep a lawyer in the loop on anything that goes to a client.

## Document generation as a business

Some founders build **automated document generation businesses**: customers answer questions and receive documents (business formation, contracts, wills). It's a real model, but be careful about:

- **Regulation:** many jurisdictions restrict giving legal advice or preparing certain documents without qualification. Know where your product sits.
- **Quality & liability:** templates need qualified legal input and maintenance as law changes.
- **Disclaimers and review options:** offer lawyer review as an upsell, not an afterthought.
