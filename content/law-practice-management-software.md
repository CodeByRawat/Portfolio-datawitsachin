**Law practice management software** (also called law firm software, law office software or legal management software) is the system a firm runs on: who the client is, what the matter is, what's due, who did what, and what to bill. The category is crowded, so choosing well comes down to your firm's actual workflow.

## The core modules

| Module | What it should do |
|---|---|
| **Contacts & conflicts** | One record per client and related party; conflict checks before you take a matter |
| **Matter / case management** | Matter details, tasks, notes, parties, status, deadlines |
| **Calendar & deadlines** | Court and limitation dates, reminders, rule-based date calculation |
| **Documents** | Central storage, templates, versioning, email filing |
| **Time & billing** | Time capture, invoices, payment tracking, trust/client-account handling |
| **Client communication** | Intake forms, portal, secure messaging, e-signature |
| **Reporting** | Utilisation, realisation, aged debt, matter profitability |

## How to compare options

1. **Map your practice first.** Litigation, conveyancing, family and corporate work have different workflows.
2. **Check billing fit.** Hourly, fixed fee, contingency and trust accounting rules vary by jurisdiction.
3. **Look at integrations:** email, calendar, accounting and e-signature save the most re-keying.
4. **Test migration.** Ask how data comes in, and how it comes *out* if you leave.
5. **Look at total cost,** including per-user fees, add-ons and onboarding.

## Cloud or on-premise?

Most firms now choose cloud for access and maintenance reasons. Verify encryption, backups, access logs and where data is hosted. Some clients and regulators care about all of those.

## When custom software wins

Off-the-shelf tools fit the average firm. If your firm has an unusual workflow (high-volume claims, specialist intake, bespoke fee structures, integrations with a government or insurer portal), you may spend more bending the tool than building the right one.
