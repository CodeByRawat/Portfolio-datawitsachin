AI vocabulary changes fast, so here's a plain guide to the main categories and the terms you'll see in 2026.

## Types of AI by capability

- **Narrow AI (ANI):** built for specific tasks. Every AI product you can use today is narrow, including chatbots and image generators.
- **General AI (AGI):** hypothetical AI that matches human ability across domains. Not here.
- **Super AI:** hypothetical, beyond human ability.

## Types of AI by technique

| Type | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Machine learning** | Learns patterns from data to predict or classify | Spam filter, demand forecast |
| **Deep learning** | ML using large neural networks | Speech recognition, image recognition |
| **Generative AI** | Creates new content | Writing, images, code |
| **Agentic AI** | Pursues goals by planning and using tools | An assistant that researches and updates your CRM |

## Which task is a generative AI task?

A task is generative if the output is **newly created content**:

- Writing an email or article ✅
- Generating an image from a prompt ✅
- Writing code from a description ✅
- Summarising a document into new text ✅
- Predicting next month's sales ❌ (that's predictive ML)
- Flagging a transaction as fraud ❌ (classification)
- Sorting photos into categories ❌ (classification)

## What is the purpose of AI?

In business, AI has three practical jobs: **understanding** unstructured information (text, documents, images), **predicting** what's likely to happen, and **acting**, by generating content or carrying out steps. The best results come from pairing it with a well-defined process, not from "adding AI" for its own sake.

## Terms worth knowing

- **AI-enabled:** a product with AI features inside.
- **Agentive / agentic AI:** systems that take multi-step actions toward a goal. See [what is agentic automation](/blog/what-is-agentic-automation).
- **RAG:** retrieval-augmented generation, where the model answers using your documents.
- **Human in the loop:** a person reviews or approves AI output.
