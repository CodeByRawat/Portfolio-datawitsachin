Most firms track clients well *after* they instruct. The leaks happen before that: the web enquiry that waited two days, the referral source nobody thanked, the former client who never heard from you again. A **legal CRM** closes those gaps.

## What a law firm CRM should do

- **Capture every enquiry:** web forms, calls, email, chat, referrals, landing pages, all into one pipeline.
- **Respond fast:** instant acknowledgement, assignment to the right lawyer, reminders if nobody replies.
- **Run a pipeline:** enquiry, consultation booked, engagement letter sent, instructed, lost (with a reason).
- **Track referral sources,** so you know which channels bring valuable work.
- **Keep relationships warm:** automated check-ins, newsletters and review requests for past clients.
- **Hand off cleanly:** when a prospect instructs, the data flows into the matter system, not re-typed.

## Confidentiality matters more here

A legal CRM holds sensitive information. Look for role-based access, audit logs, encryption, and a clear policy on where data is hosted and whether any of it is used by AI providers. If a lawyer is working across jurisdictions, check data-residency requirements.

## Buy or build?

Off-the-shelf legal CRMs are fine for standard consumer-law pipelines. A **custom CRM** makes sense when you have unusual intake (for example, high-volume claims with partner referrers), need tight integration with your case system, or want automation that generic tools can't do. Custom doesn't have to mean huge: a lean CRM with the 20% of features you use is often faster to adopt than a bloated one.
